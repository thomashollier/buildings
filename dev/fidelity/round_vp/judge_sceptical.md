## San Francisco

### img00: gable-front Stick / Queen Anne cottage, ca. 1882–90 → FAILS

Discrepancies:
1. **No entry porch or hood.** The door sits bare on a straight stair. SF houses of this date always have a hooded or porticoed entry, with columns or consoles carrying an entablature or pediment (R2 shows consoles and hoods, R1 and R3 show porticoes).
2. **The gable is bare.** It is a plain 50° triangle with no bargeboard, no fish-scale shingles or sunburst, no pent eave or cornice return, and no ornament at the peak. On a gable front of this date the gable is the main decorated feature (every gable in R3).
3. **The style is mixed.** The square bay has round-arched windows on the parlour floor and flat-headed windows above. A Stick bay has flat cornice heads on brackets (Stick hallmark), and round arches are Italianate. Mixing them inside one bay reads as a style slip.
4. **The eave line is incoherent.** A white bracketed frieze runs over the bay only (x 90–282), and the rest of the wall meets the gable with no cornice at all.
5. **The bay has no spandrel panels** between floors and no panelled base. Real bays carry raised panels under each sill (R1).
6. The stair has plain posts and no turned balusters or newel posts (R1, R2).

Top 3 fixes: add an entry porch or hood; decorate the gable (bargeboard, shingles, cornice returns); give the bay one window-head vocabulary (flat Stick heads).

### img01: Italianate, slanted bay, ca. 1870–85 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

The proportions are good: 407 × 620 px gives a ratio of 1.52, about 25 × 38 ft. Narrow–wide–narrow bay lights read correctly as a foreshortened 45° bay.

Discrepancies:
1. **The cornice brackets float.** The brackets hang from the top cornice (y 85–120) over a band of plain siding (y 120–150), and a separate flat frieze sits below that (y 150–175). On real SF Italianates the brackets spring from the frieze, with frieze panels or dentils between them (R1, R2). The detached two-band entablature does not exist in the type.
2. **No portico.** The round-arched door has only a keystone. The signature SF Italianate entry is a portico of columns or pilasters carrying an entablature, sometimes a pediment (R1).
3. **The bay corners are plain flat strips.** Real bays have engaged colonnettes with capitals, and panels under each sill (R1).
4. The bay does not have its own cornice breaking forward at the top. In R1 the cornice and brackets follow the bay's angled plan.
5. Triangular pediments over round-arched windows on the lower bay floor are possible but uncommon. Hood mouldings that follow the arch, with a keystone, are the norm (R1).
6. The stair rail is plain posts with no balusters.

Top 3 fixes: put the brackets in the frieze; add a portico; add colonnettes and panels to the bay.

### img02: Italianate, slanted bay, ca. 1870–85 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

Discrepancies:
1. **It is too tall for its width.** 377 × 737 px is a ratio of 1.95, so a 25 ft lot gives about 49 ft for two storeys over a basement. Real examples are about 35–40 ft (img01 and img03 come out at 36–38 ft). The excess sits in a parlour floor of about 17.7 ft (y 445–710) and a 50 px blank parapet zone under the brackets.
2. **The brackets float** over plain siding, separate from the frieze, as in img01.
3. **The cornices are stacked twice.** The bay's own cornice band (y 217–235) sits directly under the main frieze (y 185–215), so two crowns are piled up with nothing between them.
4. No portico on the door.
5. The bay begins at the top of the basement with nothing under it. Many SF bays run down to grade or sit on visible brackets.
6. The bay windows have no hoods, but the door has a keystone. The hierarchy is inverted.

Top 3 fixes: cut the parlour floor and parapet height; put the brackets in the frieze; add a portico.

### img03: Italianate, slanted bay, ca. 1870–85 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

The ratio of 1.44 is fine. The pedimented hoods over door and windows are a credible flat-front Italianate vocabulary.

Discrepancies:
1. **The brackets float** above the frieze, as in img01.
2. **The door has a pediment but no portico or columns.** A pedimented hood over the door needs consoles to carry it (R2). Here it rests on a flat architrave.
3. The bay corners are plain strips with no capitals or sill panels.
4. The basement windows flank the stair at the same size as the window under the bay, but the bay itself stops at the basement line.

Top 3 fixes: put the brackets in the frieze; add consoles or a portico at the door; detail the bay.

### img07: Italianate, three storeys over basement, ca. 1875–85 → FAILS

Discrepancies:
1. **The bay stops one floor short.** The slanted bay rises through the lower two floors and stops under a cornice at y 330, with a flat three-window top floor above it. On SF Italianates the bay runs to the main cornice, and the cornice breaks forward over it (R1, R4). A bay capped mid-facade by its own cornice, with a flat storey above, is not a pattern of the type.
2. The top floor's three equal arched windows are offset from the bay below, so the upper axes do not line up with the bay.
3. **The brackets float** over blank siding (y 112–140), as in img01.
4. No portico. The door has a pediment but nothing carries it.
5. The bay has no colonnettes with capitals and no sill panels.

Top 3 fixes: run the bay to the main cornice; line up the top-floor windows with the bay; put the brackets in the frieze.

### img08: gable-front Stick, square bay, ca. 1882–90 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

This is the cleanest of the three gable fronts. The flat-headed square bay with paired windows is correct Stick.

Discrepancies:
1. **The gable is bare**, as in img00: no bargeboard, no shingles, no truss or stickwork at the peak.
2. **No entry hood or porch.** The door has a transom and nothing else.
3. The window surrounds are thin frames. Stick windows carry flat cornice heads on small brackets, with sill aprons.
4. There is no stickwork (vertical and horizontal boards over the siding), which is the defining Stick element. There are no corner boards and no frieze over the entry half.
5. The white frieze covers the bay half only.

Top 3 fixes: decorate the gable; add an entry hood; add Stick window heads and stickwork.

### img09: gable-front Stick, square bay, ca. 1882–90 → FAILS

Discrepancies:
1. **The bay's window hierarchy is inverted and mixed.** The upper bay floor has round-arched paired lights and the parlour floor has flat-headed ones. When SF bays mix head shapes, the richer form goes on the parlour floor. Round arches in a square Stick bay are already a style slip.
2. The gable is bare.
3. No entry hood or porch.
4. The upper-floor window over the door is round-arched while the parlour windows are flat. The style is muddled again.
5. The corner pilasters have caps, which is good. There are no sill panels.

Top 3 fixes: settle on one head type, flat for Stick; decorate the gable; add an entry hood.

### img13: Italianate, slanted bay, ca. 1870–85 → PASSES WITH FLAWS (best SF)

The bay colonnettes have capitals and plinths, the keystones are in the right places, and the door has a pediment with a keystone. This is the most believable SF image.

Discrepancies:
1. **It is too tall and narrow.** 377 × 699 px is a ratio of 1.85, about 46 ft for two storeys over a basement. The parlour floor is about 16 ft floor to floor (y 418–662).
2. **The brackets float** over a blank band (y 135–170), as in img01.
3. The pedimented door hood has no consoles or portico.
4. The bay has no sill panels.

Top 3 fixes: cut the height to a ratio of about 1.5; put the brackets in the frieze; add a portico.

## Paris

### img04: Haussmannian, 5 bays, porte cochère, ca. 1855–70 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

The order is credible: ground floor with porte cochère, a plain floor, a floor with pediments and a continuous balcony, a floor with railings, a mansard with dormers on every axis, and party-wall chimney stacks.

Discrepancies:
1. **No rustication.** The ground floor (and entresol) of a Haussmannian block is channel-jointed stone (refends or bossage) and visibly heavier than the floors above (R5, R6, R7). Here the ground floor is the same flat ashlar as the rest.
2. **The floor above the ground floor has floor-length windows with no railing.** Every Paris porte-fenêtre has a guard rail (garde-corps), including on the entresol (R5, R6). If this floor is meant as an entresol, its windows should also be squatter than the noble floor's. Here they are the same height.
3. **The balcony has no consoles** under it. Haussmannian continuous balconies sit on carved consoles (R5).
4. There are only four storeys under the cornice. The canonical scheme (R6) has five or six levels: ground floor, entresol, 2nd floor with continuous balcony, 3rd, 4th, 5th floor with continuous balcony, then attic. That makes this one an early or minor street example.
5. The main cornice is a thin band, and there is no continuous balcony on the top floor.

Top 3 fixes: rusticate the ground floor and entresol; add a guard rail to every French window; put consoles under the balconies.

### img05: Haussmannian, 5 bays, ca. 1860–80 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

The balcony scheme is correct: continuous on the 2nd floor and on the top floor under the cornice, with individual railings between (R6).

Discrepancies:
1. **No rustication** on the ground floor.
2. **The floor above the ground floor has floor-length windows and no railing.**
3. **The entrance is a window-sized single door in the end bay** (x 460–510). A block of this size without shops would have a porte cochère: tall, double-leaf, usually round-headed, often reaching into the entresol (R5).
4. The balconies have no consoles. The segmental dormers are fine.

Top 3 fixes: rusticate the ground floor; add a porte cochère; add guard rails.

### img06: late Haussmannian, 8 bays, two-tier attic, ca. 1880–1900 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

Discrepancies:
1. **No continuous balcony on the 2nd floor.** There are railings on the 2nd and 3rd floors and a continuous balcony only on the top floor. That is possible after 1880, but the 2nd-floor continuous balcony is the commonest signature of the type (R6).
2. **No rustication** on the ground floor.
3. **The entrance is a small single door** at the end of an 8-bay, roughly 25 m frontage. A frontage that size would have a central or emphasised porte cochère.
4. The floor above the ground floor has no railings, as in img04.
5. With eight equal bays and no central accent (no projecting bay, no grouped centre axis), the facade is monotonous for its width.

Top 3 fixes: add the 2nd-floor continuous balcony; rusticate the ground floor; add a porte cochère on the centre axis.

### img10: post-Haussmannian, 6 bays, two-tier attic, ca. 1885–1902 → PASSES WITH FLAWS (borderline)

Discrepancies:
1. **There is no floor hierarchy.** Four identical upper floors carry identical railings and identical window heights. There is no taller noble floor and no 2nd-floor balcony. The result is generic.
2. No rustication, and no entresol.
3. The entrance is a small single door in the end bay.
4. The two-tier attic, with a large lower and a small upper dormer row, is right for the 1890s. Its height (y 65–235, about 2.1 storeys) is at the upper limit.

Top 3 fixes: add a noble floor (taller, with a continuous balcony); rusticate the ground floor; add a porte cochère.

### img11: Haussmannian with shops, 5 bays, ca. 1860–75 → PASSES WITH FLAWS (best Paris)

This is the closest to canonical: shopfronts with fascias, a central entrance, a taller pedimented noble floor (about 140 px against about 100 px for the others) with a continuous balcony, individual railings, a continuous top-floor balcony, and dormers with pediments.

Discrepancies:
1. **The entresol above the shops (y 545–630) has full-height windows and no railings.** In R6 the entresol windows are shorter and railed.
2. **No rustication** on the shop piers or entresol.
3. The shop piers are thin strips. Real ones are stone piers with a strong entablature over the shopfronts, carrying the entresol.
4. The balconies have no consoles.

Top 3 fixes: make the entresol squatter and railed; rusticate the base; strengthen the shop piers and their entablature.

### img12: narrow 3-bay block with shops, ca. 1880–1900 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

Discrepancies:
1. **All four upper floors are the same height** (about 125 px each). There is no noble floor and no 2nd-floor continuous balcony.
2. **The dormers skip the middle axis.** Two dormer columns sit over three window axes, so the centre axis ends in a blank roof. Paris dormers follow the window axes.
3. **The roof is too tall.** The mansard (y 100–310, 210 px) is about 1.7 times a storey height on a narrow front. The chimney stacks are oversized (about 40 px wide) and have no pots.
4. No rustication. The entry door with transom beside the shops is acceptable.

Top 3 fixes: put a dormer on every axis; differentiate the noble floor; reduce the roof and chimney size.

### img14: Haussmannian, 5 bays, porte cochère, ca. 1860–75 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

Discrepancies:
1. **Three oculi are the only dormers**, set on axes 1, 3 and 5, so axes 2 and 4 have nothing. Oculi on a mansard belong to grand buildings and corner pavilions (R7). A plain five-bay rental block would have a lucarne on every axis.
2. No rustication.
3. The floor above the ground floor has no railings.
4. The porte cochère is good: round-headed and double-leaf. It has no consoles or keystone carving above it (R5).

Top 3 fixes: put lucarnes on every axis; rusticate the ground floor; add guard rails.

### img15: Haussmannian with shops and porte cochère, 6 bays, ca. 1860–75 → PASSES WITH FLAWS

Discrepancies:
1. **No continuous balcony on the top floor.** There are railings on the 3rd and 4th floors, but no top-floor continuous balcony under the cornice. With a continuous balcony on the 2nd floor, a canonical block also has one on the 5th (R6).
2. The entresol has full-height windows with no railings.
3. No rustication on the shop piers or entresol.
4. The balconies have no consoles.

Top 3 fixes: add the top-floor continuous balcony; make the entresol squatter and railed; rusticate the base.

## Systematic errors

### San Francisco (all 8)

1. **No porch, portico or hood in any image.** This is the biggest tell. Every real SF Victorian entry is framed: an Italianate portico on columns, or a Stick or Queen Anne hood on consoles (R1, R2, R3).
2. **The cornice brackets are detached from the frieze** (01, 02, 03, 07, 13). The generator stacks a bracket course, then a blank siding band, then a flat frieze. Real brackets spring from the frieze, with panels or dentils between them, and the cornice breaks forward over the bay (R1).
3. **Gables are bare triangles** (00, 08, 09): no bargeboard, shingles, peak ornament or cornice returns (R3).
4. **Bays are undetailed:** no panels under the sills, no bay cornice breaking forward, and in most images plain strips instead of colonnettes with capitals. img13 is the exception for the capitals.
5. **Stairs have plain posts** and no turned balusters or square newels (R1, R2).
6. **Window-head vocabulary is mixed inside one facade or one bay** (00, 09): round Italianate arches in square Stick bays.
7. **The height-to-width ratio drifts above about 1.8** for two storeys over a basement (02, 13). This comes from parlour floors of 16–18 ft and tall blank parapets.
8. **No Queen Anne features appear at all:** no towers, no spindlework, no mixed siding textures. So "Victorian" here covers only Italianate and Stick.

### Paris (all 8)

1. **No rustication anywhere.** The ground floor and entresol are never channel-jointed (refends), so the base never reads heavier than the floors above. This is the single most visible Haussmannian signature that is missing (R5, R6, R7).
2. **French windows without guard rails** on the floor above the ground floor (04, 05, 06, 11, 14, 15). Every porte-fenêtre in Paris has a garde-corps.
3. **The entresol is not squatter.** Where the floor above the ground floor acts as an entresol, its windows are the same height as the noble floor's.
4. **No consoles under the balconies**, and no carved agrafes or cartouches beyond simple keystones and pediments (R5).
5. **The entrance is mis-sized** (05, 06, 10): a window-sized single door where a block of that frontage would have a porte cochère. 04, 14 and 15 get it right.
6. **The balcony scheme is applied inconsistently.** Some images lack the 2nd-floor continuous balcony (06, 10, 12) or the top-floor one (04, 15). The canonical pairing of both (R6) appears in only 05 and 11.
7. **Dormers break the window axes** (12, 14). Lucarnes should sit on every axis.
8. **Rooflines repeat:** every image has exactly two oversized chimney stacks at the party walls, with no pots. Real Paris roofs show several stacks with terracotta pots (R6).

### Both cities

- No image places the building in a row: there are no neighbours and no party-wall returns. The continuous balconies and cornices overrun the facade edge by a few pixels, which would put them into the neighbouring lot.
- Window glazing is the same 2×2 grid everywhere. This passes for SF 2-over-2 sashes, but Paris casements of the period have three or more lights per leaf (R5, R6).
