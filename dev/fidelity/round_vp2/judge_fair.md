## Per-drawing verdicts

### img00 — Paris, narrow 3-bay immeuble, c. 1860–70
**R 4 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- The ground floor works: two shopfronts with signboard fascias and a central arched porte cochère. It is the best base in the Paris set.
- Above the ground floor there is only one rusticated floor, then two floors that **both** carry full continuous balconies, then the roof. A real building has 2e and 5e continuous balconies with 3e/4e between them (Avenue de l'Opéra no. 14). Two continuous balconies on adjacent floors, with nothing above or below, reads as a building cut short.
- The porte cochère rises only to the top of the shopfronts. On Boulevard Haussmann it spans ground plus entresol, so it runs about 1.4× taller than the shop glazing.
- The 2e and 3e windows are the same height. There is no étage-noble hierarchy.
- Thin vertical lines divide the wall into panels. Real ashlar shows horizontal coursing, not vertical panel joints.

### img01 — San Francisco Italianate, c. 1875
**R 4 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- The massing is right: a false front, a bracketed cornice, a raised entry with a long stoop, and a basement window.
- The "bay" holds **one** window per floor between doubled pilasters. A real slanted bay shows three faces: a wide centre light and two narrower side lights, about 0.55× the centre width (Washington St, Stadtmuller).
- A triangular pediment sits on top of a keystone, so two crowning devices are stacked. Italianate bays in SF use a bracketed flat entablature per floor (Stadtmuller), not a pediment.
- There is a 9% blank band of siding between the top of the upper windows and the brackets. Real friezes are panelled and the brackets drop into them.
- Brackets are short stubby trapezoids at even spacing. Real brackets are paired scrolls about 1.5–2× taller than wide.
- The window bars put the cross-rail at about 40% down from the arch spring, which reads as a French transom. SF windows are 2-over-2 double-hung, with the meeting rail at half height.

### img02 — San Francisco Italianate with slanted bay, c. 1875
**R 4 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- Narrow–wide–narrow windows at the bay correctly suggest a canted bay. This is the most convincing SF Italianate trick in the set.
- The upper bay has **no entablature**: a row of white dentil dashes floats in the frieze with no cornice to hang from. The lower bay has one. Real bays carry a bracketed entablature on every floor.
- The side windows press against the pilasters with no reveal margin.
- The entry arch is a dark void about 1.2 storeys tall that reads as a tunnel. Stadtmuller shows a projecting columned portico with its own entablature in front of a recessed vestibule.
- Four stray blue horizontal lines run across the stair treads. This is a drawing artifact.

### img03 — Paris, narrow 3-bay, c. 1865

## Ranked giveaways across all 16

**1. The étage noble is not the noble floor (Paris; 6 of 8 drawings: img09, 10, 11, 12, 14, and partially img00/img07).**
In real Haussmann building, the 2e étage gets the continuous balcony on consoles, the tallest windows and the richest surrounds, and the 5e gets the second continuous balcony under the cornice (Avenue de l'Opéra no. 14). Here the continuous balcony usually sits only on the top floor, and the 2e gets a low guard.
*Fix:* P(2e continuous balcony) = 0.9; P(5e continuous balcony) = 0.85 when there are 5 or more floors; 3e/4e get individual balconettes or garde-corps (P 0.6 / 0.4). Never put continuous balconies on two adjacent floors unless one of them is the 5e. Continuous balconies run the full width with no skipped end bays (P 0.9).

**2. Flat vertical hierarchy: identical floors and identical windows (Paris, all 8).**
Real buildings use storey heights of roughly ground + entresol 5.5–6.0 m, 2e 3.3 m, 3e 3.0 m, 4e 2.9 m, 5e 2.7 m. Window heights fall about 7% per floor, and surround richness falls with them: 2e pediment or bracketed cornice, 3e flat cornice, 4e/5e plain chambranle. Keystones currently land on whichever floor happens to get them.
*Fix:* storey height multipliers [1.0, 0.92, 0.88, 0.82] from 2e up. Window height = 0.8–0.85 × storey height. Hood type keyed to floor, never random.

**3. The Paris base is wrong (img07, 09, 10, 11, 12, 14).**
Ground floors are drawn as residential windows, with an ordinary door in a corner bay. Real Paris streets have shopfronts plus an entresol behind channelled rustication (refends), and a porte cochère about 2.8–3.2 m wide that rises through ground + entresol (102 Bd Haussmann).
*Fix:* P(shops) = 0.75. Porte cochère width 1.2–1.5 bays, height 1.35–1.5× shop glazing, in the central bay on odd-bay façades (P 0.7). Draw horizontal rustication joints every about 0.45 m on ground + entresol only.

**4. Top-heavy mansards (img03, 10, 12, 14).**
Two tiers of lucarnes over three or four masonry floors, with the roof at 23–28% of total height. Real combles take 15–20% of the height, and a double tier appears only over 5 or more floors.
*Fix:* roof height ≤ 0.2 × total. Double tier only if masonry floors ≥ 5 (P 0.3). Lower-tier lucarnes sit on the cornice line behind a gutter or balustrade, each tier is uniform, and lucarnes align to every bay or every other bay.

**5. Geometry collisions and orphan parts (SF: img02, 04, 13, 15; all SF stairs).**
Windows overlap corner boards (img04, img13). Dentil courses float with no cornice (img02, 04, 13). Pediments are stacked on keystones (img01, 02, 04). Stray blue lines cross the stair treads in every SF stoop. A detached block floats beside img15.
*Fix:* add a hard rule that openings keep at least a 0.15 m reveal from pilasters and corner boards. Dentils only emit as children of an entablature. Use one crowning device per opening. Remove the stair overlay lines.

**6. SF bays don't read as bays (img01, 05, 06, 08).**
A slanted bay always shows three lights (side lights 0.5–0.6× the centre width), each floor capped by its own bracketed entablature, and the bay runs both floors into the main cornice (Washington St, Stadtmuller). The drawings show single windows between pilasters, a picture window (img05), or flush paired windows.
*Fix:* P(slanted bay) = 0.85 for Italianate and 0.9 for Queen Anne. Use three lights per floor, side/centre width ratio 0.55, and an entablature with at least 3 brackets on every bay floor. Forbid any window with width/height > 0.75.

**7. Wrong window type in SF (all 8 SF drawings).**
The cross-rail sits about 35–40% down, like a French casement with a transom. SF Victorians are 2-over-2 double-hung with the meeting rail at 50% and windows about 1:2.4. Hoods matter too: Italianate uses segmental or round heads with a moulded hood, and Stick-Eastlake uses flat heads. Round arches under spindle friezes (img08, img15) mix the two styles.
*Fix:* meeting rail at 0.5 h. Style-locked head shapes: Italianate → round/segmental (P 0.7), Stick-Eastlake → flat (P 0.95), Queen Anne → flat plus a transom light in the bay (P 0.7).

**8. SF entries: a dark arch void instead of a portico (img01, 02, 04), or no hood at all (img13).**
Real Italianate porticoes have two Corinthian columns carrying an entablature or pediment in front of the wall, with a recessed vestibule behind. The door is single-leaf or a narrow pair, about 1.0–1.2 m wide and about 2.7 m tall, with an arched transom (Stadtmuller).
*Fix:* make the portico width about 1.4× the door width with a solid entablature. Keep the void depth to the vestibule only, and never taller than the door plus transom plus 0.3 m. P(portico) = 0.9 for Italianate.

**9. SF cornice and frieze (all Italianate drawings).**
Short, evenly spaced trapezoid brackets over a wide blank band. Real Italianate frieze: paired scroll brackets 1.5–2× taller than wide, spaced over the window piers, with recessed panels between them and the frieze about 0.6–0.9 m deep.
*Fix:* bracket height/width = 1.8, pairs at each pier, and a frieze panel between pairs. Close the blank band to at most 4% of the façade height.

**10. Queen Anne gables and towers (img08, img15).**
The gable is 45° and wider than the wall with an oversized sunburst. The tower is square, half the façade wide, with a single window.
*Fix:* gable pitch 55–60°, gable base = wall width with cornice returns, sunburst at most 0.25 × gable base, shingle field filling the gable below the apex. Tower round or octagonal (P 0.8), width ≤ 0.35 × façade, conical roof with height 1.3–1.6× its width, and banded windows that continue the bay below.

**11. Wall treatment (Paris, all).**
Vertical hairlines between every bay make the stone read as cladding panels. Real Haussmann ashlar shows faint horizontal coursing, strong bandcourses at the 2e and 5e balcony levels, and smooth wall otherwise.
*Fix:* drop the vertical lines. Add bandcourses at the 2e and 5e sills.

## Bottom line

Every Paris drawing is recognisably Paris, and every SF drawing is recognisably SF (R mostly 4). None passes as a real building to an expert (P is 3 at best). For Paris, the single largest gain would come from fixing balcony and storey hierarchy (giveaways 1 and 2) together with a proper base (3). For SF, the largest gain would come from removing geometry collisions and orphan parts (5), followed by giving bays three lights and an entablature on every floor (6).

Best of set: **img09** (Paris massing) and **img02** (SF Italianate bay). Weakest: **img15**, **img05**, **img08**.
