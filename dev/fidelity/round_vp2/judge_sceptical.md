# Sceptical blind judgement: round_vp2 (16 elevations)

Judge: independent architectural-history reading, blind. I looked only at `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and at photographs on Wikimedia Commons. I judged massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements, not the flat colour. Each drawing was assumed fake until it convinced me.

The set is two cities: **Paris** (8 drawings: 00, 03, 07, 09, 10, 11, 12, 14), residential immeubles from about 1830 to 1870, and **San Francisco** (8 drawings: 01, 02, 04, 05, 06, 08, 13, 15), wood-framed row houses from about 1870 to 1895 in the Italianate, Stick-Eastlake and Queen Anne styles.

What the photographs show, in the terms the drawings get wrong:

- **P1:** every window has a moulded surround (chambranle). The 2nd-floor windows carry cornices or pediments, and the balconies sit on visible stone consoles. The ground floor and entresol form one tall commercial band, and the floor heights fall visibly from the 2nd floor upward. There is one dormer per bay, each with its own railing. The chimney stacks are wide masonry walls on the party lines carrying several pots.
- **S1, S2:** every window has a heavy moulded hood (a segmental pediment or a flat cornice) on side brackets, with a bracketed sill. The cornice uses **paired** scroll brackets with frieze panels or rosettes between them. Corners carry rusticated quoin boards. The entrance is a **columned portico with a flat bracketed entablature**, not an arch.
- **S3:** the front gable spans the full width and the bay rises *into* the gable zone, with the gable cantilevered over it. Bargeboards and an eave overhang frame the gable, and shingle bands sit at floor lines.

## Per-drawing verdicts

R = recognisability (1-5), P = plausibility (1-5), Glance = would a specialist accept it at a glance.

### img00: Paris
- **Read as:** a July Monarchy / early Second Empire immeuble de rapport, about 1840-1860. It has 3 bays, shops either side of a central porte cochère, two continuous balconies, a mansard with one row of dormers and twin end chimneys.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The windows are bare holes. There is no chambranle, no cornice over the étage-noble windows, and keystones appear on one floor only (compare P1, where every opening is framed).
  - The balconies hang on tiny triangles instead of consoles. Both balcony floors are the same height as the floor below, so the étage noble is not taller.
  - The shopfront fascias are flat boxes with no pilasters and no entablature tying them to the masonry.
  - At 3 bays, the building is too narrow for its two continuous balconies. A 3-bay lot of this date more often has a single balcony on the 2nd floor and individual garde-corps elsewhere.
  - The chimneys are two identical square posts at the outer corners. Real stacks are wide walls on the party lines carrying pots (P1, P3).

### img01: San Francisco
- **Read as:** an Italianate row house, about 1870-1880, with a raised first floor over a basement, a stoop, a bracketed cornice and a two-storey bay.
- **R 3 · P 2 · Glance: WITH FLAWS** (a lay viewer passes it; a specialist does not)
- **Giveaways:**
  - The "bay" reads as a flat panel framed by doubled thin pilasters, with **one** window per floor. An SF slanted bay seen in elevation shows a wide centre light flanked by two narrow foreshortened side lights (S4). This reads as a porch screen, not a bay.
  - The entrance is a giant round-arched grey recess with tiny columns inside it. SF Italianates use a columned portico with a flat entablature (S1, S2).
  - The windows have only a keystone, with no hood or brackets, which is the period's single most diagnostic element (S1).
  - The cornice has single, evenly spaced, plain trapezoid brackets over a blank frieze. Real cornices have paired brackets with panels between them.
  - The bay's top entablature sits about 130 px below the main cornice, leaving a dead band of wall. On S4 the bay's own cornice tucks directly under the main frieze.

### img02: San Francisco
- **Read as:** an Italianate with a slanted bay, about 1875.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The bay is now drawn correctly as centre plus two narrow sides. However, it has **no top entablature**: the pilasters simply stop under a row of white dashes. It also has no panelled apron under the windows.
  - The pediments over the lower bay windows are present, but the upper floor has only keystones, which inverts the usual hierarchy where the top floor gets the lighter hood but still gets one.
  - The entrance has the same arched-recess problem as img01.
  - The basement has one window, offset under the bay. The real raised basement is usually a garage door or two windows.

### img03: Paris
- **Read as:** a Restoration-era immeuble, about 1830-1850, 3 bays, with a balcony on the 3rd floor, garde-corps on the 4th and a two-row mansard.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The mansard takes about 40% of the elevation height, with two full dormer rows on a roof face drawn as a vertical wall. Real brisis zones read at about 20-25% of the street height, and the lower slope leans back visibly (P1).
  - There is no balcony on the 2nd floor (the étage noble), yet one appears on the 3rd. This is the wrong floor for the hierarchy.
  - The window surrounds and chimney problems are the same as img00.
  - The door is a narrow window-width slot. A porte cochère is about 1.3-1.6 times a window's width and rises into the entresol.

### img04: San Francisco
- **Read as:** an Italianate with a slanted bay, mirrored (bay on the left), about 1875.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - **Clipping bug:** the left-hand side light of the bay is cut in half by the corner board. Its arch is truncated at x≈267 on both floors. This is visible even to a lay viewer.
  - The bay has an entablature only at the top floor and nothing between the floors except dashes.
  - The entrance has the same arched-recess problem as img01.
  - The corner boards are plain strips, not the rusticated quoin boards of S1.

### img05: San Francisco
- **Read as:** a Stick-Eastlake house, about 1885, with a square bay carrying a spindle fringe and a chamfered door hood.
- **R 3 · P 2 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The lower bay floor has **one huge undivided plate of glass** (about 360×400 px), an anachronistic picture window. Stick bays have tall, narrow double-hung sash, two or three to a face.
  - The upper and lower floors of the bay have different window sets, so the bay does not stack.
  - An Italianate cornice with plain trapezoid brackets sits over Stick spindlework. Stick cornices have tall incised brackets, often with a false gable or a panelled frieze.
  - The bay has no top entablature, and the dead band above it is about 160 px.
  - The basement window is a half-round. This is rare; the norm is rectangular openings or a garage.

### img06: San Francisco
- **Read as:** a Stick-Eastlake house, about 1885.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS** (best of the SF set)
- **Giveaways:**
  - The square bay with spindle fringes and the chamfered entry hood are right in kind, but the windows have no flat Stick hoods, no incised panels and no apron boards.

### img07: Paris
- **Read as:** an 1830s-1840s immeuble, 8 bays wide by 4 storeys, with an off-axis arched porte cochère in the end bay, two continuous balconies and one dormer every second bay.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS** (best of the Paris set)
- **Giveaways:**

  - The ground floor is residential and the same height as the upper floors, with no rustication (bossage) and no taller RDC.

### img08: San Francisco
- **Read as:** a front-gabled Queen Anne / Stick transitional house, about 1890, with a sunburst in the gable, a fish-scale shingle band, a square bay and a chamfered door hood.
- **R 4 · P 2 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The bay stops at the eave line. In S3 the bay rises into the gable, and the gable cantilevers over it with brackets.
  - The gable has **no eave overhang and no bargeboard**: the raking edge is a bare line.
  - The fish-scale band floats in the middle of the gable rather than marking the gable floor line.
  - The square Stick bay has round-arched Italianate sash. This style mix is rare in SF.

### img09: Paris
- **Read as:** a Haussmannian or post-Haussmannian immeuble, about 1860-1880, 8 bays, ground floor plus 5 floors plus a mansard.
- **R 4 · P 3 · Glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - **There is no continuous balcony on the 2nd floor.** That is the defining Haussmann marker (P1). Instead, individual balconettes repeat identically on the 2nd, 3rd and 4th floors, so the façade has no hierarchy.
  - The 5th-floor balcony stops short of the end bays. A balcon filant runs the full width.
  - The building is 8 bays wide by 7 levels, yet its entrance is a plain single-window-width door in the corner bay. A building this size has a porte cochère about 1.5 times a window's width, usually centred or marked.
  - The ground floor windows are the same size as the upper ones, with no entresol and no shop.
  - Thin full-width lines mark every floor where moulded bandeaux should be.

## Top giveaways across the set, ranked, with fixes

1. **Bare window openings in both cities.** This is the single biggest tell. Every period source frames every opening.
   - *Paris fix:*
     - Give every window a chambranle about 0.12 times the window width on each side.
     - On the étage noble (2nd floor), add a window cornice: a horizontal hood projecting about 0.15 times the window width, alternating with triangular or segmental pediments about 40% of the time.
     - On the 3rd and 4th floors, use a lintel with a keystone or an agrafe.
     - On the top floor, use a plain chambranle.
   - *SF fix:*
     - Give every window a hood of height about 0.25-0.3 times the window width. Use a segmental pediment for about 50%, a flat bracketed cornice for about 40%, and a "bonnet" arch for round-headed sash.
     - Carry the hood on two side brackets.
     - Add a bracketed sill about 0.08 times the window height.
     - On Stick houses, use flat hoods with incised sunburst blocks instead.
2. **No floor hierarchy in Paris (00, 03, 07, 09-14).**
   - *Fix:*
     - Set the floor-to-floor heights relative to a typical floor at: RDC plus entresol 1.6-1.8, 2nd floor 1.15, 3rd 1.05, 4th 1.0, 5th 0.9.
     - Put a continuous balcony on the 2nd floor in about 70% of 1855-1880 façades, and on the 5th floor in about 85%.
     - Use individual garde-corps or balconettes elsewhere, never identical balconettes stacked on three floors (img09).
     - Run the balcon filant over the full width (img09 truncates it).
3. **The SF bay is drawn as a flat panel (01, 04, 05, 06, 13) or lacks its entablatures (02, 04, 13).**
   - *Fix:*
     - Show a slanted bay as a centre light plus two side lights at about 0.7 times their true width (cos 45°).
     - Separate the lights with mullion posts, not pilasters set beyond the glass.
     - Give the bay its own bracketed cornice at the top, about 0.6 times the depth of the main cornice, sitting directly under the main frieze with no dead wall band.
     - Add an intermediate belt course between the floors, and panelled aprons about 0.25 times the window height under each window.
     - Show the bay's side return so that it projects.
     - Square Stick bays should show two narrow sash per face, never one plate (img05).
4. **The SF entrance is a giant round-arched recess (01, 02, 04), or has no shelter at all (13).**
   - *Fix:*
     - For Italianate (about 85% of entries), use a portico: two columns or square posts with Corinthian capitals, carrying a flat entablature with brackets that matches the main cornice and reaches about 1.1 times the door height.
     - Add an arched or rectangular transom over the door.
     - For Stick, use the chamfered hood already used in 05, 06, 08 and 15, which is fine.
     - Arched recessed entries should appear in under 10% of cases.
5. **The SF cornice uses single plain trapezoid brackets over a blank frieze (all 7 flat-roofed SF drawings).**
   - *Fix:* use **paired** scroll brackets at each bay division and at the ends. Each bracket should be about 0.3 times the cornice height wide. Put recessed frieze panels or rosettes between the pairs and a dentil course under the corona. Stick variant: tall incised brackets running into a panelled frieze. Add rusticated quoin boards at both corners: blocks of alternating length, block height about 1/12 of the storey height.
6. **The Paris chimneys are identical twin square posts at the outer corners on all 8 drawings.**
   - *Fix:*
     - Put the stacks on the party walls as wide masonry walls, 1.5-3 m wide and rising 1-2 m above the ridge, carrying 4-10 terracotta pots (mitrons).
     - Vary them per building, sometimes on only one side.
     - Show them in front of or behind the roof, not pasted on the roof face.
7. **The Paris mansard is too tall and drawn as a vertical wall (03, 10, 12, 14).**
   - *Fix:*
     - Keep the visible roof height to 18-25% of the eave height.
     - Draw the brisis at 70-80° with the upper slope rarely visible, using one dormer row in about 80% of cases.
     - Put one dormer per bay below in about 75% of cases. Never use 2 dormers over 3 bays (img11).
     - Give the dormers stone or zinc surrounds with alternating triangular and segmental heads, and a little railing (P1).
8. **The Paris entrance is undersized (03, 09-14).**
   - *Fix:* make the porte cochère 1.3-1.6 times a window's width, with its head reaching into the entresol band. Use a round arch or a heavy lintel in about 60% of cases and add a carved imposte. Centre it, or mark it with a vertical accent, on wide façades. Give the ground floor horizontal bossage channels, with the shopfront or entresol taller than the upper floors.
9. **Balcony construction in Paris is unconvincing.**
   - *Fix:* replace the tiny triangles with stone consoles, 2-3 per bay at about 0.3 times the window width. Align the railing's heavy posts to the window piers, with ornamental panels centred on the openings (img07 and img00 ignore the rhythm). Draw the slab as moulded stone, not a dark grey bar.
10. **SF gables and towers (08, 15).**
    - *Fix:*
      - Give the gables a 0.3-0.6 m eave overhang, a bargeboard and a cantilevered gable floor carried on brackets over the bay.
      - Let the bay rise into the gable.
      - Put the shingle bands at floor lines.
      - Make Queen Anne towers round or octagonal, set at a corner, with a conical or bell roof.
      - Never leave a half-gable or a stray box outside the outline (img15).
11. **Rendering bugs that betray the generator.** The corner board clips the bay's outer arch in img04 and img13. Every Paris drawing reuses the same door, dormer and chimney template, so a specialist viewing the set side by side spots the template at once.
    - *Fix:* clip the bay lights to the inside of the corner boards. Vary the door type, dormer head and chimney placement per building, for example with about a 30% chance of each alternative.

## Bottom line

The drawings get the big silhouette right: a Parisian mansard over cream stone with ironwork balconies, and an SF raised wooden row house with a stoop and a bracketed cornice. They also get the bay counts and rough window proportions right.

They fail on the second layer that a specialist reads first: window surrounds, floor hierarchy, how the bay is built and where its cornice sits, the type of entrance, paired brackets and real chimney stacks. Fixes 1 to 4 alone would move most of the set from WITH FLAWS towards YES.
