# Blind fidelity test: Amsterdam and Venice, rounds 1–3

Each round, 16 fresh, unlabelled drawings (6 Amsterdam, 6 Venetian Gothic,
4 Veneto-Byzantine, shuffled) went to independent judges who saw no code,
no captions and no key. Round 1 judged the original rules; round 2 the rules
rewritten from the independent review (`amsterdam/grading.md`,
`venice/grading.md`); round 3 after fixing round 2's six giveaways.

Caveats: judges differ between rounds (round 3 ran as separate sessions, and
the verdict wording differs: "with flaws" vs "pass-at-a-glance"), so treat the
trend, not the decimals. Round 2 had one judge only.

## What still gives the drawings away (round 3, both judges)

1. Template reuse visible side by side: cornice houses share one cornice,
   dormer and stoop; Venetian fronts share one roof, chimneys and plinth.
2. Context: every front stands on grass. Venice should stand on water with
   steps and a fondamenta; Amsterdam on a brick quay.
3. Glazing: Amsterdam windows read as plate glass; 1700–1780 houses have
   small-pane sashes (6-over-6 to 24 lights) in white frames. Venice has no
   shutters (scuri).
4. Venetian roofs are too prominent; canal palazzi read as a flat block under
   a dentilled cornice.
5. Veneto-Byzantine loggias use turned balusters (Renaissance); 12th–13th c.
   fronts have solid marble slabs (plutei) or nothing.
6. No relieving arches over Amsterdam windows, no quoins, carved claw pieces
   drawn as plain quarter circles, the stoop reads as a Brooklyn one to the
   fair judge.
