## What both judges found, and what changed for round 3

1. **A gap of sky between the cornice and the pretil (10 of 16).** The pretil now stands on the cornice's top fillet.
2. **Collisions.**
   - Pediments only go on the principal floor, and only where they leave room for the balcony corbels above. Otherwise the opening gets a hood.
   - Upper openings stop 1.2 m under the ceiling.
   - Entresuelo windows stop 1.0 m under it.
   - A portal's arches are true semicircles under a 0.8–1.1 m entablature, so their crowns clear the slab above.
3. **Supports that carry nothing.**
   - Portal columns rise to the arch springing or to the full depth of the beam.
   - Gallery posts always carry the gallery's tile cover.
4. **Rainbow mediopuntos everywhere.**
   - They now appear only on the principal floor and over the zaguán.
   - The glass is mostly clear: either alternate clear and coloured panes, or clear panes inside a ring of colour. Each house uses two muted colours.
   - The grid pattern is gone.
5. **Pilasters without capitals.** Pilasters now have capitals of 0.40–0.55 m and bases.
6. **Card crests and weak parapets.**
   - The crest is a scrolled cartouche on 85% of fronts, about one central bay wide.
   - Balustrades use vase-shaped balusters.
   - Plain pretiles have dies.
   - Iron-bar pretiles are rare.
   - Eclectic cornices sit on consoles on 60% of fronts.
7. **Short ground floors and blank walls.**
   - The ground-floor door head sits at 0.72–0.82 of the storey height.
   - The rejas run from 0.4–0.6 m up to that same line.
   - Iron rejas appear on 85% of later fronts.
   - Single-storey houses only occur on lots under 10.5 m.
8. **Floor hierarchy.**
   - The principal floor is 8–15% taller than the floor above.
   - A continuous balcony only appears on the principal floor, running from end to end with the guardavecinos at the party walls.
   - On fronts of four storeys or more, the top floor has no hoods.
9. **Openings with no guard.** A colonial front without balconies now has a railing across each opening.
10. **Colonial details.**
    - The tile roof shows only 0.6–1.0 m of slope.
    - A flat colonial roof has a cornice under its tile-capped pretil.
    - There are no pilasters under a tile roof.
    - 60% of colonial zaguanes have a portada of pilasters and an entablature.
11. **The bodega sign** carries lettering.

Still open: varied corbels and balcony railings, a wicket door in the zaguán, and corner buildings.
