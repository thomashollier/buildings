# Round 2, blind judgement (fair judge)

Judged from `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` only, compared against real photographs on Wikimedia Commons. Flat colour, sky and ground were ignored. I judged massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements.

**Scales.** R is recognisability: 1 means it could be anywhere, 5 means unmistakably this city. P is plausibility: 1 means it could not be built or would not be, 5 means a real building. "Expert at a glance" asks whether an architectural historian, shown it in a line-up of survey elevations, would accept it as a record of a real façade.

Mean R 3.4, mean P 2.7. None is a clean YES. The set reads as Havana because of the elements it uses (mediopuntos, persianas, portales, guardavecinos, rejas). It fails mostly on how those elements meet one another: pillars that stop short of their beams, posts that carry nothing, consoles that run into pediments and arches.

## Per drawing

### img00: yellow, 3 storeys, 4 bays, continuous balcony on the first floor
- **Right:** the continuous balcony is on the piano nobile and the upper floor has individual balconies. That is the correct hierarchy (C, H). The parapet has piers and finials on the bay axes.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The pediments of the first-floor windows run into the consoles of the second-floor balconies. The apex of each pediment touches the console teeth, with no clear wall between them. Real fronts leave at least 0.4–0.6 m of wall, or they drop the pediment on the upper floor.
  - Both floors carry identical triangular pediments. A real neoclassical front gives the pediments (often alternating triangular and segmental) to the piano nobile and plain cornice hoods to the floor above.
  - The central crest is a plain seven-sided slab with no cartouche, scroll or inscription. It reads as a house roof cut out of card. The parapet's central pier and finial poke through its lower edge.
  - The "pilasters" are hairlines with no capital or base. Compare the scrolled capitals in D.
  - The cornice modillions are tiny squares about 0.9 m apart and read as nail heads.
  - The ground-floor windows are short panelled shutters with no reja. Real ground floors use tall rejas or shop doors (F, G).

### img01: peach, 2 storeys, 2 bays, red top band
- **Giveaways:**
  - The upper persianas run to floor level with no railing or balconette. Nobody builds a full-height opening without a guard; in Havana every one has at least an iron balconette.
  - The bright red band at the top is not a recognisable element. A tile-capped pretil would show a moulded cornice under the coping.
  - The front is mostly blank wall, with a 3 m reja and a door in a 7 m-wide bay. It could be Seville, Mérida or San Juan, nothing says Havana.

### img02: turquoise, 3 storeys, 4 bays, bodegas
- **Right:** three roll-down shutters and one zaguán door is a believable Centro Habana mix (H, C). Individual balconies with panelled doors are fine.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The corner pilasters are plain white strips with no capital, so they never meet the cornice properly.
  - The crest is the same card slab as img00.
  - The attic band is a blank box with no balusters or pierced panels.
  - The consoles are identical small "teeth".
  - The railings repeat one circle-in-square motif.

### img03: yellow, 3 storeys, 4 bays, mediopuntos on every opening
- **Right:** paired corner pilasters, a continuous balcony on the first floor, and guardavecinos at the ends of the balcony are genuine Havana features.
- **Giveaways:**
  - There are 12 full-saturation rainbow mediopuntos on one façade, on every door and window including the ground floor. Real eclectic fronts use the fanlight sparingly, and the glass is mostly clear or white, with colour in the outer ring only (the Casa Bayona fanlights in A are white-mullioned with blue glass).
  - Equal-width wedges in strict red, yellow, green and blue rotation read as a toy pinwheel. Real mediopuntos have concentric rings of petal-shaped lights.
  - All the mediopuntos are identical in size, including the main door's. Overloaded vocabulary makes this one fake at a glance even though each piece is correct.

### img04: narrow, 2 bays, 3 storeys, recessed portal
- **Giveaways:**
  - The portal pillars stop about 0.6 m below the portal beam, so they support nothing. Real portales (B, C) have pillars or columns that rise to an arch springing or to the lintel.
  - The portal is trabeated, with a flat beam and no arches. That exists on some calzadas, but the beam is too thin, about 0.3 m against a real 0.6–0.9 m entablature.
  - The crest is about half the width of a two-bay front and is a plain slab.
  - The doors and the reja inside the portal are fine, but the reja is short for the storey.

### img05: blue, 3 storeys, 4 bays, mediopuntos on the upper floors, 3 shop shutters
- **Right:** the pilaster rhythm (paired at the ends, single between bays) and the stacked individual balconies are good. Mediopunto doors set in rectangular frames under a flat hood are believable.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The mediopuntos are again rainbow pinwheels.
  - Two identical upper floors with no hierarchy, both with hoods, balconies and fans.
  - The pilasters have no capitals and simply stop under the cornice.
  - The parapet is a blank band floating above the cornice.
  - With three shutters and one arched door, the door is the only arched opening on the ground floor, so the ground floor reads as two different buildings.

### img06: blue, 1 storey, wide
- **Giveaways:**
  - A front about 13 m wide with one reja and one door leaves over 60 % blank wall. Real one-storey houses in Centro Habana are 5–8 m wide, with a door and one or two floor-tall rejas, a moulded cornice and a pretil (E, F).
  - The red stripe at the top is the same unexplained band as img01.
  - There is no plinth. Reads as a shed.

### img07: turquoise, hipped tile roof, upper balconies, entresuelo windows, portal
- **Right:** the colonial vocabulary is correct: a tile roof, fan transoms set in rectangular door heads (as at Casa Bayona, A), individual balconies on corbelled slabs, turned iron balusters, and an entresuelo.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The portal pillars stop about 0.7 m short of the portal beam, the same fault as img04.
  - The entresuelo windows sit directly under the balcony consoles with no wall between them.
  - Colonial portales in Old Havana (Plaza Vieja, B) are arcaded on stone pillars, not flat-beamed.
  - The roof is a tall smooth trapezoid on a flat two-tone fascia. A real colonial roof shows a projecting tejaroz eave of corbelled tile courses (A) and very little roof slope from the street.

### img08: peach, hipped tile roof, 3 bays, 2 storeys (the best of the set)
- **Right:** this is close to Casa Bayona (A) and the houses on Calle Oficios. It has three balcony doors with fan transoms over persianas, individual balconies on stone slabs, a central zaguán with a fan transom, and floor-tall rejas with projecting sill and head. The rhythm and the proportions are good.
- **Giveaways:**
  - There is no tejaroz eave. The roof sits on a flat dark fascia.
  - The main door has no stone portada (pilasters and entablature, as A and G show). The entrance is the most elaborated element of a colonial casa, and here it is plain.
  - The hairline pilaster lines fall in the wrong places (not on the bay axes), so they read as joints, not pilasters.

### img09: yellow, hipped roof, 3 bays, white pilasters
- **Giveaways:**
  - White full-height pilasters with no capitals under a colonial tile hip. The neoclassical order and the colonial roof are mixed without a cornice to reconcile them; the pilasters die into the fascia.
  - The ground-floor rejas are only about 47 % of the storey height and sit high. Real rejas run from about 0.5 m above the pavement to near the lintel line of the door (F).
  - The main door has a plain grey transom. In this type it should be a fan or an iron grille (J).

### img10: narrow, 2 bays, 3 storeys, paired doors on the ground floor
- **Giveaways:**
  - The hierarchy is inverted. The continuous balcony is on the top floor and the individual balconies are on the floor below. Havana puts the long balcony on the principal floor (C, H; img00, img03 and img14 get it right).
  - Two identical carriage doors side by side on a 6–7 m front are unusual. One door plus one reja or shop is the norm.
  - The corner pilasters have no capitals.

### img11: stone (sillería) front, 2 bays, crest with urns
- **Right:** this reads as Old Havana stone. The crest with side urns is the most convincing crest in the set.
- **Giveaways:**
  - A blank white panel floats between the balcony consoles and the arched door, with no mouldings or lettering. It reads as a placeholder.
  - Checkerboard squares in the mediopunto arch are rare. Use radial petals.
  - The attic is a blank band with no balusters.
  - The arched zaguán sits next to a roll-down shutter of a different height, so the two lintels don't align.

### img12: blue, 2 storeys, iron-railed parapet, continuous balcony with guardavecinos
- **Right:** the continuous balcony with guardavecinos and the large arched zaguán with a fan are good Havana signals.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The parapet is iron bars like a fence. Havana parapets are masonry balusters or solid pretiles with piers (B, C).
  - The guardavecinos are set about 0.4 m in from the façade end. They exist to block the neighbour's balcony, so they belong at the party wall.
  - There is a large blank wall between the floors.
  - The ground-floor window carries an arched fan the same size as the upper doors, so the ground floor has no hierarchy of its own.

### img13: blue, 3 storeys, 3-arch portal
- **Right:** the arcaded portal with pilasters aligned over the piers, and repeated balcony doors above, is the Calzada de Reina / Monte type.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The arches are faceted seven-sided polygons, not semicircles. That is a drawing artefact any viewer sees.
  - The arch crowns rise into the balcony consoles of the first floor. Real portal arches stop under a frieze about 0.6–1.0 m deep that carries the floor (C).
  - The upper floors have no capitals on the pilasters.
  - The parapet is a wooden-looking picket fence. Use turned balusters at about 0.15–0.20 m spacing, with piers on the pilaster axes.

### img14: green, 5 storeys, 3-arch portal, continuous balcony on the first floor
- **Right:** the massing and the balcony hierarchy are good, and believable for Galiano or Reina c. 1920.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The arch crowns disappear behind the continuous balcony slab.
  - The crest is the card slab again, over an iron-bar parapet.
  - The openings are identical on all four upper floors, each with a keystone and a segmental cap, so there is no attic-floor reduction. Real 5-storey eclectic fronts shorten the top floor and simplify its surrounds.
  - The portal pillars don't line up with the corner pilasters above.

### img15: blue, hipped roof, wooden balcony with posts, entresuelo, tall blank ground floor
- **Right:** the green timber balcony with turned balusters and posts is a real Old Havana feature (E, Calle Oficios).
- **Giveaways:**
  - The balcony posts rise about 2.5 m and stop in mid-air. They carry nothing. In every real example (E) they hold a small tiled tejaroz roof over the balcony.
  - The balcony floor is a grey slab with tiny triangle brackets. Real ones sit on timber joist ends (canes) or carved wooden corbels.
  - The ground floor is about 5 m of blank wall with one short reja and a door pushed to one side.
  - The entresuelo windows are fine.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked, with fixes

1. **Structure that carries nothing: pillars, posts and arches that don't meet what they hold up** (img04, img07, img13, img14, img15). The eye of any expert goes here first.
   - *Fix:* portal pillars must rise to the beam soffit or the arch springing. Set the pillar height to (portal height − beam depth), with a beam of 0.6–0.9 m.
   - Arch crown ≤ beam soffit − 0.1 m, so it is never under the balcony slab.
   - Wooden balcony posts must end in a tejaroz: a tiled lean-to 0.8–1.0 m deep, 0.4–0.6 m high in elevation, with its eave line running the full balcony length.
   - Draw arches as true semicircles, or as segmental arches with rise/span 0.25–0.35, never as polygons.

2. **Collisions between stacked elements** (img00 pediments into consoles, img07 consoles onto windows, img13 and img14 arches into slabs).
   - *Fix:* enforce a minimum clear wall of 0.4 m between any opening head or pediment apex and the underside of the console above.
   - If a storey can't fit it, drop the pediment to a flat hood (cornice cap), or shorten the opening.
   - On the floor above the piano nobile, use pediments at most 20 % of the time.

3. **Rainbow pinwheel mediopuntos everywhere** (img03, img05, img12; checkerboard in img11).
   - *Fix:* use the mediopunto on at most the principal floor and the zaguán (probability ≈ 0.6 per building, not every opening).
   - Make 60–75 % of the glass clear or white, with colour only in the outer ring.
   - Use 2–3 concentric rings of petal-shaped lights with white muntins, not equal wedges.
   - Use a palette of 2–3 colours per building, with blue and red dominant, desaturated by about 30 %.
   - Use the variant of a fan set in a rectangular transom (A, img08) for colonial types.

4. **Pilasters without capitals or bases** (img00, img02, img05, img09, img10, img13, img14).
   - *Fix:* give every pilaster a capital 0.4–0.6 m tall (a simple Tuscan band, or scroll consoles as in D) and a base of 0.3–0.5 m.
   - Break the cornice forward over each pilaster (+0.05–0.10 m).
   - When the pilasters stop at string courses, add an impost block.

5. **Cartoon crests and parapets** (img00, img02, img04, img14 slab crests; iron-bar or picket parapets in img12, img13, img14; blank attics in img02, img05, img11).
   - *Fix:* make the crest a cartouche of 1.2–2.0 m width, with flanking scrolls and an oval or shield panel (B). Its width should be ≤ one central bay plus 20 %.
   - Make the parapet masonry: piers on the pilaster axes with turned balusters (0.15–0.20 m on centre) or solid pierced panels.
   - Use urns or finials on the piers with probability ≈ 0.5. Use an iron-bar parapet with probability ≤ 0.1.

6. **Ground floors under-designed** (short rejas in img09 and img15; blank expanses in img06 and img15; shuttered windows without rejas in img00).
   - *Fix:* ground-floor windows should be floor-tall rejas, with sill 0.4–0.6 m above the pavement and head at the door's lintel line, with projecting iron at 0.10–0.15 m (F).
   - Keep wall-to-opening ratio on the ground floor at ≤ 55 % wall.
   - Use one zaguán and the rest rejas or shopfronts, at ~3.5–4.5 m centres.

7. **Every door is the same brown 4-panel door with a plain grey transom.**
   - *Fix:* vary the zaguán: a fan or iron-grille transom (J) with probability ≈ 0.5, a postigo (wicket door) inside one leaf, and studs or rails.
   - For colonial types, add a stone portada: pilasters plus a flat entablature or an arched surround, 0.4–0.6 m wide each side.

8. **Uniform corbels and one railing motif.** Every slab uses 2–3 identical "teeth" and every railing uses circle-in-square.
   - *Fix:* corbels should be scroll ménsulas 0.4–0.7 m tall, 2 per individual balcony, and every 1.2–1.5 m on continuous ones. Give the slab edge a moulded profile.
   - Give railings 3–4 patterns (vertical balusters with collars; S/C scroll; lyre; cast-iron panels) and pick one per building.

9. **Colonial roofs without a tejaroz** (img07, img08, img09, img15).
   - *Fix:* replace the flat fascia with a projecting tile eave of 2–3 corbelled courses, 0.5–0.8 m projection.
   - Lower the visible roof rise to ≤ 1/10 of wall height. The street sees very little roof (A).

10. **Hierarchy errors.** img10 has its continuous balcony on the top floor. img05 and img14 have identical upper floors.
    - *Fix:* put the continuous balcony on the first floor with probability ≈ 0.9.
    - Make the top storey 10–15 % shorter than the piano nobile, with simpler surrounds (no pediment, smaller or no balcony) on the top 1–2 floors of 4–5-storey buildings.

11. **Openings with no guard** (img01: full-height persianas on the upper floor with no railing).
    - *Fix:* any opening whose sill is below 0.9 m on an upper floor must get a balconette or a full balcony.

12. **Vague or generic types** (img01, img06). They carry nothing Havana-specific.
    - *Fix:* even the simplest house needs a moulded cornice plus a pretil, a plinth band of 0.5–0.8 m, and floor-tall rejas.
    - Drop the red top stripe, or make it a tile coping on top of a cornice.
