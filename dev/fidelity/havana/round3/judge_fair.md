# Round 3: blind judgement of 16 elevations

Judge: an independent architectural historian, working blind. I looked only at `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and at photographs on Wikimedia Commons. I ignored the flat colour rendering and judged massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements.

Reference photographs (Wikimedia Commons, all viewed):

- **[A] Plaza Vieja (postcard, c.1910).** Shows a colonial house with a low hipped tile roof and arcaded portales, and eclectic neighbours. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Habana_-_Plaza_Vieja.jpg
- **[B] Calle Mercaderes, Habana Vieja (2016).** Shows a stone colonial palace with tall openings close under the cornice, individual iron balconies on moulded slabs, and ground-floor windows with full-height rejas. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Calle_Mercaderes,_Habana_Vieja,_La_Habana,_Cuba._agosto_de_2016.jpg

- **[D] Facade in Habana Vieja / Centro.** Shows dense bays, openings almost filling the storey, single balconies mixed with one long corner balcony, and entresuelo openings with rejas, not glazing. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fachada_de_un_edificio_de_La_Habana_Vieja_-_panoramio.jpg

Scale: R is Recognisability (1 = could be anywhere, 5 = unmistakably Havana). P is Plausibility as a real building (1 = impossible, 5 = could be surveyed). "Expert" is whether a specialist would accept it at a glance.

Mean R 3.4, mean P 3.0. Thirteen of the sixteen would pass at a glance with flaws, and none would pass clean. The neoclassical two-storey fronts (04, 06, 07) are the strongest. The two-bay colonial houses with hipped roofs (01, 09, 13) are the weakest.

### img00: colonial two-storey, three bays
- **What works:** the stone portada in the end bay, the full-height rejas on the ground floor, and the persianas behind individual balconies all agree with [B].
- **Blank wall above the upper openings.** The opening head sits about 34% of the storey height below the eave. In [B] and [D] that gap is about 10–15%.
- **No tejaroz.** The roof meets the wall in a flush dark band. Tiled colonial roofs ([A]) project 0.5–0.8 m on a tile or wooden eave.
- **Balcony railing.** The upper rail is drawn as a square lattice. Havana ironwork uses vertical bars with a mid-rail or scrolls.

### img01: colonial two-storey, two bays
- **Too few bays.** Only two openings sit on a wide front, so each pier is about 3.5 times the opening width. In [B] and [D] piers are about 1 to 1.5 times the opening width.
- **Blank wall above the upper openings.** The gap is about 36% of the storey. Combined with the hipped roof, the house reads as a Mediterranean villa, not as Havana.
- **Collision.** The door's cornice runs straight into the balcony brackets above it.
- **Upper leaves.** These are plain panelled leaves. Havana balcony doors are louvred persianas, or have a louvred upper half.

### img02: Centro eclectic, three storeys
- **Band of dark squares.** It sits over the ground floor and reads as a sign or a perforated fascia. I know no Havana precedent for it.
- **Bracket collision.** The brackets of the long balcony overlap that band.
- **Parapet pedestals off the pilasters.** The pedestals sit at roughly x = 200, 510, 818, 1127 and 1435. The pilasters sit at 270/355, 756, 1157 and 1558/1643. In [C] and [E] every pedestal stands on a pilaster or column axis.
- **Guardavecinos in the wrong plane.** These are the spiked fans at the ends of the long balcony. They stand perpendicular to the facade, so in elevation they should read as a narrow vertical spiked bar, not as a fan.
- **What works:** the roll-shutter shopfronts are believable for Centro.

### img03: entresuelo house, four bays
- **What works:** the storey order is right (ground floor, entresuelo, principal floor with balconies).
- **Ground floor.** Four identical double doors and no hierarchy. Only one should be the zaguán portón; the others should be windows with rejas or shop openings.
- **Entresuelo windows.** They are drawn as four-pane glazing. In [D] they are low openings with iron rejas or persianas.
- **Mediopunto fan.** It is drawn inside a rectangular dark box. A mediopunto is a semicircular arched head.
- **Band of dark squares** again.

### img04: neoclassical two-storey house with a long balcony
- **What works:** this is the most convincing front. The long iron balcony on many brackets, the end pilasters, the bracketed cornice and the balustraded pretil are close to the Cerro and Centro quintas.
- **Overframed upper doors.** Each has an arched head, then a rectangular frame, then a flat hood: three frames, where one surround would do.
- **Ground-floor fanlight out of scale.** It is wider than any upper opening, so it dominates the facade.
- **Persianas.** The upper leaves are solid. They should be louvred.

### img05: narrow rusticated two-storey house
- **What works:** the turned wooden balusters on the balconies are right for Habana Vieja.
- **Mediopunto in a box** again.
- **Door surround collision.** The door surround rises into the balcony brackets.
- **Blank attic.** A tall, blank attic band runs above the cornice.
- **Rustication.** The joints run uninterrupted behind every element. In [B] the rustication is coursed stone, broken by the openings and the cornice.

### img06: three-bay version of 04
- Strong overall, with the same faults as 04: overframed arched doors and planar guardavecinos fans.
- **What works:** the dark zócalo is a good touch.
- **Ground floor.** Flanking full-height rejas beside a central portón is the correct colonial arrangement.

### img07: plain three-bay neoclassical house
- **What works:** the rhythm is good and the portal stands in the end bay.
- **Blank wall above the upper openings.** The head-to-cornice gap is about 40% of the storey.
- **Token balcony slabs.** The slabs on two tiny teeth are too thin. Even a minimal balcón de antepecho has a moulded slab about 0.15–0.2 m deep with a profiled underside ([B]).

### img08: narrow eclectic house, two bays
- **Crowded pilasters.** Five pilasters (a pair at each end and one in the middle) for only two bays is crowded.
- **Brackets on pilasters.** Some cornice brackets land on pilaster tops.
- **No hierarchy between floors.** The second and third floors are identical; the principal floor should be taller and richer.
- **Ground floor.** Two identical portones and no window.
- **Remate.** It is a faceted polygon with an octagonal "eye". Real Havana remates are curved or segmental pediments, or cartouches with scrolls.

### img09: colonial house with an entresuelo, hipped roof
- **Roof type.** A hipped tile roof on a house with an entresuelo is unusual. By the time entresuelo houses were built (c.1780 onwards), most had flat azotea roofs behind a pretil.
- **Blank wall above the upper openings.** The gap is about 42% of the storey.
- **Too few bays** (two).
- **Entresuelo windows** are glazed four-pane windows, as in 03.

### img10: tall narrow rusticated house
- **What works:** the proportions are believable for a narrow Habana Vieja lot.
- **Floating hoods.** Each flat hood sits well above its arch, with a gap of about 0.5 m.
- **Ground-floor reja.** It has no sill or projecting base and runs straight into the ground. In [B] rejas stand on a stone sill about 0.4–0.6 m above the pavement.

### img11: five-storey apartment block
- **Repeated floors.** Four identical bays are stacked on four nearly identical storeys, so the facade reads as a copy-pasted pattern.
- **Ground floor.** Four identical portones.
- **Band of dark squares** again, with bracket collisions.
- **What works:** the end pilasters and the continuous balcony are right.

### img12: four-storey eclectic block
- **What works:** good overall. The fanlights are graded by floor, the parapet panels are solid, the remate is centred, and the ground floor mixes windows with rejas and a portón with a fan.
- **Repeated upper floors.** The top two floors are identical.
- **Remate** has the same faceted shape as in 08.

### img13: two-storey colonial house, two bays, hipped roof
- Same faults as 01: too few bays, blank wall above the openings, and a villa-like roof.
- **Unrelated doors.** The two ground-floor doors share no logic: one has a grey transom, the other a fan in a box.
- **No ground-floor windows.** Two doors and no reja reads as a garage block.

### img14: four storeys over portales
- **What works:** recognisably Reina or Galiano. The arcade carries a long balcony.
- **The portal is drawn as a wall.** The arches are shaded recesses with doors and windows framed inside them. In [E] the portal is fully open, and the doors sit 3–4 m back in a dark gallery.
- **Arch geometry.** The arches are faceted, with flattened shoulders, and the piers between them are thin pilasters, not columns with capitals.

### img15: three-storey eclectic house
- **Ground floor.** Good asymmetry: an arched portón with a fan and two rectangular openings.
- **Band of dark squares.**
- **Planar guardavecinos fans.**
- **Parapet pedestals off the pilaster axes.** Pedestals at about 190, 518, 832 and 1146; pilasters at about 220/250, 627, 1003 and 1380/1410.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked, with fixes

1. **Band of dark squares over the ground floor** (02, 03, 11, 15). It has no precedent and is the most "generated-looking" element in the set.
   - Fix: remove it.
   - If a commercial frieze is wanted, use a plain entablature (architrave, frieze, cornice) at about 0.6–0.8 m, or a lettered shop board on no more than 25% of commercial fronts.
   - Never let balcony brackets overlap it. Brackets need at least 0.15 m clearance.

2. **The same door in every ground-floor bay** (03, 08, 11, plus 02 and 15 in part). Every drawing uses the same four-panel brown double door.
   - One portón per facade, at 1.2–1.4 times the width of the other openings, with a postigo (wicket) in one leaf.
   - Other bays get windows with rejas (about 60%) or shop openings (about 40% in Centro).
   - The odds of two portones on a front of four bays or fewer should be no more than 0.1.
   - Vary the leaf: 6–10 panels, or a studded plank leaf, and use a grilled fixed light instead of the flat grey transom.

3. **Too much blank wall above the upper openings** (00, 01, 07, 09, 13: gaps of 34–42% of the storey).
   - Havana openings nearly fill the storey ([B], [D]). Set opening height to 0.75–0.82 of floor-to-floor height on the principal floor and 0.70–0.78 on others.
   - Put the head, or the hood above it, no more than 0.2 of the storey below the cornice or stringcourse.

4. **Too few bays on wide fronts** (01, 09, 13; to a lesser degree 04 and 08).
   - Pier width should be 1.0–1.6 times the opening width; bay pitch about 2.0–2.6 times the opening width.
   - When the lot is wide, add bays rather than widen the piers. A lot of 10–12 m should carry 3–4 openings.

5. **Guardavecinos drawn as a fan in the facade plane** (02, 06, 15).
   - In elevation, draw a vertical spiked bar or a narrow strip of spikes at the ends of the balcony, no wider than about 0.15 m.
   - Use them only where a balcony meets a party wall or a neighbouring balcony, not freely at both ends of every long balcony.

6. **Parapet pedestals off the pilaster axes** (02, 15; check 04).
   - Pedestals go on every pilaster axis and at the corners. Balustrade runs go only between pedestals.
   - Optionally make the parapet solid over the piers and balustraded over the openings ([C], [E]).

7. **Clashes between elements:** door cornices run into balcony brackets (01, 05), and brackets cross friezes (02, 11).
   - Add a layout check that keeps at least 0.2 m vertical clearance between a bracket tip and any element below it.
   - Shorten the brackets (no more than 0.5 of the slab-to-next-element gap) or lower the door's cornice.

8. **Mediopunto drawn as a fan inside a rectangular dark box** (03, 05, 13).
   - Use a fan only under a true semicircular head, where the arch springs at the top of the jambs.
   - Rectangular openings take a straight transom with iron bars, or a lucetas grid of small panes.

9. **Glazed four-pane entresuelo windows** (03, 09).
   - Use low windows (height about 0.6–0.8 of their width, or square) with an iron reja or a small balcón de antepecho, and wooden persianas instead of glass.

10. **No hierarchy between floors in tall blocks** (08, 11, 12 upper floors).
    - Make the principal (first) floor 1.10–1.15 times the height of a standard floor, and the top floor 0.88–0.95 times.
    - Give the principal floor the richest surround (pediment or fan). Make the top floor plainest: a flat lintel and no hood.

11. **Colonial roofs without an eave** (00, 01, 09, 13).
    - Add a tejaroz, a tile eave on wooden canes projecting 0.5–0.8 m, drawn as 2–3 courses.
    - Keep the visible pitch at 18–25° (the current pitch of about 21° is fine).
    - Use a hipped tile roof only on two-storey houses without an entresuelo, and on no more than about 30% of colonial fronts. The rest should be flat azoteas behind a plain pretil.

12. **Remate drawn as a faceted polygon** (08, 12, 14).
    - Replace it with a smooth segmental or curved pediment, or a cartouche flanked by scrolls.
    - Width: 0.8–1.2 of the central bay. Height: 1.0–1.5 times the pretil height.
    - Drop the polygonal "eye", or make it an oval oculus.

13. **Portales drawn as recessed arches in a solid wall** (14).
    - Open the arcade completely: a dark gallery void behind the arches, with shop doors set back and only faintly visible.
    - Use round columns or pilasters with capitals, about 0.5–0.7 m wide at 3.5–4.5 m centres.

14. **Smaller points:**
    - Square-lattice balcony rails (00): use bars with a mid-rail.
    - Floating hoods (10): place the hood within 0.15 m of the arch crown.
    - Rejas that reach the ground with no sill (10): give the window a 0.4–0.6 m stone sill.
    - Rustication drawn behind everything (05): break the joints at openings and string courses.
