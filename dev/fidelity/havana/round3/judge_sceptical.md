# Round 3: sceptical blind judgement (16 drawings)

Judge stance: each drawing is assumed fake until it convinces. Only `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and Wikimedia Commons photographs were consulted. Flat colour rendering is ignored; massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements are judged.

Scale: **R** = recognisability as a specific place and type (1 = could be anywhere, 5 = unmistakable). **P** = plausibility as a real building (1 = impossible, 5 = indistinguishable from a survey drawing). **Expert at a glance**: would a specialist in Caribbean colonial architecture accept it as a measured elevation of a real building? A drawing that fools a lay viewer but not a specialist is WITH FLAWS.

Verdict in one line: **the set reads as Havana to anyone, and to a specialist none of the 16 passes.** Ten are WITH FLAWS, six are NO, none is YES. The city is right. What gives the drawings away is a small kit of invented parts repeated on almost every sheet.

## Drawing by drawing

### img00
- **Reads as:** Havana (Habana Vieja), a two-storey colonial house of the late 18th century. Three bays, door to one side.
- **R 3 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS**
- **What gives it away:**
  - The roof is drawn as a hipped trapezoid with 45° ends across the whole front. This is a terraced house between party walls, so it cannot hip at the sides. Real fronts show a straight tile eave (D) or a flat parapet over a flat roof.
  - The upper windows stop about 34% of the storey height below the cornice, which leaves a large blank band. In colonial Havana the openings rise almost to the beams (A, B).
  - The door has a flat grey slab across the top that matches no Havana element. A real door has a solid head, an iron grille fanlight or a stone lintel.
  - The balconcillo corbels are tiny cones. Compare the deep moulded slabs in A and D.
  - There is no zócalo. Almost every real front has a painted or stone plinth (B, H).
  - The faint pilaster lines with no capitals or bases read as panel joints, not as architecture.

### img01
- **Reads as:** Havana, a narrow two-bay colonial house of about 1800.
- **R 3 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS**
- **What gives it away:**
  - The same hipped roof problem as img00.
  - The panelled cream upper doors are plausible, but the window heads stop far below the cornice again.
  - The balcony slabs sit on three identical "icicle" cones.
  - The reja is free-standing iron bars with a thick top and bottom cap. It has no stone frame, guardapolvo or sill plinth like the rejas in C and H.
  - The door has the grey slab transom again.

### img02
- **Reads as:** Centro Habana, an eclectic three-storey block of about 1905–1915 with a bodega or shop on the ground floor.
- **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS**
- **What works:** the roller shutters, the guardavecinos fans at the ends of the long balcony, the balustrade and the shaped attic crest are all genuinely local.
- **What gives it away:**
  - Every storey is the same height. Real eclectic fronts have a tall ground floor and a shrinking top floor (F, G).
  - The pilasters have no capitals. They are chopped into stacked segments by plain blocks at each floor line, and the pairing is uneven (two at each end, one in between).
  - A row of dark squares floats in a white strip above the shopfronts. It reads like a ticker tape, not like a frieze or a shop sign.
  - The arched heads are plain blue half-ovals with a keystone floating above them.
  - The balusters are lozenge-shaped spindles.
  - A building this size on a calzada would often stand over a portal (E, G).

### img03
- **Reads as:** Havana, a colonial house with an entresuelo (mezzanine), about 1790–1820, four bays.
- **R 3 · P 2 · NO**
- **What gives it away:**
  - The mezzanine windows are square, four-paned and glazed. Real entresuelo openings are small barred or louvred openings, or they get their own little balcony.
  - The same floating strip of dark squares appears above the ground floor.
  - The ground floor has four identical doors in a row, one of them with a fan for no reason. A colonial house has one portón (main door), and the other bays are rejas.
  - The parapet is a plain band with a red line along the top and no cornice moulding.
  - The upper-floor fans are dark wedges inside a square frame, so the fan is not inside an arch.

## Top giveaways across the set, ranked, with fixes

1. **The grey slab transom over doors** (on 10 of 16: 00, 01, 03, 07, 08, 09, 11, 13, 14, 15). This is the single most repeated false element. **Fix:** remove it. Give the door head one of these, by odds:
   - 45%: a solid wood head with the leaf running to the lintel.
   - 30%: a semicircular or segmental luceta filled with a wrought-iron radial grille (abanico), drawn as dark bars on a dark ground.
   - 15%: a stone lintel with a keystone.
   - 10%: coloured glass, and only on the patio side or behind a reja.

   Use one head type for every door on a given front.

2. **Identical cone corbels under every balcony** (all 16). **Fix:**
   - For a balconcillo, draw a moulded stone slab with a projection of 0.6–0.8 m and an edge thickness of 0.18–0.25 m (about 1/12 of the opening width). Put 2 consoles under it, each about 1.5× the slab thickness tall and S-profiled, or use none and give the slab a stepped soffit.
   - For a long balcony, use 60% iron scroll cartelas at the bay axes, never more than 2 per bay, or 40% stone consoles at the pilaster axes only.
   - Never use a row of equal cones at an even spacing.

3. **Upper windows too short, leaving a dead band of wall** (00, 01, 05, 07, 09, 13). The blank band is currently 34–43% of the storey. **Fix:** put the opening head at 85–90% of the clear storey height, so the gap to the cornice soffit is 0.4–0.6 m. Keep the opening's height to width at 2.4–2.8:1, keep the sill at floor level for a balcony door, and keep the balcony rail at about 1 m.

4. **Hipped roofs on houses between party walls** (00, 01, 09, 13). **Fix:**
   - 65%: a flat roof (azotea) behind a parapet (pretil) 0.6–0.9 m high, with a moulded cornice and 1–3 gárgolas.
   - 30%: a tejaroz, a tile roof sloping to the street with its ridge parallel to the street. Show it as a horizontal band of tile courses 0.4–0.7 m high projecting 0.6–0.9 m on rafter ends (canecillos) (D, H), with no side hips.
   - 5%: hips, and only on a corner lot.

5. **Pilasters without capitals, chopped into stacked segments** (02, 04, 06, 08, 10, 11, 12, 15). **Fix:**
   - Every pilaster gets a base and a capital, and the capitals line up directly under an entablature.
   - Either superpose the orders (Tuscan or Doric on the ground floor, Ionic above, Corinthian or composite at the top), or use one giant order across two storeys (I).
   - Pilaster width is 1/10–1/8 of the storey height.
   - A balcony slab must die into the pilaster or pass behind it; it must not slice through it.
   - Pair pilasters only at the ends, with a gap of at least 0.5× the pilaster width, at about 40% odds; otherwise use singles.

6. **A floating strip of dark squares above the ground floor** (02, 03, 11, 15). **Fix:** delete it. Over a commercial ground floor, use a proper entablature (architrave, plain frieze, then cornice, 0.6–0.9 m in all) or a shop fascia with painted lettering at about 25%. Dentils, if any, belong inside the cornice moulding and are small, at about 1/3 of the cornice height.

7. **Equal storey heights on multi-storey fronts** (02, 08, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15). **Fix:** on eclectic fronts, use these heights relative to a typical floor of 1.0:
   - ground floor 1.3–1.45 (with a mezzanine about 20% of the time)
   - piano nobile 1.1
   - typical floors 1.0
   - top floor 0.85–0.9

   Vary the window detail by floor as well: the richest surrounds on the piano nobile, the plainest at the top.

8. **Portal missing or drawn as blind arches** (11, and 14 drawn wrongly; 02 and 12 are candidates). **Fix:** fronts of 3 or more storeys on a calzada get a portal about 55% of the time. The portal has free-standing columns with capitals on the front plane, either arched (60%) or carrying a flat entablature (40%). The bay is 3.5–4.5 m and the portal 4.5–5.5 m high, and the doors are drawn set back and darker. Never draw it as recessed blind arches.

9. **Lozenge spindle balusters** (02, 04, 06, 08, 10, 14, 15). **Fix:** draw vase balusters 0.75–0.85 m tall with a maximum bulge of about 0.13–0.16 m. Leave a gap between balusters of about 0.5–0.7× the bulge, and draw them on a solid plinth 0.10–0.15 m high. Put solid dados at the pilaster axes, at most every 3–4 m. That gives about half as many balusters as now, each about three times fatter.

10. **A crest with floating pinnacles** (08, 12, 14). **Fix:** stand the remate on the parapet, centred, about 1/4–1/3 of the front wide. Use a shaped pediment or a cartouche, with a year or initials at 50%. Put urns or pinnacles only on pedestals over the pilaster axes, never detached in the sky.

11. **Coloured-glass fans facing the street** (04, 06, 10, 12, 15). **Fix:** street-facing fans are 70% iron grille, 20% plain glazing behind a reja, and 10% coloured glass. Draw coloured mediopuntos large mainly on loggias and patio galleries.

12. **No zócalo** (00, 01, 03, 04, 05, 07, 13, and others). **Fix:** use a plinth band 0.6–1.0 m high, darker than the wall, on about 90% of fronts. It runs continuously, and door and reja frames stand on it (B, H).

13. **Rejas without frames or plinths** (00, 01, 07, 09). **Fix:** set each ventana de reja in a moulded frame 0.15–0.25 m wide, on a projecting sill or plinth 0.4–0.6 m above the pavement. Draw it running to within about 0.3 m of the storey head, with a small guardapolvo (drip cap) above it (A, C, H).

14. **Generic four-panel doors** (all 16). **Fix:** draw the colonial portón at 80–88% of the ground-storey height. Put a postigo (wicket door, about 0.8 × 1.9 m) in one leaf about 60% of the time, show a nail-head grid, and frame the door in a stone portada (jamb pilasters with an entablature or a moulded architrave with ears) on 18th-century fronts.

15. **Square glazed mezzanine windows** (03, 09). **Fix:** draw them as small openings about 0.9 × 1.0 m with turned wood or iron bars or louvres, aligned over the ground-floor openings below. About 25% of the time, give the mezzanine its own small balcony.

16. **Clones within the set** (04 and 06, 00 and 07, 12 and 14 share a crest). This is a lesser point, but a specialist looking at a street would notice it. **Fix:** randomise the cornice type, the head type, the balcony type (wooden on the colonial fronts at about 30%) and the pilaster scheme independently, so that no two fronts share more than about half of these choices.

## What would move a drawing to YES

On the colonial fronts (00, 01, 05, 07), fixes 1, 2, 3, 4 and 12 together would likely give P 4. On the eclectic fronts (02, 06, 15), fixes 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 9 would likely give P 4. Both estimates are my inference, not tested. None of these changes needs new building types. They correct parts that are already being drawn.
