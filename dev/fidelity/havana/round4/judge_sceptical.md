# Round 4 — sceptical blind judge (Havana)

Judged blind from `images/img00.png`–`img15.png` only, plus Wikimedia Commons photographs. No other repo file was opened. Flat colour rendering is ignored; massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements are judged. Every drawing is treated as fake until it convinces me. "WITH FLAWS" means a lay viewer would accept it but a specialist would not.

## Per-drawing verdicts

R = recognisability as Havana (1–5). P = plausibility as a real building (1–5).

### img00 — cream, 4 storeys, 3 bays, crest
- **Guess:** Centro Habana, neoclassical-eclectic tenement, c. 1890–1915.
- **R 3 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - Three upper storeys are identical (same height, same arched opening, same balcony). Real buildings of this type (Ref F, Ref D) grade the storeys: the piano nobile is tallest and richest, the top floor lowest and plainest.
  - The ground floor is no taller than the upper floors. In Centro Habana the planta baja is usually 5–6 m against 4–4.5 m above (Ref C).
  - The arched heads of the two top floors are solid blue-grey half-discs. They read as holes, not as a mediopunto, a louvred fanlight or glazing.
  - Paired corner pilasters plus single intermediates, with a plain box "capital" at every floor line and no actual capital. In Ref F the order has real capitals and the entablature breaks forward over each pilaster.
  - The crest (cartouche with oval and two flame finials) sits on top of the balustrade like a sticker, with no scrolls or base tying it in.

### img01 — mint, 4 storeys, 4 bays, crest, continuous first-floor balcony
- **Guess:** Centro Habana / Calzada de Reina eclectic, c. 1900–1920.
- **R 3 · P 2 · Expert at a glance: NO**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The consoles under the continuous balcony are evenly spaced with no regard for the pilasters. One sits squarely on the centre pilaster (x≈920 at display scale) and others clip the pilaster edges. No builder lets a console collide with a pilaster.
  - The cornice modillions also ignore the pilaster grid, and the pilasters run up *in front of* the cornice frieze rather than the entablature breaking over them.
  - The crest is offset from the parapet piers and floats above a parapet that is just a coping rail, with seven thin finial posts. It reads as clip-art.
  - The four bays at four storeys are again all identical. The ground floor has only plain brown door leaves, with no rejas (barred windows), which is unlike any street-front ground floor in Ref C or Ref E.
  - The guardavecinos (the side spikes at the balcony ends) project beyond the building line into the neighbour's air space without a party wall to justify them.

### img02 — salmon, 2 storeys, 5 bays, tiled hip roof, timber gallery
- **Guess:** reads as Trinidad, Remedios or Cartagena (or even the New Orleans French Quarter), not Havana; 19th c.
- **R 2 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: NO (for Havana)**
- **Giveaways:**
  - A full hip roof seen in elevation, plus a second pent eave carried on thin blue posts over a continuous timber balcony, is the provincial Cuban or Gulf-coast type. Havana's intramuros houses of this size are overwhelmingly flat azotea with a pretil (Ref B, Ref E, Ref G), or have a short tiled tejaroz, not a two-tier roof.
  - The double eave (main hip plus gallery skirt) produces a doubled roof edge that no specific building I can name has.
  - The upper doors are full-height louvred leaves directly behind a balustrade, with no wall between the gallery posts and the openings. Fine for Trinidad, not for Havana.
  - The ground floor is plain, with no zócalo (base band), no sill under the rejas and no portada at the door.

### img03 — mint, 3 storeys, 2 bays, polychrome fan door, balustrade
- **Guess:** Centro Habana / Habana Vieja 19th-c. neoclassical house, c. 1850–1890.
- **R 3 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Good:** the hierarchy is right. The piano nobile has a continuous balcony and a hood mould, and the top floor has individual balconies. The polychrome yellow/blue fan is the most mediopunto-like of the set.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The top floor again has solid blue arched heads.
  - The door fan has a keystone that protrudes above the arch extrados, sitting on nothing.
  - The ground-floor reja has cream slab caps above and below (like a shelf), not a stone sill and a guardapolvo.
  - There are only 2 bays on a 3-storey façade, with the door + window pair at a third of the width. That is plausible, but the pilaster pairs at both ends make it look like a slice cut from a wider building.

### img04 — sand ashlar, 2 storeys, two-arch portal, red coping
- **Guess:** Habana Vieja colonial casa with soportal, late 18th c. (Plaza Vieja type).
- **R 4 · P 3 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Good:** the portal-plus-balconies parti and the sillería coursing are right for Ref A and Ref E.
- **Giveaways:**
  - The coursing lines stop at the first-floor line. The ground floor and arch spandrels are smooth, as if a different material.
  - The rear-wall openings inside the arches rise into the arch. The left door's transom touches the arch crown, and the right reja's top rail pokes outside the intrados. A portal's back-wall openings sit well below the crown.
  - Dark vertical slots above the outer imposts (between the spandrel and the end wall) read as a drawing error, not a soffit.
  - The arches have no archivolt or impost moulding. They are just a hole with faceted curves.
  - The internal pier is about 1/7 of the span. Plaza Vieja supports are Tuscan columns or piers of roughly 1/4–1/5 span (Ref A).
  - The tall blank attic above the cornice is capped by a thin red stripe. Colonial azoteas end in a cornice plus a low pretil, not a 1.5 m attic with a red line on top.
  - The balconies are thick white slabs on three cone teeth. In this period they were wooden (turned balusters, often a tejaroz above) or iron on scrolled consoles.

### img07 — mint, 5 storeys, 2 bays, very tall and narrow
- **Guess:** Centro Habana apartment building, c. 1915–1930.
- **R 3 · P 2 · Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS**
- **Giveaways:**
  - Five identical storeys. Real 5-storey Centro Habana blocks vary storey and opening types, and the top storey is often set back or given a different order.
  - The balcony balustrades are cream bars drawn like masonry balusters but at iron-railing scale.

  - Two plain doors on the ground floor and no shopfront, which on a narrow 5-storey Centro Habana building is unusual. These almost always have a commercial ground floor (Ref C shows roll-up shutters).
  - Ghost quarter-lines on the wall.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked, with fixes

1. **One kit, visibly reused.** The same vocabulary recurs across 10+ drawings: paired corner pilasters with box "caps" at every floor, the identical floating crest (00/01/05/08), three-cone balcony slabs, a fan over the portón, and solid dark arched heads. Shown as a set, they read as one generator in under ten seconds.
   *Fix:* draw per building from mutually exclusive schemes, not additive parts:
   - Pilasters: paired 20%, single giant order 30%, none (plain or rusticated corners) 50%.
   - Crest: at most 15%, only with an odd bay count and a central door.
   - Fan over the door: at most 30%. Otherwise use a rectangular iron transom grille, as already in 10/11/15.
   - Balcony supports: iron S-consoles 50%, stone ménsulas with volutes 30%, a plain thin slab 20%.

2. **Consoles and modillions ignore the pilaster grid** (01, 07, 11, 14, 15; modillions in 01). This is the single fastest tell for an architect.
   *Fix:* place consoles on pilaster axes and subdivide each bay evenly between them. Reject any console within 0.25 m of a pilaster edge. Modillions likewise: one on every pilaster axis, then an equal pitch of 0.6–0.9 m within each bay. Break the entablature forward over each pilaster (a ressaut of 5–10 cm) instead of drawing the pilaster over the frieze.

3. **The portal arcade is drawn as holes, and the rear wall clashes with it** (04, 06, 09, 12, 13).
   *Fix:*
   - Keep the rear-wall opening head (including any transom, fan, keystone or reja cap) at least 0.5 m below the arch crown and inside the intrados.
   - Give arches an archivolt band of 0.25–0.35 m and an impost moulding.
   - Pier or column width at 0.2–0.25 of the span, with base and capital. Use Tuscan columns 60% of the time in Habana Vieja (Ref A), and coupled Composite columns or piers for Prado types (Ref D, Ref F).
   - Delete the dark slots above the outer imposts.

4. **No storey hierarchy.** Equal storey heights and identical openings on every floor appear in 00, 01, 05, 07, 08 and 15.
   *Fix:*
   - Storey heights: ground floor 5.5–6.0 m, piano nobile 4.8–5.2 m, second floor 4.2–4.5 m, top floor 3.6–4.0 m.
   - Opening heights scale with the storey (about 0.6–0.65 of storey height).
   - Ornament decreases upward: the piano nobile gets a hood, pediment or continuous balcony, the top floor gets a plain surround.
   - Do not give the top floor alone the arched heads, which inverts the hierarchy.

5. **Arched heads are solid dark half-discs or "bun" lids** (00, 01, 03, 05, 08, 09, 11, 13).
   *Fix:* fill semicircular heads with one of these: a louvred fanlight (persiana) with radial slats 40%, glazing with radial plus concentric muntins 40%, or a coloured mediopunto 20%. For segmental heads, make the rise 1/6–1/4 of the span, springing at the jamb line, with the surround following the curve. Never draw a half-ellipse sitting on top of a rectangle.

6. **Mediopunto styling and overuse.** Alternating two-colour sunbursts (especially red/white in 13) read as a Georgian fanlight.
   *Fix:* draw concentric bands, an inner fan of 8–12 radial panes plus 1–2 outer rings of rectangular panes, in 4–5 colours (red, blue, amber, green, clear). Allow one row of them per façade at most.

7. **Too plain for the Eclectic types they claim to be** (00, 01, 05, 08, 15). Compare Ref F.
   *Fix:*
   - Add capitals to the pilasters: height about 1.2× pilaster width, with an astragal and an abacus.
   - Add moulded architraves of 0.15–0.20 m around every opening.
   - Add a keystone that is part of the architrave, not floating on top of it.
   - Optionally add a festoon or cartouche panel under the piano-nobile sills (odds 40%).

8. **The floating crest** (00, 01, 05, 08).
   *Fix:* seat it on a pedestal block that interrupts the balustrade, add flanking scrolls of about 0.6 m, and set its width to 0.8–1.0 bay. Or omit it (see item 1).

9. **Ghost vertical lines** with no relief and no relation to the openings (06, 07, 08, 10, 13, 14).
   *Fix:* remove them, or turn them into real pilaster strips (with a width, a shadow edge and caps) placed on bay boundaries.

10. **Tall blank attic with a red stripe on top** (04, 06, 10, 12).
    *Fix:*
    - Colonial flat roof: put the cornice at the top, with a pretil 0.8–1.0 m high capped by a moulded coping. No red line.
    - Or, 30% of the time for Habana Vieja colonial: a tiled tejaroz/alero projecting 0.4–0.6 m on carved wooden brackets.

11. **Openings at grade.** Rejas and doors stop at ground level.
    *Fix:* add an acera curb 0.15–0.20 m high. Rejas sit on a stone sill 0.4–0.6 m above the sidewalk and project 0.15–0.25 m, with a cap rail and finials. The portón gets one step.

12. **Ground-floor windows without rejas, and rejas drawn as printed shutters** (01 has none; 11 is unbarred; 08/10/14 are ambiguous).
    *Fix:* every street-level window gets an iron reja: square bars at a pitch of 0.10–0.12 m with 1–2 horizontal bands, drawn in a distinct iron colour *in front of* the shutter.

13. **The type is outside Havana** (02).
    *Fix:* for a Havana palette, drop two-tier hipped roofs on post galleries, or keep them at 5% or less and only for 1–2 storey houses outside the walls (Cerro, Regla). Otherwise default to an azotea with a pretil.

14. **The wall-to-void ratio is too wall-heavy** (07, 11, 14).
    *Fix:* aim for a void width/bay width ratio of 0.45–0.55 in Habana Vieja and Centro. Bays of 3.5–4.5 m with openings of 1.6–2.2 m.
