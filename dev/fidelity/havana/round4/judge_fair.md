All 16 read unambiguously as **Havana**. None reads as Seville, San Juan or New Orleans except No. 02, which drifts toward provincial Cuba or the Gulf Coast. The vocabulary is right throughout: medio-punto fans, rejas with turned bars, louvred puertaventanas, iron balconies, balustraded azoteas, portales and zócalo plinths. What gives them away is syntax rather than vocabulary. Elements are correct one by one but assembled in ways no builder of the period would assemble them.

## Per-drawing notes

### img00: Centro Habana eclectic, 4 storeys, 3 bays
R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS
- **Pilasters pass straight through the cornice frieze.** They have no capitals, and doubled pairs sit at both corners with a sliver of wall between them. In Ref A the pilasters stop under an entablature with capitals and the cornice breaks forward over them.
- **The upper arched heads are solid flat dark lunettes.** They read as awnings or blind panels. Real heads are glazed fans (vitral) or carved wooden or shell fans (Ref A).
- **The bays are too wide for their openings.** The opening is about 0.40 of the bay. In Refs A and B openings fill about 0.5–0.6 of the bay.
- **All three storeys are the same height.** The ground floor should be 1.15–1.3× an upper floor.
- **The crest floats above an iron roof railing.** A crest sits on a masonry pretil or balustrade, on axis. An iron railing as the azotea parapet is atypical.
- The zaguán door surround extends below the ground line.

### img01: same type, 4 bays, continuous piano-nobile balcony
R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS
- **The crest is detached.** There is visible sky between the parapet rail and the cartouche base. Finials balance on a thin rail.
- The pilasters have the same faults as No. 00: doubled at the corners, no capitals, running through the cornice.
- The continuous balcony slab runs past the facade edge. The erizo (balcony-end spike guard) is drawn as a hatched pole.
- Every upper opening is a solid door. That is plausible for colonial work but monotonous with the eclectic crest.
- The ground floor has 3 identical shop doors plus a zaguán. That is good and typical.

### img02: two storeys under a hipped tile roof with a full-width timber gallery
R 3 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS

- **The gallery posts are bare sticks** with no base, capital or zapata (bracket), and the gallery roof butts the wall with no alero detail.
- The roof is drawn as two stacked red bands with no visible eave, rafter ends or tejas.
- Hairline bay divisions on the ground floor do not correspond to anything.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked

**1. Pilasters with no capitals that run through the cornice. Seen in 00, 01, 03, 05, 08, 11, 14, 15.**
This is the single most obvious tell for anyone trained. A pilaster is an order element: base, shaft, capital, then the entablature above.
- Fix: stop the shaft under the architrave and add a capital 0.6–1.0× the pilaster width tall.
- Break the cornice and frieze forward over each pilaster by 0.05–0.10 m.
- Doubled pilasters only at the arris, with a gap of 0.5–1.0× pilaster width, and in at most about 20% of buildings.
- Suggested odds: pilaster at every bay line 45%, end pilasters only 35%, flat wall with rusticated quoins or none 20%.

**2. Floating or detached crests and finials. Seen in 00, 01, 05, 08, 15.**
- Fix: the crest (remate) sits on the parapet with a continuous base and is never separated by sky. Width is 0.25–0.35 of the facade, and it is centred on the axis bay.
- Give a crest to at most 30% of buildings, mostly those of 3 storeys or more with an odd bay count.
- Pinnacles (jarrones or macetones) sit on balustrade pedestals, never on a thin rail.

**3. Geometry collisions. Seen in 00, 01, 03, 04, 05, 06, 08, 09, 12, 13, 15.**
- Door surrounds drop below grade (several of these).
- Transoms are clipped by arches (04, 06, 12).
- Surrounds spill outside arch voids (09, 13).
- Fix: clamp all surrounds to y ≥ grade. Inside a portal arch, set opening width ≤ span − 2×0.3 m and keep the opening head below the intrados at that width; drop the opening, never the arch.

**4. Portal construction. Seen in 04, 06, 09, 12, 13.**
- The piers sit behind the wall plane with dark slots at the ends.
- The arches are faceted.
- There are too few, too wide arches.
- Fix: piers flush with the facade, and the end pier is a solid wall return 0.8–1.2 m wide.
- Span 2.8–4.0 m, pier 0.6–0.9 m, 3–6 arches per frontage, and the portal runs the full frontage.
- Draw a true semicircle with at least 48 segments.
- Supports: round Tuscan columns with flat or arched heads about 40% (Refs A and C), square piers with impost moulding about 60% (Ref B).

**5. Solid flat lunettes and "lid" segmental heads. Seen in 00, 01, 03, 05, 08, 09, 11, 13.**
- Fix: a round-headed opening carries a glazed fan with radiating muntins, or a shell or wooden fan as in Ref A. Never draw a solid dark half-disc.
- A segmental head is the top edge of the opening itself, with a rise of 1/6–1/8 of span, plus an optional keystone. It is never a cap that sits proud of the jambs.

**6. Openings too narrow and storeys all equal. Seen in most drawings, worst in 00, 05, 08, 11.**
- Fix: opening width 0.45–0.6 of bay width.
- Upper-floor opening height to width 2.4–3.0:1.
- Ground floor 1.15–1.3× an upper floor, with the top storey 0.85–0.95× the piano nobile.
- The piano nobile carries the fans or the continuous balcony and gets the tallest openings.

**7. Balconies that do not behave like balconies. Seen in 01, 06, 11, 12, 14.**
- The railing is interrupted by a door (06).
- The slab overruns the facade edge (01, 11, 14).
- Solid panels stand in for railings (12).
- Fix: the railing runs continuously across openings. A continuous slab ends 0.1–0.3 m inside the facade edge or at the party wall.
- Slab 0.12–0.20 m thick, projecting 0.6–1.0 m.

**8. Generic tooth brackets under every balcony and cornice. Seen in all drawings.**
- Fix: use scrolled iron cartelas (iron balcony brackets), carved stone ménsulas (consoles, as in Ref E) or timber beam ends on colonial houses (Ref D).
- Use 2 per individual balcony up to 1.5 m wide, and space them at 0.9–1.2 m under continuous ones.
- Size modillions to the cornice. Their depth should be 0.5–0.7 of the corona height, not small squares.

**9. Erizos drawn as hatched ladder poles. Seen in 01, 07, 09, 15.**
The idea is right, since spiked balcony separators at party walls are very Havana. The drawing is wrong.
- Fix: a quarter- or half-circle fan of radiating spikes about 0.9–1.2 m high, mounted on the balcony end at the party wall only.

**10. Colonial tops ending in a tall band with a red stripe. Seen in 04, 06, 10, 12.**
- Fix: end at the cornice, or with a low pretil (parapet) 0.4–0.7 m above it. If tile is meant, show a tejaroz (tiled eave) or alero (projecting eave) with tile ends, not a flat red line.

**11. One element kit everywhere.**
The same 4-panel brown portón, the same fan, the same cartouche and the same railing roundel appear across all 16. A street of these would read as a stage set.
- Fix: vary panel counts (6, 8 or 12), add a postigo to about 60% of portones and studs to about 40% of colonial ones.
- Rotate 3–4 railing patterns: plain bars with collars, lyres, C-scrolls, and roundels.
- Vary the fan design between radiating, concentric and geometric glazing.

**12. Hairline "bay" divisions with no articulation. Seen in 02, 06, 08, 09, 10, 12, 14.**
- Fix: either articulate the line as a pilaster or a rusticated strip, or delete it.

### What is already right and should be kept
- The piano-nobile hierarchy expressed by fans and continuous balconies (03, 08, 09, 13).
- Entresuelo windows (06).
- Roller-shutter commercial ground floors (15).
- Rejas with wooden guardapolvos (10).
- Balustraded azoteas.
- Zócalo plinths.
- The off-centre zaguán in odd-bay houses.

No. 10 is the closest to passing as real. Nos. 08 and 14 are next, needing only fixes 1, 2 and 7.
