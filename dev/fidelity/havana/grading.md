# Old Havana model: grading, round 1

Assessor brief: grade `typologies/havana/havana.toml` and `docs/strips/havana.png` against the two round-1 evidence files. I wrote neither the model nor the evidence. I changed no file but this one.

**Inputs**
- Model: `src/buildings/typologies/havana/havana.toml` (commit 467fc51); strip `docs/strips/havana.png`, rendered from the same commit. Panels: colonial 1720 11 m, colonial 1760 14 m, neoclassical 1820 plaza portal, neoclassical 1850 12 m, eclectic 1905 14 m, eclectic 1920 18 m.
- `observer.md`: 22 buildings from 38 photos, blind to the rules. Strongest on plaza palaces and one H-rated 19th-century tenement (#16/17/19).
- `adversary.md`: about 30 buildings from 43 photos, plus 12 extra renders and a 300-derivation tally. Strongest on ordinary streets (Calle Brasil, Empedrado).
- Both are photo estimates, ±15-20%. Where they agree I treat the point as settled. Where they disagree, the weighing is in §2.

**Overall: C−.** The skeleton is right: the three manners by date, the storey heights, the entresuelo, the pastel palette, and pilasters by manner. What the eye reads first is wrong:
- glass sash grids in openings that should be wooden leaves;
- openings too tall and too close together;
- a black-void portal;
- a colonial roof and gallery that look like neither;
- one flat colour per front.

## 1. Grades

| Aspect | Grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Proportions** (storeys, heights, bays, openings) | **C** | Storey heights hold in both files (A). Three things pull it down. Bays at 2.4-3.6 m are too narrow: both files put street bays at 3.2-4.5 m and colonial bays wider still. Openings run to within 0.5 m of the ceiling, about 4.1-4.9 m on a colonial upper floor, so h:w is about 3.3. The observer measures 3.0-3.5 m tall and h:w 2.2-2.6 (H). Together these give colonial fronts about 50% void where the observer reads 25-35%. Also, colonial is fixed at 2 floors (the one-storey house is missing) and eclectic is capped at 3 uppers. |
| **Openings** (glazing, leaves, heads, mediopunto incidence) | **D** | The `sash` 2×3 glazing is the single biggest giveaway (adversary H9, observer "persianas TYPICAL on upper openings of every period"). Neither file found a sash window. Mediopuntos go to 85% of colonial houses as round-headed glass arches over narrow openings, but 18th-century street fronts are flat-headed. Heads are round or flat only, with no segmental heads or hoods for the 19th century. |
| **Balconies / ironwork** | **C** | Both kinds exist (per-window and continuous), which is right. Against that: every colonial front gets a balcony, and the choice is per house rather than per floor. No corbels or moulded slab edge are drawn. The bar-and-rosette railing is unattested; scroll panels and stone balusters are missing. The colonial wooden gallery has no posts and no roof, and the `Tejaroz` rule can never fire. There are no guardavecinos. |
| **Ground floor** (portal, zaguán, rejas, bodega) | **D** | The zaguán holds (B). The portal is drawn as black-filled arches on 0.5 m piers (strip panel 3); both files describe Tuscan columns with a lit gallery behind. Bodegas are plate glass across the whole floor (panels 5-6), not posts with folding doors or roll shutters. Rejas run nearly floor to ceiling, appear only on colonial fronts, and have no projecting sill or cap. Three of six strip panels have a wrong ground floor. |
| **Top** (alero, cornice, pretil, crest) | **C** | The neo/eclectic cornice and balustered pretil are plausible (B). The colonial roof is a 0.55 m dark slab; both files describe a red tile eave, and the adversary a visible tile slope (D). The pretil has one form where four recur. The crest is a plain beige segmental slab where real ones are scrolled cartouches flanked by urns. |
| **Ornament** (grown mediopunto, reja, persiana, balusters) | **C** | The mediopunto's radial rays round a hub, and the ring, are right in shape. But the panes are filled dark, where real ones are coloured glass or white-painted wooden slats. Persianas cover only the lower part of the opening and appear on only 35-60% of houses. The reja has the right bars and the wrong form. The railing rosette is unattested. The balustrade is fine. |
| **Colour** | **C** | The palette and its leaders (yellow, blue, mint) hold (B+). Every front is one colour with white frames: no contrasting trim, no zócalo, and never bare coral limestone, which both files call the finish of the plaza palaces. Louvre colours underweight brown and omit white. |
| **Variety** | **C** | The date ramp gives real variety between manners, and within a manner the builder draws plenty. But each house makes one balcony choice for every floor, uses one pretil, and one portal form, and has no one-storey colonial houses or tall eclectic fronts. Panels 5 and 6 read as the same building at two widths. |

Colonial 0.15-0.25. | **Both are right about different streets.** The observer's colonial examples are plaza palaces; the adversary's are side streets. Tie it to `plaza`: colonial street 0.2, colonial plaza 0.6. The colonial form is the **shallow fan inside a flat head**, 0.4-0.7 m tall, in wooden slats, not a round arch of glass. |

## 3. Ranked fixes

These are ordered by how much each one changes what the eye reads. Every number is a photo estimate from the evidence above.

**1. Openings are wooden leaves, not sash** (openings D → B).
- `an_opening_to_the_floor`: drop `'sash'`. Every ground and upper opening is a pair of full-height leaves:
  - louvred 0.5 / panelled 0.25 / louvred over panelled 0.25 (adversary H9);
  - 4-leaf folding on 0.2 of colonial ground doors (observer #21).
- The nearest existing hook is `glazing = 'scuri'`. A real leaf glazing is probably a new `glazing` kind.
- Glass appears only in the mediopunto or luceta above.
- `persianas`: colonial 0.8 / neo 0.7 / eclectic 0.5, and **full height** (y + 0.05 up to the head), not "the lower part".

**2. Openings shorter, and a wall band above them** (proportions C → B).
- Upper opening height: **3.0-3.6 m** with h:w 2.2-2.6, instead of `h - 0.5`.
- Ground doors and windows: 2.8-3.6 m.
- This leaves 1.0-1.8 m of wall over every opening, which is where the head, hood or guardapolvo sits.
- `open_w`: [1.3, 1.6] on streets.
- Add a wide-arch plaza variant, chance 0.4 when `portal`: the upper opening is 0.7-0.8 of the bay, with 4-6 leaves under a mediopunto that spans the whole arch (adversary H8, Plaza Vieja p003/p008).

**3. Wider bays** (proportions).
- `bay` by manner: colonial [3.8, 4.6, 5.5], with the zaguán bay 0.5-1.0 m wider than the others; neoclassical and eclectic [3.4, 3.9, 4.5].
- When `portal`, bay [4.2, 4.4, 4.8], so that clear span is about 3.5 m (observer, H).
- Target void share: colonial 25-35%, 19th century 40-50%.
- `end`: [0.6, 1.0, 1.5]. Add a corner pilaster or quoin at the end piers on 0.6 of neo/eclectic fronts (adversary H25).

**4. The portal** (ground floor D → B).
- Supports: round Tuscan **column** 0.6 / pier with engaged column 0.4. Column diameter 0.5-0.6 m.
- Head: round arch 0.75 / flat beam 0.25.
- Impost at about 4.0 m, or 0.65-0.7 of the storey; crown 5.5-6.0 m.
- Behind the arches, **draw the back wall** in the house's own colour (a shade darker for shadow), with its own doors and rejas. Never draw a black void.
- Ground storey +0.5 m when `portal`.
- Allow portales on eclectic plaza fronts at 0.3 (adversary p008).
- One upper opening is centred over each arch, and the balcony sits on the arcade's top course (observer).

**5. The colonial roof and gallery** (top C → B, balconies).
- Tile roof 0.65 / azotea 0.35.
  - The tile roof is a red slope 1.0-1.5 m tall in elevation (a hip at about 22-25°) over a **red** tile alero of 0.3-0.5 m.
  - The azotea has a low plain pretil with tile coping.
- `wood_gallery`: 0.3, only when there is an upper floor.
- The gallery has turned posts (*pies derechos*) at **1.5-2.0 m** centres, rising to carry the eave or a tile cover. The deck shows joist ends and a fascia.
- Paint the gallery in `louvre_paint`, not dark brown.
- Rewrite the `Tejaroz` condition. `index < count - 1` can never hold on the top floor. Draw the cover whenever there is a gallery, joined to the alero when the gallery is on the top floor.

**6. Colour: trim, zócalo, stone** (colour C → B).
- Exposed coral-limestone ashlar (grey-buff, unpainted): colonial plaza 0.6, colonial street 0.15, neoclassical 0.1, eclectic 0.05. Cairo's per-storey `wall_material` is a pattern to follow.
- Otherwise, a pastel plus a **trim colour** on frames, bands, pilasters, cornice and balcony slabs: white 0.5 / a darker tone of the wall 0.5.
- **Zócalo**: a darker painted base band 0.6-1.0 m tall on 0.5 of plastered fronts.
- `louvre_paints`: brown 3, green 3, blue 3, teal 1, white 1.

**7. Balconies by floor, on corbels** (balconies C → B).
- Choose per floor, not per house: continuous 0.4 / per opening 0.6.
- Common 19th-century pattern: a continuous balcony on one floor and per-window on the others (both files). Flip the top floor with chance 0.3.
- Colonial: a balcony on 0.7 of fronts with an upper floor, else none (adversary H13). Without a gallery, it is per window: iron or wood, on a **bowl-shaped stone corbel** 0.25-0.4 m thick.
- Neo and eclectic: a flat moulded slab edge 0.25-0.4 m thick on visible corbels. Continuous balconies project 0.6-1.0 m, per-window ones 0.4-0.6 m and 1.2-1.5× the opening width.
- **Guardavecinos**: a semicircular fan of radiating iron spikes at the ends of a continuous balcony, or between adjoining balconies. Neo 0.2, eclectic 0.35 (observer #18/20). It is distinctively Havana and cheap to grow with the mediopunto's ray code.

**8. Railings** (ornament).
- Drop the rosette.
- Colonial: turned wood.
- Neoclassical: straight bars 0.5 / cast-iron scroll panels 0.5.
- Eclectic: scroll 0.4 / stone or cement balusters 0.4 / bars 0.2.

**9. Mediopunto incidence and form** (openings, ornament).
- `mediopunto`: colonial 0.2 on streets and 0.6 on a plaza; neoclassical 0.5; eclectic 0.3.
- The colonial form is a shallow fan **inside a flat-headed frame**, 0.4-0.7 m tall, in wooden slats painted white or `louvre_paint`.
- Neoclassical and eclectic use the round-arch form with **coloured panes** (red, blue, amber, green), and white tracery against them.
- Patterns in every manner: ring 0.6 / fan 0.25 / grid 0.15. Remove the colonial-only ring override.
- Add a **rectangular luceta** (coloured glass or an iron grille transom) over the doors of 0.35 of neoclassical houses without a mediopunto, and of 0.5 of the eclectic ones. Grilled transoms over doors at about 3.5-4 m are TYPICAL in the 19th century (observer).

**10. Heads and surrounds** (openings).
- Colonial: flat head, plain stone frame 0.3-0.4 m wide, with a small cap moulding (guardapolvo) over upper openings on 0.5.
- Neoclassical: segmental head with a hood mould 0.5 / flat head with a cornice hood 0.5.
- Eclectic: segmental or round head with a keystone cartouche 0.5 / pediment 0.25 / moulded architrave 0.25.
- `keystone_present` only on that eclectic share; keystones are uncommon otherwise (adversary H27).

**11. Rejas** (ground floor).
- On every non-door ground window.
- Colonial: turned wood. Neoclassical and eclectic: iron (scrolled cast or wrought) on 0.6 of non-shop ground windows.
- Sill 0.3-0.9 m; top at the lintel.
- Projecting reja (voladiza: 0.2-0.4 m out on a moulded sill, with a cap) on 0.5 of colonial fronts and 0.25 of later ones.

**12. Storeys** (proportions, variety).
- Colonial `uppers`: 0-1. One storey on 0.35 of street fronts and 0.05 on a plaza. The entresuelo only when there is 1 upper.
- Eclectic `uppers`: 2-4, weighted 2:3, 3:4, 4:2. Allow 5 when `width ≥ 16`.

**13. Bodega** (ground floor).
- Replace the plate glass with wooden posts between openings, filled with folding panelled doors 0.6 / roll-down shutters 0.4.
- A painted fascia sign over them on 0.5.
- Put the shop at the corner bay when the site is a corner; keep incidence as is.

**14. Pretil and crest** (top).
- Pretil forms: plain solid with moulded coping 0.4 / balustrade 0.25 / dies with urns or pinnacles 0.2 / dies with iron railing between 0.15.
- Crest: a scrolled cartouche (initials or date panel) flanked by urns on 0.6 of crests, in place of the plain segmental slab.

**15. Later, if corners come in.** Both files show corner buildings: square with a giant pilaster (colonial), rounded (19th century), and canted with a corner shop door (c.1900). In each case the continuous balcony wraps the corner. This is site-level work, and out of scope for this file.
