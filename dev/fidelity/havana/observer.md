# Old Havana street fronts: independent observer, round 1

Derived from photographs only (Wikimedia Commons), without reading the project's
rules. 38 photographs were read; 22 distinct buildings or street walls are
recorded below. Heritage texts were thin (Wikipedia en/es "Old Havana" / "La
Habana Vieja" give history and plaza functions, not dimensions), so every
number here is a photo estimate.

## How dimensions were estimated

- Scale references: adult 1.65-1.75 m, child ~1.35 m, teenager ~1.55 m,
  balcony railing ~1.0 m, one known storey to scale the next.
- Confidence: **H** = a person stands at the façade plane in a near-frontal
  view; **M** = person a few metres off the plane, or scale carried from a
  railing; **L** = perspective/fisheye/postcard, proportion only.
- Ratios (h:w of openings, arch rise, bay rhythm) are more reliable than
  absolute metres; trust ratios over metres where they disagree.

## Rules, with frequency

### Massing and storeys

- **TYPICAL (colonial, 17th-18th c.):** 2 storeys. Ground floor ~5.0-5.5 m and
  upper floor ~4.5-6.0 m to the eave (H: #38, #31, #16/17/19). Total eave
  height 9.5-12 m.
- **TYPICAL (19th c. tenement):** 3-4 storeys. Ground ~5.0-5.5 m, upper floors
  ~4.5-4.7 m, top floor a little shorter (H: #19; M: #9, #11).
- **VARIES:** a mezzanine (entresuelo) inside a tall ground storey, shown
  by a second row of small openings or transoms over the doors. I saw
  grilled transoms over doors at about 3.5-4 m (#16/17, #9); there was a mezzanine
  behind the portal at #30. Ground storeys with a mezzanine reach 6.5-9 m.
- **RARE:** 5+ storeys before 1930, found only on large commercial or
  institutional blocks (#25 Lonja, #30).
- Floors-to-height correlate with date. Width does not track storeys:
  narrow 19th c. lots go up instead.

### Bays and wall

- **TYPICAL:** wall dominates. Openings take ~25-35 % of the colonial front's
  width and ~40-50 % on 19th c. fronts.
- Colonial bay spacing is **wide and irregular**: 5.5-8 m centre to centre at
  #38, with the zaguán bay often off-centre. In the 19th c. it is regular,
  ~3.5-4.5 m.
- **TYPICAL:** upper openings align vertically with the ground openings.
- **VARIES:** flat pilaster strips or recessed panels between bays (#4, #9,
  #19). They are mostly 19th c.; colonial fronts are flat ashlar or plaster.

### Openings

- **TYPICAL:** every upper opening is a **door to the floor** (puerta-balcón),
  never a sill window. Size 1.3-1.6 m wide by 3.0-3.5 m tall, so h:w ≈ 2.2-2.6
  (H: #19, #31).
- **TYPICAL:** ground openings are also near floor length: doors 1.6-2.0 ×
  2.7-3.6 m. Ground windows have rejas from ~0.3-0.5 m above the pavement up to
  the lintel, which makes them read as doors.
- **Zaguán / carriage door (one per house, TYPICAL in wealthy houses):** 2.7-3.5 m
  wide by 4.4-5.0 m tall. It has a flat stone frame (#16/17, #38) or a round
  arch (#28). Leaves are heavy plank or panelled timber, often with a wicket.
- Heads: colonial heads are **flat** (lintel) with a stone frame ~0.3-0.4 m wide.
  The 19th c. uses **segmental** heads with a hood mould (#9, #19). Eclectic
  fronts use round or segmental heads with keystone cartouches (#18).
- **VARIES:** 4-leaf folding panelled doors (#21), 2-leaf doors with a louvred
  upper half (#16, #19), all-louvre persianas (#27, #36).
- **Persianas (louvred leaves): TYPICAL** on upper openings of every period,
  painted white, blue, green or brown. They are often combined with an inner
  glazed or solid leaf.

### Mediopuntos (fan lunettes)

- **TYPICAL on plaza palaces, VARIES on street houses, rare on plain tenements.**
- Two forms:
  1. **Full semicircular lunette** over a round-arched opening, radius
     ≈ half the opening width (1.0-1.4 m); #8 and #36 (Arcos). The pattern is
     wooden radial **sun-ray fan** slats with a small centre semicircle. These
     are white or painted.
  2. **Shallow fan inside a flat-topped frame**: a segmental or semicircular
     glazed fan ~0.4-0.7 m tall in the top of a rectangular opening (#27, #31,
     #38). These are blue-and-white petal/fan patterns.
- Coloured-glass mediopuntos (blue, red, yellow) are characteristic of the 19th c.
  refits on Plaza de Armas (#24). In the 18th c. originals they are mostly wood
  slats.

### Balconies

- **Per-window iron balcony: TYPICAL in all periods.** It projects 0.4-0.6 m and
  is 1.2-1.5× the opening width. It sits on a **moulded stone or stucco
  corbel slab** 0.25-0.4 m thick, bowl-shaped in the 18th c. (#21, #38) and a
  flat moulded slab later (#9). The railing is ~1.0 m of wrought iron scrolls.
- **Continuous balcony: TYPICAL on plaza fronts and on the main floor of
  19th c. tenements.** It projects 0.6-1.0 m and often wraps the corner.
  It is carried on a moulded slab (#16/17/19), on iron diagonal struts (#27/36,
  #1) or on stone corbels.
- Common 19th c. pattern: a continuous balcony on one floor and per-window
  balconies on the others (#9, #11, #30).
- **Wooden balcony gallery with turned posts and a tiled roof: RARE, and early
  (17th-early 18th c.)** (#7). Plain wooden-bar guards in the opening are found
  on modest houses (#10).
- **Guardavecinos** (radial iron spike fans between neighbouring balconies):
  VARIES, 19th-early 20th c. tenements (#18, #20). Distinctive to Havana.
- Concrete balconies with pierced or balustered parapets appear after ~1900
  (#6, #37).

### Rejas (window grilles)

- **TYPICAL on every ground window.** Colonial rejas are plain vertical bars,
  iron or turned wood. 19th-20th c. ones are scrolled cast or wrought iron
  (#16/17).
- **Rejas voladas** (grille cages projecting ~0.3-0.5 m on a corbel base):
  VARIES, found on street houses (#35, #3).
- Grilled transoms (fixed iron fanlight panel) over doors: TYPICAL in the 19th c.

### Portales (arcades)

- **TYPICAL on plaza fronts, absent on ordinary streets.** Plaza Vieja,
  Plaza de Armas, Plaza de la Catedral and Prado all have them.
- Round arches on Tuscan columns. Columns are ~4 m to the impost, and the arch
  crown is ~5.5-6 m (H: #31). Bay ~4.4 m centre to centre, clear span ~3.5 m;
  rise = half the span. In the 19th c. and on Prado the arches sit on pilasters
  with imposts, bay ~3.9 m, and the portal storey can be ~7.5 m.
- One upper opening is centred over each arch. The balcony sits directly
  on the arcade's top course.
- Inside the portal: a full front of doors and barred windows, sometimes with a
  mezzanine row.

### Roof line

- **Colonial: TYPICAL clay-tile roof with a thin tile eave** (alero of 1-2
  courses of projecting tile and brick, ~0.3-0.5 m), with no parapet (#7, #8,
  #36, #38). A low pretil capped with tile also occurs (#8).
- **19th c.: TYPICAL flat roof (azotea) behind a moulded cornice plus a parapet**,
  either solid or a balustrade. Pedestals at the bay lines carry urns or
  pinnacles on the richer houses (#24, #28, #30).
- **Eclectic:** heavy projecting cornice, sometimes bulging or crested (#37).
- Small cap mouldings (guardapolvos) over upper windows: TYPICAL on colonial
  ashlar palaces (#31, #38).

### Corners

- **VARIES:** square corners with a giant pilaster (colonial, #1); **rounded**
  corners (19th c., #16/17/19); **canted chaflán** with a corner shop door
  (c.1900, #33). Balconies follow the corner shape.

### Materials and colour

- Colonial palaces on the plazas are **exposed coral-limestone ashlar**, grey-buff
  and pitted (#1, #8, #31, #38).
- Everything else is **lime plaster painted in strong pastels**: ochre and yellow,
  aqua and turquoise, pink, sky blue, cream. Trim (frames, balcony slabs,
  pilasters) is in a contrasting white or a darker hue. Woodwork is blue, green,
  turquoise or brown. Plaster scored to imitate ashlar occurs (#21).
- A dark painted base band (rodapié) ~0.5-0.8 m tall is TYPICAL on plastered
  street houses (#3, #33).

### Ground floors and shops

- **Colonial: domestic** zaguán plus barred windows; shops appear only in
  converted bays.
- **c.1880-1930 commercial streets (Obispo, O'Reilly, Muralla):** shops
  across the whole ground floor, with wide openings, roll-down shutters and awnings
  (#5, #6, #12, #33). Shopfront signs are painted on the wall or on a fascia
  board.

## Correlations

- **Date → storeys, roof, balcony.** 18th c.: 2 storeys, tile roof, flat-headed
  openings, small balconies on stone corbels. 19th c.: 3-4 storeys, azotea plus
  parapet, segmental heads, continuous balconies on moulded slabs, grilled
  transoms. 1900-1930: ornament and storeys increase, with guardavecinos,
  concrete balconies, shopfronts and rounded or canted corners.
- **Wealth/location → exposed stone, portal, mediopuntos.** Plaza palaces are
  ashlar with an arcade and fan lunettes. Ordinary streets are plaster with no
  arcade and plainer openings.
- **Width → bay count, not opening size.** Opening sizes stay at about
  1.3-1.6 × 3-3.5 m whatever the lot. Narrow lots have 2-3 bays, palaces 5-11.

## Gaps and uncertainty

- No photograph gave a clean frontal elevation of a narrow 19th c. tenement
  with a person at the façade, so tenement storey heights rest on one building
  (#16/17/19) plus proportion.
- I could not find an entresuelo shown clearly in elevation. Its height (if
  present, ~2.2-2.8 m) is inferred.
- The identities of #28 and #30 are unconfirmed. Their dates are style guesses.
