# SoHo round 3: sceptical blind judgement

Judge: independent architectural-historian pass, blind. I looked only at `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and at reference photographs from Wikimedia Commons. Every drawing was treated as fake until it convinced me. Flat colour rendering was ignored; massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and characteristic elements were judged.

## Per-drawing verdicts

R = recognisability (1-5), P = plausibility (1-5).

### img00
- **Guess:** New York, SoHo, cast-iron store-and-loft, 6 bays, 5 storeys, c. 1868-75.
- **R 4, P 3. Expert at a glance: WITH FLAWS.**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The windows are flat-headed rectangles with a thin segmental "lid" stuck on top. In B and D the opening itself is segmental and the sash follows the curve; here the arch is an applique.
  - The central segmental pediment sits on the cornice like a hat, about 2 bays wide, with no answering projection in the floors below. In C the pediment crowns a projecting pavilion that runs the full height.
  - Cornice band (slab plus brackets) is about 0.15 of a storey; in A, D and F it is roughly 0.35-0.45.
  - All four upper storeys are identical in height; C and A both diminish.
  - Left entrance: a short door with a blank panel filling about 40% of the bay height above its transom. Real SoHo doors are tall with deep glazed transoms (B).
  - Pilasters have block caps, not Corinthian capitals; there is no floor-by-floor projecting entablature.

### img01
- **Guess:** New York, SoHo, cast-iron loft, 6 bays, 5 storeys, c. 1870, rooftop water tank.
- **R 4, P 3. WITH FLAWS.**
- **Giveaways:** same applique segmental lids on flat-headed sash; thin cornice; uniform storeys; plain pilasters without capitals. Balustraded sill panels are right (A). The storefront has no left entrance at all, so the 6-bay front has one narrow door at the right edge; a front this wide almost always has two (B, C). No fire escape on a 6-bay, 5-storey front, which is unusual (A and C both carry one).

### img02
- **Guess:** New York, red-brick Italianate, 3 bays, 6 storeys, cast-iron shopfront, c. 1855-70. Reads as much Lower East Side tenement as SoHo loft.
- **R 3, P 3. WITH FLAWS.**
- **Giveaways:**
  - The top-floor lintels swell into tall blank blocks running up to the cornice. I found no SoHo precedent; E shows uniform lintels and a separate frieze.
  - Cornice is a thin slab with small teardrop brackets; no frieze, no modillions.
  - Fire-escape stairs are drawn as a single diagonal line with no second stringer or handrail (compare E, where each run is a clear band with rails).
  - Faint vertical lines at every pier read as panel joints, not brickwork.

### img03

Every drawing reads at once as New York, and none would fool a specialist. None is a NO: a lay viewer would accept all sixteen. The cast-iron fronts are recognisably SoHo; the brick fronts are recognisably New York but drift towards tenement.

## Top giveaways across the set, ranked, with fixes

1. **Cornice far too shallow (all 16).** Measured about 0.09 storey for the slab and about 0.2 storey including brackets. Fix: cornice zone (frieze plus brackets plus crown) at 0.35-0.45 of the top storey's height; frieze 0.15 storey; scrolled console brackets 0.2-0.25 storey tall, spaced at every pier plus one or two between; crown projection at least 0.6 times its own height; add a modillion or dentil row under the crown.

2. **Arches are lids, not openings (00, 01, 04, 07, 12, 14).** Fix: make the sash follow the head. Segmental heads with a rise of 0.12-0.18 of the opening width for the common SoHo type (B, D); full semicircles only for the palazzo type (A), and then with an arched upper sash, a keystone and impost blocks. Odds: segmental 70%, round 20%, flat 10% for cast iron.

3. **No classical order on cast-iron piers (all cast iron).** Fix: give every pier an engaged column or pilaster with a Corinthian capital (capital height about 1.1 times shaft diameter) and a projecting entablature at each floor (0.08-0.1 storey). For the round-arch type, add colonnettes framing each arch (A). Add rusticated or quoined end piers on about 40% of fronts (C).

4. **Blank panel above short doors (00, 03, 05, 07, 09, 10, 13, 14).** Fix: run the door plus transom to the full storefront height; transom 0.25-0.35 of the opening, glazed. Never leave solid infill above a door in a cast-iron storefront.

5. **Equal storey heights (all 16).** Fix: second floor 1.0, then 0.96, 0.92, 0.88, top 0.85 (or a 4% step); ground floor 1.3-1.45 times the second floor. Keep the cornice tied to the top storey after the change.

6. **Fire-escape stairs drawn as one line (02, 05, 06, 07, 09, 11, 12, 13, 15).** Fix: draw each run as a band about 0.08 storey thick with two stringers and a handrail, treads hinted. Odds of a front fire escape: 85% on 3-bay fronts, 65% on wider cast-iron fronts (currently 0 of 5 wide cast-iron fronts have one).

7. **Tall blank top-floor lintel blocks on brick fronts (02, 05, 08, 09, 10, 11, 13).** Fix: top-floor lintels the same height as the others, about 0.12 of the window height, with a separate frieze band beneath the cornice. Vary the brick-front lintel type per building: cornice-head 50%, pedimented 25%, segmental 25% (E).

8. **No raised ground floor or loading platform (all 16).** Fix: raise the shop floor 0.6-1.0 m above the sidewalk with a full-width iron platform or 2-4 steps (B), plus a vault-light strip, on about 60% of fronts.

9. **Repetition across the set (brick fronts 05, 08, 09, 11, 13 and storefronts generally).** Same paired fluted end pilasters, same teardrop brackets, same lintel profile. Fix: vary bracket form (scroll, paired, foliate), ground-floor column type and end-pier treatment per building; give fronts of 7 or more bays a central emphasis (wider centre bay, pediment, or projecting pavilion as in C) about half the time.

10. **Wide fronts with a single entrance and tenement window ratios (01, 03, 04).** Fix: two entrances on fronts of 6 or more bays (one to the store, one to the upper lofts); upper-floor glazing 55-70% of bay width on lofts (03 is near 40%).

11. **Inconsistent bay (12).** Fix: spandrel treatment must be identical across bays unless it marks an axis.
