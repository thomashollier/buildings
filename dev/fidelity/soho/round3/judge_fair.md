## Overall verdict

Every drawing reads at once as **Lower Manhattan, SoHo / Cast Iron Historic District, c. 1855-1885**. The set has two families: cast-iron lofts (00, 01, 04, 06, 07, 12, 14, 15) and brick tenements or store-and-loft buildings over a cast-iron shopfront (02, 03, 05, 08, 09, 10, 11, 13). Recognisability is high. Plausibility is held back by a short list of repeated tells (ranked below), most of which sit at the cornice, the column capitals and the ground floor. None of the 16 passes an expert as a real building without flaws; the three narrow cast-iron fronts (06, 12, 15) come closest.

## Per drawing

**img00**: SoHo/Broadway cast-iron loft, Italianate, c. 1865-70. 6 bays, 4 loft storeys over a shopfront, segmental pediment with nameplate on the cornice. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The pilasters have no capitals, only a small trapezoid block. Every real front (A, B, C, F) has a full Corinthian or Composite capital, which is the strongest sign of the type.
- The segmental window heads are a blue cap sitting on a rectangular sash, with no archivolt moulding and no keystone. Compare the moulded arches on C and the cast-iron front in E.
- All four upper storeys are the same height. In C the storeys clearly diminish upward.
- At the ground floor a door sits low in the left bay with a blank cream panel above it, beside full-height shop glazing (see Giveaway 1).
- The segmental pediment is a good touch and a plausible centre emphasis.

**img01**: Same type, c. 1860-70, with balustraded sill panels in the manner of the Haughwout. Water tower. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- Same capital-less pilasters and frameless segmental caps as 00. The balustrades sit inside the bay as flat panels; on the Haughwout (A) the balustrade runs as a continuous projecting balcony band over the entablature, broken only by column pedestals.
- The water tower legs stand on the cornice line in front of the parapet. In a true elevation the tank sits set back, its legs hidden behind the cornice.
- The ground floor (5 shop bays plus a framed door) is the most believable in the set.

**img02**: Lower Manhattan / South Village old-law tenement, c. 1880-95. 3 bays, 5 storeys of red brick over a cast-iron shopfront, full-width fire escape. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The top-floor lintels are tall plain blocks that run up into the cornice, and there is no frieze. Real tenements (E, left building, dated 1880 in its frieze) have a continuous panelled frieze 0.6-0.9 m deep under the cornice, with brick between the top lintels and that frieze.
- The cornice brackets are not aligned with the bays (paired at the ends, then irregular).
- Faint hairlines on the brick at the bay lines read like panel joints and suggest precast panels rather than brick.
- The fire-escape stairs slope at about 38°, across nearly the full balcony. Real NYC escape stairs are steep (about 55-65°) and run about one bay. Compare E and F.
- Clustered paired pilasters at both ends of the shopfront appear here and on every brick drawing. That is plausible once, but it has become a stamp.

**img03**: Buff-brick store-and-loft building, c. 1870-80. 11 bays, 5 storeys, fire escape over 3 bays, water tower. **R 3 · P 2 · NO.**
- These are tenement-sized punched windows (void about 50% of bay width) stretched across 11 bays. Real brick lofts of this width (E, left building) have larger windows with moulded or pedimented lintels and a void of about 60-65%, often with segmental arches. As drawn, it reads as a tenement copied sideways.
- 11 identical bays with no centre or end emphasis, and no frieze under the cornice.
- The floor lines are drawn as hairlines across the brick, with no string course or sill course.

**img04**: Venetian-palazzo cast iron, c. 1857-70, closest in vocabulary to the Haughwout (A). 9 bays, 5 loft storeys, round-arched windows with archivolts and imposts. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- A 9-bay front with no centre pavilion, end pavilions or pediment. The wide real fronts (B: projecting 3-bay portico with pediment; C: three mansard pavilions) always mark a hierarchy.
- The arched windows are framed by small flat strips. On A each arch springs from engaged colonnettes with capitals, and the larger columns between bays carry their own capitals.
- The ground floor has only one door in 9 bays, at the extreme left. A front this wide normally has a central or second loft entrance.

**img05**: 7-bay red-brick tenement or store-and-loft building, c. 1875-90, fire escape over the central 3 bays, water tower. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- Same frieze-less cornice with tall top lintels, hairlines on the brick and shallow fire-escape stairs as 02.
- The left door has a blank panel above it.
- All the lintels in the set are the same simple shelf. Real examples vary (E: eared, pedimented; G: plain stone; Neo-Grec incised on many Broome Street tenements).

**img06**: Narrow Neo-Grec cast-iron loft, c. 1875-85 (a Greene/Mercer Street type). 3 bays, 5 loft storeys, flat-headed wide windows, fire escape over 2 bays. **R 4 · P 4 · WITH FLAWS.**
- This is the strongest drawing. The proportions (large void, thin piers, diamond spandrel panels) match the later Greene Street fronts and D.
- The pilasters still have no capitals, and the inter-storey entablatures are flat bands with no cornice moulding or projection line.
- The fire-escape gooseneck ladder stops below the cornice. It should rise over the parapet.

**img07**: 3-bay cast iron with segmental heads, c. 1870, full-width fire escape. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The ground floor has two doors in three bays (one low, under a blank panel; one in a black recess) and only one shop bay. On a 25 ft front that is one door too many.
- The thin dark outline down the ground-floor edges reads as a drawing artefact.
- It has the same frameless segmental caps as 00.

**img08**: 7-bay brick tenement or loft, c. 1870-85, no fire escape, no tower. **R 3 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- A 6-storey, 7-bay walk-up on a SoHo street front with no fire escape is unusual. The frieze-less cornice and hairline grid are the same as in 02.
- The ground floor (door plus 6 shop bays) is reasonable.

**img09**: 7-bay brick, c. 1875-90, fire escape over 2 bays, doors at both ends. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The right door sits under a blank panel. The other issues are the same as 05.

**img10**: 3-bay brick tenement, c. 1870-85, no fire escape. **R 3 · P 2 · NO.**
- The ground floor has two doors of different heights (one full height, one low under a blank panel) flanking a single shop bay. No real 25 ft storefront looks like this.
- A 5-storey, 3-bay tenement with no front fire escape is possible but uncommon.
- The cornice and frieze have the same problem as 02.

**img11**: 7-bay brick, c. 1875-90, 1-over-1 sash, fire escape over the left 3 bays. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The 1/1 sash is a believable later replacement. The cornice, frieze and fire-escape angle have the same faults as 02.

**img12**: Narrow cast iron with segmental heads, c. 1870-80, 3 bays, 1/1 sash, fire escape over 2 bays. **R 4 · P 4 · WITH FLAWS.**
- The massing and void ratio are good. The segmental caps have no archivolt, the pilasters have no capitals, and the storeys are all the same height.
- The ground floor (2 shop bays plus a framed door) is believable.

**img13**: 11-bay red-brick tenement or loft, c. 1875-90, fire escape over the left 3 bays, doors at both ends. **R 3 · P 2 · NO.**
- If residential, an 11-bay tenement front with a single fire escape over 3 bays cannot serve the apartments in the other 8 bays. Real wide tenements carry 2-3 escapes or are several buildings of 3-5 bays with party-wall breaks and separate cornices.
- If a loft, the windows are too small (the same issue as 03).
- The left door has a blank panel above it, and there is no hierarchy across 11 bays.

**img14**: Venetian arched cast iron with balustrade panels, c. 1857-70, 6 bays. **R 4 · P 3 · WITH FLAWS.**
- This is a good Haughwout echo. The issues are missing capitals, constant storey heights, the left door under a blank panel, and the balustrade drawn as an in-bay panel rather than a continuous balcony band.

**img15**: Narrow Neo-Grec cast iron, c. 1875-85, 3 bays, full-width fire escape. **R 4 · P 4 · WITH FLAWS.**
- Along with 06, the most convincing in the set. The fire escape behaves well over the large windows, and the ground floor (2 shop bays plus a framed door) is right.
- The issues are capital-less pilasters, flat entablatures, shallow stairs and the gooseneck stopping short.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked, with fixes

1. **The low door under a blank panel** (00, 03, 05, 07, 09, 10, 13, 14). This is the single most repeated tell. In every real storefront (D, E, F) the door and its transom fill the full opening up to the storefront lintel, level with the shop-glass heads.
   *Fix:* door leaf height = 0.72-0.78 of the storefront clear height, with a glazed transom filling the rest. Never use an infill panel. Allow at most one door per 25 ft of frontage, plus one loft entrance per front.

2. **No capitals on the cast-iron pilasters** (all 8 cast-iron drawings and every shopfront). Corinthian capitals are the defining mark of A, B, C and F.
   *Fix:* capital height = 1.0-1.2 × shaft width, with an abacus 1.25-1.35 × shaft width and a plinth/base 0.5 × shaft width. At loft storeys use engaged three-quarter columns rather than flat strips on about 60% of fronts.

3. **A cornice with no frieze, and top-floor lintels running into it** (02, 03, 05, 08, 09, 10, 11, 13).
   *Fix:* add a continuous frieze 0.6-0.9 m deep (about 5-7% of facade height) under the cornice slab, with recessed panels between brackets placed exactly on the bay lines (paired at the ends). Keep at least 0.4 m of brick between the top-floor lintel and the frieze. Total cornice plus frieze should be 8-10% of facade height (it is about 5% now).

4. **Fire-escape geometry** (02, 03, 05, 06, 07, 09, 11, 12, 13, 15). The stairs are about 38-42° and span 2-3 bays. Real NYC stairs are steep and short (E, F).
   *Fix:* stair angle 55-65°, horizontal run about 0.55-0.65 × floor height (roughly 1 bay), alternating direction each floor and contained inside the balcony length. Run the gooseneck ladder up and over the parapet, about 1 m above the cornice top.

5. **No hierarchy on wide fronts** (03, 04, 13, and 08/09/11 less so). Real 7+ bay fronts mark a centre or ends (B: projecting 3-bay portico with pediment; C: three mansard pavilions; E: pedimented centre on the cast-iron neighbour).
   *Fix:* for 7+ bays, about 65% odds of a 3-bay centre pavilion (paired columns, pediment or raised cornice section) or 2 end pavilions (quoins or rusticated piers, as on the right of B). About 15% odds of a mansard on cast iron. For 9+ bay brick tenements, split the front into 2-3 party-wall units with separate cornices and fire escapes instead.

6. **Uniform storey heights** (all 16).
   *Fix:* ground floor 1.35-1.5 × the typical upper floor; second floor 1.1 ×; each floor above shrinking 3-6%; top floor about 0.85-0.9 × the second.

7. **Segmental and arched heads without surrounds** (00, 01, 07, 12; the arched 04 and 14 are better).
   *Fix:* draw an archivolt moulding 6-8% of window width, with a keystone on segmental heads, springing from the capitals or imposts. Make the upper sash follow the arch, framed in the sash colour, rather than a frameless cap above a rectangle.

8. **Hairline grids on the brick** (all 8 brick drawings). They read as precast panel joints.
   *Fix:* remove them. If floor lines are wanted, use a real sill course (a stone band about 0.15 m) on about 40% of buildings.

9. **Brick lofts with tenement windows** (03, 13, partly 08).
   *Fix:* in store-and-loft brick (6+ bays), use a window-to-bay void of 0.6-0.65, with segmental-arched or pedimented and eared lintels (E). Keep the 0.5 void and shelf lintels for 3-5 bay tenements only.

10. **One vocabulary across the whole set.** The same lintel, the same panelled double door and the same paired end pilasters appear on every brick front, and the same flat pilaster on every iron front. Shown side by side, the set reads as one kit.
    *Fix:* use at least 3 lintel families (plain stone shelf, bracketed Italianate cap, eared/pedimented Neo-Grec), chosen per building. Make about 50% of doors glazed. Use single end piers on about 60% of shopfronts.

11. **Missing street-level SoHo signs.** None of the drawings has a raised loading platform with vault lights and steps, and the storefront bulkheads are very thin.
    *Fix:* on about 50% of cast-iron lofts, add a raised platform 0.5-0.9 m high across the front with 2-4 steps at one end. Make the bulkhead 0.5-0.7 m.

12. **Water tanks in front of the parapet** (01, 03, 05).
    *Fix:* set the tank back so the legs are hidden behind the cornice and only the tank and roof show. Tanks should appear on about 50% of buildings, not 3 of 16.
