# SoHo / Tribeca cast-iron loft fronts, 1849–1880s — observer notes, round 1

Written blind: nothing under `src/`, `tests/` or `docs/` was opened. Everything
below comes from photographs and HABS survey photographs fetched through the
Wikimedia Commons API (thumbs at 960 px). I could not reach a SoHo photograph
of vault lights themselves, nor an NYC LPC report, so statements about those
are flagged as **inferred**.

How dimensions were estimated: from a person (1.7 m), a door leaf (2.4–2.7 m),
or a known lot width (25 ft = 7.6 m per lot, 50 ft = 15.2 m for a double lot)
in the same plane as the facade. Most street photos look up, which shortens
the upper storeys, so **ratios measured on near-frontal shots (HABS telephoto
of Haughwout, the frontal 502–504 Broadway) are trusted more than absolute
metres.** Confidence is marked H / M / L.

## 2. Massing and storeys

- **TYPICAL — 5 storeys** (shop storey + 4 lofts): 17 of 25 cast-iron or
  iron-ground buildings. H.
- **VARIES — 6 storeys**: Gunther, 47–49 Mercer, 40 Crosby, 472 Broadway (4/25),
  all 1865 or later. M.
- **RARE — mansard roof**: 28–30 Greene only (1 / 27). Adds a storey of dormers
  (3 dormers: two pedimented, central arched) on a dark slate roof. H.
- Older brick neighbours are 3–4 storeys (474 Broadway, Prince St), so the iron
  fronts read 1–2 storeys taller than what they replaced. H.
- Cornice lines along Greene St (8–34 photo) step by **±½–1 storey** from
  building to building; not a continuous line. H.
- Roofs are flat behind the cornice; a timber **water tank** shows on Haughwout
  and on several neighbours (VARIES). H.

- Upper storeys look **almost equal**. On the frontal Haughwout HABS photo the
  two full storeys measure the same (340 px each). Some 1850s fronts (Hopkins,
  Cary) may reduce slightly (≤10 %) going up, but perspective in my photos is
  too strong to confirm this. L.
- The shop storey is the tallest: **≈1.2–1.4 × an upper storey**. M.

## 3. Bays and bay rhythm

- **Bay pitch ≈ 2.5 m** (TYPICAL): a 25 ft lot carries **3 bays** (Hopkins, 85
  Leonard, 102 Greene, 40 Crosby) and a 50 ft lot carries **5–6** (47–49
  Mercer 5, 502 Broadway 6, 72–76 Greene 5 per unit). 502–504 Broadway gives
  15.2 m / 6 = 2.53 m. M.
- VARIES: 7 bays on Condict (≈2.0–2.2 m, tight arches) and 28–30 Greene. M.
- Corner buildings run long: 254 Canal ~21 × 8, Haughwout ~9 × 15, 75 Grand ~12
  × 5. H for "many", M for exact counts.
- **Uniform rhythm on all floors** (one column per bay boundary, the same column
  line from shop storey to cornice) is TYPICAL, at 23 of 25. The shop columns sit on
  the same axes as the upper columns. H.
- **Centre emphasis** VARIES (5/25): a 3-bay pavilion that projects slightly,
  with breaks in each storey cornice and a pediment above (72–76 and 28–30 Greene,
  47–49 Mercer, Cary, Woods). These are mostly 1865–75 and on 5+ bay fronts. H.
- **End bays framed by rusticated piers or quoins** VARIES (6/25: 72 Greene,
  47–49 Mercer, Condict, 75 Grand, Cary, 155 Mercer). The pier is ≈ 0.6–0.8 m wide,
  with banded rustication (horizontal channels), block quoins, or the prismatic
  "diamond-point" blocks on Condict. M.
- **Giant order spanning 2 storeys** VARIES (5/27: Condict, 85 Leonard, 502
  Broadway, 478 Broadway, 40 Crosby). Storeys are paired 2+3 and 4+5, with a
  recessed spandrel panel between the paired windows. H.

### Proportions measured on Haughwout (HABS, near-frontal telephoto) — H for ratios

- Storey height ≈ **2.0 × bay pitch** (340 px / 170 px). On 502 Broadway it is
  ≈ 1.8. So upper storeys are about twice as tall as a bay is wide.
- Vertical split of one storey: **column/opening zone ≈ 65 %**, entablature
  band ≈ 18 %, sill-level balustrade/pedestal ≈ 15 %.
- Opening width ≈ **0.5 × bay pitch** on Haughwout (the rest is big column +
  small colonnettes).
- Window opening height : width ≈ **2.4 : 1**; the arch springs at ≈ 56 % of the
  opening height (small colonnette tops).
- The big column is ≈ 0.8 × storey height, and its height ≈ 9 diameters.

## 4. Openings: arched vs lintelled — strongest date correlation

| Head form | Buildings | Dates | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat lintel in a thin pilaster–spandrel grid, almost no ornament | Laing Stores | 1848–49 | RARE (earliest Bogardus type) |
| **Full round arch** in every bay | Haughwout, 254 Canal (3rd–5th), Cary, Hopkins, Condict, 85 Leonard, 502 Broadway | **1856–61** | TYPICAL for the 1850s |
| **Segmental arch** (shallow) between columns | Gunther, 72–76 & 28–30 Greene, 47–49 Mercer, Woods, 75 Grand, most of the 8–34 Greene row | **1865–1875** | TYPICAL; the commonest head on Greene and Mercer |
| Flat heads, very wide glass, thin colonnettes; pierced iron spandrel arches (Neo-Grec) | 101 Spring, 478 Broadway, 40 Crosby, 472 Broadway, 102 Greene | **1870–1880s** | VARIES |

- Glass fraction of the bay rises with date: about 0.5 (Haughwout 1857), 0.6–0.65
  (Gunther 1871), 0.7 (28–30 Greene 1872), and 0.8–0.85 (101 Spring 1870, 478
  Broadway 1874). M.
- Mixed heads on one front: 254 Canal has a lintelled 2nd storey under
  round-arched storeys above. RARE, 1/25. H.
- Arched bays sometimes carry an **archivolt moulding with a keystone/console**
  (Haughwout, Hopkins, Cary); segmental bays carry a plain raised hood (Woods,
  Gunther). M.
- Sash: **2-over-2 double-hung**, with the top sash following the arch or
  segment, is TYPICAL. Modern replacements are often 1-over-1. Frames are painted
  **dark** (brown, bottle green, black) against a light front, a strong contrast
  seen on 18/25. H.

## 5. Column orders and ornament

- **Corinthian is the default** for both shop and upper columns, at about 16/25. H.
  - Shop-storey columns: Corinthian or Composite capital; the **lower ⅓ of the
    shaft is fluted or reeded above a ring**, with a smooth upper shaft (72–76 Greene,
    Gunther, 28–30 Greene). On 72–76 Greene the column is ≈0.45 m in diameter
    and ≈4.0 m tall to the top of the capital, on a 0.4–0.5 m square plinth
    (door-scaled). Height is ≈ 8.5–9 diameters. M.
  - Upper columns are engaged ¾ or ½ columns, fully fluted on rich fronts
    (Haughwout, Hopkins) and smooth on most Greene St fronts. H.
- VARIES — plain Tuscan or banded columns on cheaper fronts and shop storeys
  (Woods shop storey, 102 Greene, 101 Spring). H.
- VARIES — Neo-Grec: square piers with incised lines, stylised
  capitals, and pierced lace-like spandrel brackets (478 Broadway, 40 Crosby,
  472 Broadway). 1870s. H.
- RARE — paired colonnettes per pier (Cary), and the Palladian/Sansovino bay of a big
  column plus two small colonnettes carrying the arch (Haughwout). Both 1856–57,
  both on rich corner or Chambers St fronts. H.
- Each storey carries its **own full entablature** (architrave, frieze, cornice
  with dentils or modillions) on 22/25, which gives the stacked-layer look. On rich
  fronts a **sill-level balustrade** sits in every bay (Haughwout every floor;
  Gunther 2nd floor only). Otherwise sill panels carry a raised diamond or lozenge
  (28–30 Greene, Woods, 72 Greene). H.
- Small balconettes on brackets in one bay VARIES (Woods ×2). H.
- Name plaques: in the centre pediment (Cary, Woods, 47–49 Mercer), as a
  raised-letter band over the shop storey (Cary), or on a corner panel (Gunther). M.

## 6. Cornice, pediment, roofline

- TYPICAL: a **bracketed cornice** with modillions/consoles at ≈ 0.6–1.0 m
  centres over a dentil course and a frieze. Projection is ≈ 0.6–1.3 m. Larger
  console brackets sit over each pier on later fronts (Gunther, 75 Grand). H for form, M for size.
- Haughwout: a very heavy entablature with a rinceau (running-scroll) frieze, an
  egg-and-dart course and widely spaced modillions; ≈0.7 × the top storey in height. H.
- VARIES — a **pediment** rising from the cornice over the centre bays:
  triangular (Cary, Woods, 47–49 Mercer, 72 Greene) or segmental with a date
  roundel (75 Grand). About 6/25; 1856–75. H.
- RARE — mansard with dormers (28–30 Greene), and corner urns/finials (502 Broadway
  posts, 28–30 Greene dormers). H.
- Flat cornice with no pediment remains the most common (≈ 17/25). H.

## 7. Shop storey, loading and sidewalk

- The shop storey is **all columns and glass** in TYPICAL cases: a column on every
  bay line; between them a large plate-glass show window above a low panelled
  bulkhead (≈0.4–0.6 m); a transom band at ≈ 2.6–3.0 m; then the shop
  entablature. H.
- Entrances: **paired doors with tall glazed leaves and panelled bottoms, and a
  transom over them**. On 72–76 Greene, scaled from the doors, the pair is ≈1.35 m
  wide × ≈2.6 m with a ≈0.9 m transom, so the opening is ≈3.5 m tall. M.
  One bay is usually the **upper-floor (loft) entrance** with a narrower door
  (VARIES, 0.9–1.2 m).
- **Freight bay** TYPICAL on Greene, Mercer and Crosby: wide double doors of wood
  or sheet iron, 1.8–2.4 m wide and ≈3 m+ tall, sometimes studded (102 Greene,
  159 Mercer, 40 Crosby, 148 Mercer). On Broadway these are mostly gone. H.
- **Raised loading platform** in front of the shop storey: TYPICAL on Greene and
  Mercer (8–34 Greene, 148 Mercer, 72 Greene). It stands ≈0.4–1.0 m above the
  sidewalk at about wagon-bed height, runs the full frontage, and has steps (1–3
  risers) at the ends or at each door. 101 Spring has a low platform with cast-iron
  grilles in its face over the basement. H for existence, M for height.
- Vault lights: **inferred, not observed in my photos.** The platforms and
  sidewalk slabs next to the fronts are where iron plates set with small round
  glass lenses (≈ 3–4 cm, on a grid of ≈ 8–10 cm) would light the basement. L.
- Curbs stand ≈ 0.15–0.2 m above **granite-block (Belgian block)** street paving,
  which is visible on Greene, Mercer and Broome. H.
- Period (Holmes c.1855–59): **full-width canvas awnings on iron poles** over
  the sidewalk, and painted name boards on fascias. TYPICAL then, RARE now. H.

## 8. Materials and colour

- Iron fronts are painted to read as **stone**. Observed today, of 25 fronts:
  - white or off-white, about 11;
  - cream, ivory or buff, about 9;
  - pale grey or stone-grey, about 3 (Haughwout's is a warm pale grey);
  - grey-blue, 1 (101 Spring);
  - ochre or tan, 1 (Greene St row).
  The usual range is therefore **white to buff** (H). Strong colours are RARE.
- The **shop storey is often painted darker** than the front above, in charcoal,
  black, grey-green or dark green (Woods unit, 478 Broadway, 40 Crosby, 148 Mercer,
  155 Mercer, St Nicholas, Greene St row). Seen on about 8/25. VARIES. H.
- Rusticated or channelled surfaces cast into the iron (Cary's ground, the 72 Greene
  piers, the Condict quoins) imitate masonry. H.
- Brick neighbours and brick lofts are **red brick or red/pink paint** with
  brownstone or cast-iron window hoods painted dark brown (159 Mercer, 474
  Broadway, #26). Shutters are grey-green (148 Mercer). H.
- **Fire escapes** are TYPICAL (≈ 15/25): black, or painted the facade colour,
  across 2–3 bays per floor with drop ladders. They are a later addition, but they are
  part of the present look. H.

## 9. Correlations

- **Date to opening shape**: round arch (1850s), then segmental arch (late 1860s–70s),
  then flat-headed Neo-Grec glass walls (1870s–80s). This is the strongest and
  most reliable signal. H.
- **Date to glass fraction**: about 50 % rising to about 85 % of bay width (M). Ornament
  per bay drops at the same time (Neo-Grec is flatter).
- **Street to wealth**: Broadway fronts are taller per storey (+0.5–1 m), longer,
  and carry richer orders, balustrades and heavy cornices (Haughwout, 502).
  Greene St has the richest *side-street* iron fronts (72–76, 28–30, with
  pavilions, pediments and the mansard). Mercer and Crosby are plainer: about
  half of my Mercer sample has **brick upper storeys with only a cast-iron shop
  storey** (159, 148, 155 Mercer). H.
- **Width to centre emphasis**: pediments and pavilions appear only on fronts of 5+ bays;
  3-bay fronts are symmetrical without a centre feature. H.
- **Corner to treatment**: square corners with a quoined pier are TYPICAL. A
  rounded corner is RARE (Gunther, with curved sash; Laing). H.

## 10. Quick TYPICAL / VARIES / RARE summary

**TYPICAL**
- 5 storeys; a tall shop storey (4.5–5 m, or 5.5–6.5 m on Broadway); upper storeys 3.6–4.5 m.
- A bay pitch of ≈ 2.5 m, so 3 bays per 25 ft lot, with column lines continuous
  from shop storey to cornice.
- Every storey has its own entablature; engaged Corinthian columns between the bays.
- Segmental-arched (1865–75) or round-arched (1850s) 2-over-2 sash with dark frames.
- Flat bracketed and dentilled crowning cornice.
- A shop storey of columns and plate glass, with paired glazed doors and transoms;
  a freight bay and a raised loading platform on the side streets.
- White, cream or buff paint; fire escapes.

**VARIES**
- 6 storeys; a 3-bay centre pavilion with a pediment; quoined or rusticated end piers.
- A giant order spanning 2 storeys; a sill balustrade in every bay; a darker shop storey.
- Neo-Grec glass fronts; brick upper storeys over an iron shop storey.
- Balconettes; a roof water tank.

**RARE**
- Mansard roof; rounded corner with curved glass.
- Laing-type flat-lintel grid; mixed lintelled and arched storeys (254 Canal).
- The Palladian big-column-plus-colonnettes bay (Haughwout); paired colonnettes (Cary).
- Strong colours (grey-blue, ochre); canvas awnings today.
