- **Scale.** Floor heights were taken as about 3 m (Venice piani nobili 4.5-5 m), doors 2.2-2.4 m, and Amsterdam front doors 2.6-3.0 m including the transom. Pixel measurements were taken off the 600 px-wide images. Each frontage is a range, and confidence is stated each time.
- **Photos.** Reference photos came from Wikimedia Commons, through the API given in the brief.
  - Marked (V): I looked at the image myself during this review.
  - Marked (K): compared from the image URL plus prior knowledge of the building.

## img00

**Identification.** Amsterdam canal house with a neck gable (halsgevel). It has quarter-round claw pieces and a triangular pediment over the neck. It has a raised ground floor (bel-étage) with a stoop (stoep) and a basement (souterrain). Date c. 1640-1700. Confidence high on the city, medium on the date.

**Frontage.**
- The front is 2 bays, 348 px wide.
- The four storeys measure 183, 200, 234 and 295 px. At about 3 m average that is roughly 75-90 px/m, so the frontage is **about 4-4.7 m**.
- Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Cromhouthuizen, Herengracht 364-370 (ref 15); the narrow neck-gable houses on the Brouwersgracht and the Leliegracht.

**Right**
- Neck gable with claw pieces and a pediment, and a hoisting beam above a hoist door in the neck.
- Wall anchors (muurankers) at each floor.
- Upper-floor windows diminish upwards.
- A stoop with railing leads to the bel-étage door, and the basement door is tucked under the stoop landing. This is exactly the Amsterdam arrangement: Herengracht 334 (ref 16) has the basement entrance under the stoop.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **Neck gable on a 2-bay front.** Neck gables almost always crown 3-bay fronts of 6-8 m, like the Cromhout houses. Fronts of 4-4.5 m nearly always carry spout or bell gables. A 2-bay neck gable exists but is rare.
2. **The stoop runs down across the left bay's basement window.** The steps and balusters are drawn over a glazed basement opening. Real single-flight stoops run in front of blank plinth or a cellar hatch, never across a window.
3. **The upper windows are nearly square.** The top floor is about 1:1 and the 2nd floor about 1:1.2. Real Amsterdam upper sashes are 1:1.6-2.0 even on the top floor (refs 15-17).
4. The top storey is about 2.0-2.3 m tall, which is too low. Real ones are 2.6-3.0 m.
5. No white-painted window frames and no small-paned sashes. Frames are the strongest visual signature of these streets, though this is partly a rendering limit.
6. The claw pieces are plain quarter-circles. Real ones are carved sandstone scrolls, dolphins or festoons, and nothing in the drawing hints at carving.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** the neck gable on a 2-bay house, together with squat square windows.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img01

**Identification.** Amsterdam step gable (trapgevel) canal house, Dutch Renaissance type (Hendrick de Keyser school). The gable has 4 steps a side and two gable floors (2 windows, then 3). There are stone bands (speklagen) and keystones over the windows. It has a stoop and basement. Date c. 1600-1665. Confidence high on the city, medium on the date.

**Frontage.**
- 3 bays, 414 px wide.
- The body storeys are about 160, 180 and 220 px, roughly 58 px/m, so the frontage is **about 6.5-7.5 m**.
- Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Huis met de Hoofden, Keizersgracht 123 (ref 19); Herengracht 334 (ref 16); Huis Bartolotti (ref 18).

**Right**
- The step profile has stone caps.
- The light horizontal bands double at sill and impost level, as on Herengracht 334.
- Keystones sit over the windows.
- The bay count steps down in the gable (3 then 2).
- Hoisting beam at the apex.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **The windows belong to the wrong century.** These are large plain single-light openings 1.0-1.2 m wide. A step gable of 1600-1665 has cross-windows (kruiskozijnen) with shutters on the lower half. It also has brick relieving arches with tympana, and often painted shutters. Herengracht 334 shows the relieving arches and block keystones the drawing omits. Later sash replacements are common, but the relieving arches would remain.
2. **The stoop runs over a basement window again**, the same clash as img00.
3. **The facade has no ornament at all.** These fronts carry scroll blocks, masks or vases at each step. Huis met de Hoofden and Bartolotti have heavy sculpture, and even the modest Herengracht 334 has scroll blocks at the gable foot.
4. The gable apex is a small triangular pediment. Step gables end in a narrow top step, often with a finial; a pediment cap belongs to neck gables.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** 18th-century sash-type openings without relieving arches or cross-windows on a 17th-century step gable.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img02 (identical to img07)

**Identification.** Veneto-Byzantine fondaco or casa-fondaco. It has a ground-floor water portico of 7 round arches on columns and a continuous upper arcade of 13 lights with a balustrade. Paired arched windows sit in the end wings, and there is a frieze of paterae (roundels) and square inlays. Venice, 12th-13th c. Confidence high.

**Frontage.**
- Two storeys, 172 px high in total.
- With Fondaco-type storeys of about 5-6 m, that is roughly 14-17 px/m, so the 530 px width is **about 32-38 m**.
- Confidence low-medium, because nothing in the drawing gives a human scale.

**Closest real buildings:** Fondaco dei Turchi, both pre-restoration (ref 1) and as restored (ref 2); Ca' Loredan and Ca' Farsetti (ref 3); Ca' da Mosto (ref 4).

**Right**
- Upper arcade arches against ground arches: 13 to 7, about 1.9:1. The Fondaco has 18 to 10, 1.8:1. Ca' Loredan's portico stands under a denser upper colonnade in the same way.
- Paterae in a band between the floors: the Fondaco has roundels in the spandrels and an inlay band.
- The open portico is drawn dark.
- The end wings are treated differently from the centre, which is the "tower" (torreselle) scheme.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **The skyline is flat and the building has only 2 storeys, with no merlons, no roof and no towers.** Every surviving example either keeps merlons and corner towers (the Fondaco), carries later upper storeys (Loredan, Farsetti, Ca' da Mosto), or shows a tiled roof. A two-storey box with a bare cornice line looks unfinished.
2. Turned balusters run between the columns. The originals had carved plutei panels. Loredan and Farsetti have later balustrades, so this is tolerable.
3. The arches are not stilted, and the extrados has no dentil or "dogtooth" moulding framing them.
4. Ground arches about 2.2-2.6 m wide are a bit narrow against the Fondaco's spans of about 3 m.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** the truncated flat-topped silhouette with nothing above the upper arcade.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img04

**Identification.** Venetian Gothic palazzo with a tripartite front. The central 5-light ogee window (pentafora) with balcony is repeated on both piani nobili. There are paired single ogee lancets in each wing and a hipped tiled roof with two inverted-cone chimneys (fumaioli). The ground floor has two pointed portals. The finish is pale intonaco or Istrian stone. Venice, c. 1420-1480. Confidence high.

**Frontage.**
- Three storeys are about 405 px high, at 4.5-5 m each, giving roughly 28-30 px/m.
- So the 510 px width is **about 17-18 m**.
- Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Palazzo Giustinian (ref 9); Palazzo Barbaro (ref 11); Palazzo Pisani Moretta (ref 6), which also has two water gates.

**Right**
- The tripartite scheme: polifora over the portego, with two single lights in each wing.
- The polifora is repeated on both noble floors.
- Ogee heads and stone-framed lights.
- Dentil string courses, Venetian funnel chimneys, a corner cable or quoin edge, and a stone base course.
- The 5-light count suits a front of about 17 m.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **The two pointed portals sit at the outer wings, under the side windows.** The water gate serves the central androne under the portego. Pisani Moretta has two gates, but both are central, side by side under the polifore. Gates in the outer bays lead nowhere in the tripartite plan.
2. **The wing windows have no balconies** (poggioli). Most Gothic palazzi have them on the piano nobile (Pisani Moretta, Giustinian, Barbaro). This is a common variation rather than an error, but it makes the front look bare.
3. The ground-floor windows have the same ogee heads as the noble floors. Ground and mezzanine lights are normally small and rectangular, or simple pointed arches.
4. The two piani nobili are equal in height. The first is usually taller.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** the pair of water gates in the outer bays instead of the centre.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img05

**Identification.** Amsterdam neck-gable canal house, 3 bays and 4 storeys over a basement. It has a stoop to the left-bay door, claw pieces and a pediment. Date c. 1640-1720. Confidence high.

**Frontage.**
- Storeys of 134, 153, 174 and 222 px give roughly 53-60 px/m.
- So the 414 px width is **about 7-7.8 m**.
- Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Cromhouthuizen (ref 15); the neck gables on the Herengracht and Keizersgracht.

**Right**
- A 3-bay front with a neck gable whose neck is one bay wide (about 40% of the front), as in the Vingboons engraving.
- The bel-étage is the tallest floor and the floors diminish upwards.
- The door is in the end bay with a transom light.
- The stoop runs parallel to the facade, and the basement door is under the landing.
- Wall anchors at each floor.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **Upper windows are about 1:1.1.** The Cromhout engraving shows about 1:1.8 on every floor.
2. **The stoop runs across a glazed basement window** (blue panes are visible between the stair balusters).
3. The neck carries a hoist door, where a window is more usual. This is acceptable, because hoist doors do occur.
4. No pediment or console over the door. A canal house of this rank usually marks the door, as the Cromhout houses do.
5. The claw pieces are bare quarter-circles.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 4/5.
**Most damaging error:** squat upper windows.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS. This is the best of the Amsterdam set.

## img06

**Identification.** Venetian Gothic palazzo in red intonaco.
- **Ground floor:** a central pointed water gate, with two rows of small square windows (ground floor and mezzanine) on each side.
- **Noble floors:** two piani nobili, each with a 4-light ogee polifora (quadrifora) and balcony, and single ogee lights with individual balconies in the wings.
- **Top:** an attic floor of 7 small square windows, under a dentil cornice and hipped roof with fumaioli.

Venice, 15th c., with a later attic. Confidence high.

**Frontage:** 4 levels in about 550 px gives roughly 30 px/m, so the frontage is **about 15-17 m**. Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Palazzo Pisani Moretta (ref 6), whose pink intonaco and wing lights with balconies match closely; Palazzo Bembo (ref 13); Palazzo Soranzo (ref 12).

**Right**
- A single central water gate under the polifore.
- The ground floor and mezzanine have small square openings in pairs, exactly as on Pisani Moretta.
- Balconies on every wing light, and the polifora is repeated on both noble floors.
- The attic of small windows is plausible as a later storey.
- Red intonaco with white Istrian stone trim.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. The polifore have no tracery. That is fine, since many have none.
2. The 7 attic windows do not align with the bays below: there are 3 over a 4-light polifora.
3. The polifora lights are slightly squat, at about 1:2.2 against 1:2.6-3 in reality.

**Scores:** Recognisability 5/5, Plausibility 4/5.
**Most damaging error:** the attic windows out of alignment with the bays below (minor).
**Passes at a glance:** YES.

## img09

**Identification.** Amsterdam cornice-front house (lijstgevel), 18th or early 19th c. It has a console frieze, a central dormer with hoisting beam and pediment, 3 bays, 5 storeys and a basement, and a stoop. Confidence high.

**Frontage.**
- Storeys measure 114-212 px, roughly 48-55 px/m.
- So the 414 px width is **about 7.5-8.6 m**.
- Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Herengracht 476 (ref 20); Museum Van Loon (ref 17); Huis De Vicq (ref 21).

**Right**
- A cornice on consoles, and a dormer (dakkapel) with a hoist door and hoisting beam.
- The bel-étage is the tallest floor and the floors diminish upwards.
- Stoop and basement, and wall anchors.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **There are five full storeys above the basement.** Lijstgevels on the canals are usually 3-4 storeys plus the frieze attic, about 14-16 m. Five pushes this to about 17-18 m. That happens in the late 19th century, but it is rare on a Louis-style console cornice.
2. **The top-floor windows crowd up under the console frieze.** On real fronts the frieze is a distinct band, often with small attic lights between the consoles.
3. The cornice is thin. It has no deep moulded crown or balustrade (as at Van Loon).
4. The stoop crosses a basement window.
5. The windows are again squat, about 1:1 to 1:1.2 on the upper floors.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** too many storeys, with squat windows.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img10

**Identification.** Venetian Gothic palazzo, 3 storeys, wide (3 single lights in each wing). It has central trilobed or ogee 3-light openings on both noble floors, a ground floor of 10 rectangular windows and a central pointed gate, and fumaioli. Venice, 15th c. Confidence medium-high.

**Frontage:** 3 storeys in about 280 px gives roughly 20-22 px/m, so the frontage is **about 23-26 m**. Confidence low-medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Ca' Foscari (ref 8), for the wide front; Palazzo Giustinian (ref 9); the Sagredo front (ref 14).

**Right**
- The tripartite scheme, with 3 wing lights a side on a wide front.
- Balconies on the wing lights.
- A continuous ground-floor rhythm aligned with the bays, and a central gate.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **The central polifora lights are about 57 px wide by about 70 px tall,** a ratio of about 1:1.2, with broad, flattened ogee heads. Real Venetian Gothic polifore have narrow lights of about 0.9-1.1 m by 2.8-3.5 m (1:2.5-3), divided by slender colonnettes. Here three huge squat lights replace the 5-8 lights such a wide front would carry; Ca' Foscari has 8. This reads as Moorish or Orientalist pastiche.
2. Each polifora is only as wide as about 3 wing bays, yet holds just 3 openings.
3. The wing lights are slender and correct, which makes the clash with the centre more obvious.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 2/5.
**Most damaging error:** squat, broad 3-light central polifore.
**Passes at a glance:** NO.

## img11

**Identification.** Venetian Gothic palazzo in red intonaco, 4 levels.
- **Ground floor:** two rows of small windows (ground floor and mezzanine), and a round gate.
- **Piano nobile:** a 4-light polifora with balcony.
- **2nd piano nobile:** a 4-light polifora with a band of 4 quatrefoils above it.
- **Top floor:** a broad 3-light opening.
- **Wings:** 3 single lights a side, with dark iron balcony grilles.

Venice, 15th c. Confidence high.

**Frontage:** 4 levels in about 420 px gives roughly 24-27 px/m, so the frontage is **about 19-22 m**. Confidence medium.

**Closest real buildings:** Palazzo Pisani Moretta (ref 6); Ca' d'Oro (ref 7); Palazzo Bernardo and the Sagredo front (ref 14), all with quatrefoil tracery.

**Right**
- Quatrefoil tracery, the signature of the Doge's Palace school.
- Polifore stacked on the centre axis.
- Small windows on the ground floor and mezzanine, and red intonaco with stone trim.

**Wrong or implausible**
1. **The tracery is drawn as a separate band of 4 isolated quatrefoils on a pale panel above the arches.** On Pisani Moretta, Ca' d'Oro and Bernardo, each quatrefoil rises out of the apexes of the ogee arches in one interlaced web. Here they are disconnected, so the "Doge's Palace" reading is half lost.
2. **The top floor carries a third, broad, squat 3-light polifora,** the same 1:1.2 problem as img10. Where a third tier exists, it has the same slender lights as below, or the attic simply has small windows.
3. The gate is round-headed (Renaissance) on a Gothic front.
4. The wing balconies are dark, solid grilles. Venetian Gothic poggioli are stone balustrades.

**Scores:** Recognisability 4/5, Plausibility 3/5.
**Most damaging error:** tracery floating apart from the arcade, plus the squat top polifora.
**Passes at a glance:** WITH FLAWS.

## img13

**Identification.** Amsterdam (less certain) cornice-front house in cream render or stucco. It has a console frieze, a central dormer with hoist, 3 bays, 5 storeys, a basement and a stoop. 19th c. neoclassical render. Confidence medium on the city: without brick, the dormer hoist is the only strong Amsterdam cue.

**Frontage:** about 7.5-8.6 m, as img09.

**Closest real buildings:**
- Plastered 19th-century fronts on the Herengracht and Keizersgracht, for example around Herengracht 502-508 (K).
- Huis De Vicq (ref 21).

**Right**
- Rendered fronts do exist in Amsterdam.

### Recurring errors, ranked by damage

1. **Venice: squat, broad polifora lights** (img10, img11 top floor, img15 top floor). Lights are about 1:1.2-1.7 instead of 1:2.5-3, and there are too few of them. This alone turns a Gothic palazzo into Orientalist pastiche. Rule: a polifora light is about 1 m wide and 2.5-3 times as tall. The number of lights scales with the width of the central bay (4-8). Never use 3 broad lights on a wide front.
2. **Amsterdam: squat upper-floor windows** (about 1:1 to 1:1.2) and top storeys only 2.0-2.3 m tall, in every Amsterdam image. Real canal-house sashes are 1:1.6-2.0 on all floors, diminishing in height but not becoming square.
3. **Amsterdam: the stoop is drawn across a glazed basement window** (img00, img01, img05, img09, img12, img13: every one). The stair should run in front of blank plinth, or the basement window should move to the other bay.
4. **Venice: quatrefoil tracery is drawn as a detached band** above the arcade (img11, img15). In reality it springs from the arch apexes as one interlaced web.
5. **Venice: water-gate placement and shape.**
   - img04 has twin gates in the outer bays. Pairs, as at Pisani Moretta, sit together at the centre.
   - img08 and img11 have round-headed gates on Gothic fronts.
6. **Amsterdam: style-period mismatches.**
   - The step gable (img01) has no cross-windows or relieving arches.
   - The neck gable sits on a 2-bay front (img00, img12).
   - The lijstgevel has 5 storeys (img09, img13).
   - Gable ornament is absent everywhere: the claw pieces are plain quarter-circles.
7. **Byzantine: the flat 2-storey silhouette with no merlons or towers** (img02, img03, img07). img14 shows the generator can do the right thing.
8. **Generator:** img02 and img07 are byte-identical.
