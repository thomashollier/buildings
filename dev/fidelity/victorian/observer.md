# San Francisco Victorian row house: the street facade, observed

Independent observer report. I worked only from photographs and published sources on the web. I did not open the existing model.
Scope: wood-built row houses and flats of 1865-1905 (Italianate, San Francisco Stick / Stick-Eastlake, Queen Anne), with Edwardian (1901-1915) for contrast.

How I measured: I scaled each straight-on photo from the front width, taking the lot as 25 ft = 7.62 m and the facade as roughly the full lot width less 0-0.3 m. I cross-checked with door leaves (2.4-2.7 m on Italianate double doors), garage doors (about 2.3-2.4 m) and stair risers (about 18 cm). Heights from photos are ±10-15%. Counts (bays, faces, steps, brackets) are exact where the photo allows.

## 2. Rules

Confidence: **H** = seen consistently in many buildings or stated by an official source. **M** = seen in several, or inferred with good scale references. **L** = few examples, or estimated.

### A. Lot, width and layout

**R1. Front width.** Typical 25 ft (7.6 m); range 20-30 ft; double lots of 50 ft and over for the rich. Lots are about 100-137 ft deep (Liberty: "about 115' deep and usually about 25' wide"; the street was subdivided "into 25-foot lots" in 1868). Western Addition lots of 27.5 ft exist (vara survey; unverified). The facade fills the lot width less a 0-1 m side gap or light well. **H.** Evidence: Liberty NRHP; Scott, Steiner, Bush and Walter photos, scaled.

**R2. Setback.**
- Western Addition, Haight and Pacific Heights rows: facade at or within about 1-3 m of the property line, so the stoop starts at the sidewalk.
- Mission/Liberty: about 12 ft (3.7 m) front gardens ("Most houses are set back about twelve feet").
- Generator: setback is a district parameter, 0-4 m. **H.**

**R3. Layout follows width (single family, 20-27 ft).** Two vertical columns, deliberately asymmetric:
- A **bay column** of about 50-60% of the width.
- An **entry column** of about 35-45%: door at the parlour level, and above it one single window, or a pair on older/flat fronts.
- Mirrored pairs exist, with entries adjacent (1239/1245 Scott) or bays adjacent.

Measured bay share of facade width: 2432 Washington 56%; TREA about 40%; Stick 2023 about 50%; Walter about 45%. **H.**

**R4. Flat-front variant (c.1860s-1870s, cheaper or narrower lots).** No bay. 3 equal openings per floor:
- Parlour floor: 2 windows plus the door.
- Upper floor: 3 windows aligned over them.
- Door column always at one side.

Evidence: 2119 Bush, 45-47 Beideman, 1321, Liberty #12 ("2 curve-pedimented windows per floor instead of bay"). Narrower or cheaper houses may have 2 openings per floor (door plus a paired window). **H.**

**R5. Wider fronts (37-50 ft, wealthy).** Symmetrical: **two bays flanking a central portico**, 2-3 storeys, side yards, and sometimes quoins and a pedimented Corinthian portico. Evidence: Liberty #31 "portico between pair of bays", #22 "very large house, pedimented Corinthian portico ... quoins", Westerfeld. **M.**

**R6. Flats (2 or 3 units).**
- **2-flats:** two doors side by side under one hood or within one recessed porch, at the top of one stoop. One door serves the lower flat; the other opens onto an internal stair to the upper flat.
  - Evidence: Beideman 33-35, with the two doors split by a window; Liberty #6 "one entry on the facade's west side, another ... recessed along the east side".
- **3-flats:** typically Edwardian, 1900-1915. Three doors in one deep recessed entry, a 3-storey facade, two bays (Hermann St).
- Flats get *one door per unit* at the street. The door count is the cheapest signal of tenure. **H** for Edwardian, **M** for Victorian 2-flats.

**R7. Corner lots** carry the bays and cornice round onto the side street: slanted or square corner bays, often a store on the ground floor with flats above (Sacramento HABS, Sanchez, 1249 Scott). Corner Queen Annes get the **round turret** (Pierce & Waller; Masonic row). **H.**

### B. Storeys and heights

**R8. Storey scheme.** The standard is **raised basement + 2 full storeys**, with an attic in Queen Annes. Liberty: almost every 1863-1896 house is "2 stories & basement"; Queen Annes "2 stories & basement & attic". 3 storeys + basement appears for larger houses and Edwardian flats. 1-storey cottages (raised basement + 1) are a minority (Liberty Hill cottage; Liberty #... Stick cottage). **H.**

**R9. Raised basement.** Height above the sidewalk is typically 1.8-2.7 m (from stoop counts × 18 cm risers); range 0.9 m (5-6 steps, Beideman) to 3.0+ m on downhill sides.
- Originally a service or storage storey with small windows, sometimes arched (2119 Bush) or round "porthole" (Liberty #22).
- Today **garages are commonly cut in under the bay**. Liberty: "addition of a garage, usually under the bay". It is not original.
- Seen in Scott ×2, Washington, Stick 2023, Walter, Noe and Liberty Hill (about 50% of the photographed houses).
- Basement face: rusticated channel boards, panelled wood, or (Edwardian) rusticated concrete block. **H.**

**R10. Storey heights (estimated, scaled from a 7.6 m front).**

| Level | Typical | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Parlour floor, floor-to-floor | 4.1 m | 3.8-4.6 m |
| Upper floor | 3.7 m | 3.4-4.0 m |
| Frieze + cornice / false-front band | 1.4 m | 1.1-1.8 m |
| Queen Anne gable above the eave line | 0.6-0.9 × gable width | |
| Edwardian floors (lower, more even) | 3.2-3.5 m | |

**M.** Evidence: 2119 Bush (about 105 px/m), Stick 2023 (about 50 px/m), Washington.

**R11. Overall proportion.** Sidewalk to cornice top ≈ **1.6-1.9 × front width** for the standard 2+basement house (Stick 2023 1:1.85; 2119 Bush about 1:1.6; Scott about 1:1.6). That is about 12-14.5 m on 7.6 m, and a Queen Anne gable apex adds about 3-5 m. The facade reads **tall and narrow** (Liberty: "Proportions tend to be tall and narrow ... balanced by one or two strong horizontals"). **H.**

### C. The bay window

**R12. Bay shape by date.** This is the most important style switch. **H.**
- **Italianate (c.1870-1885): slanted (canted) bay**, half-octagonal: centre face parallel to the street, two side faces at 45°. Liberty: "half-octagonal bay rising into the cornice".
- **Stick / Stick-Eastlake (c.1880-1892): square (rectangular) bay**, the "box bay": wide front face, narrow return faces at 90°.
- **Queen Anne (c.1888-1900):** any of slanted, square, round or part-hexagonal (Liberty: "bays may be round, part-hexagonal or rectangular"). Often **square on the upper floor over a slanted bay on the parlour floor**, the upper bay corbelled out on brackets at its corners (Haight row; Steiner).
- **Edwardian (1901-1915):** slanted bays with narrow sides, or **round "swell" bays**, rising 2-3 storeys to the roof cornice (Hermann, Sanchez).

**R13. Bay faces and widths.**
- **Slanted bay:** 3 faces, **one window per face**. Centre face ≈ 1.3-1.7 m with a window 0.9-1.1 m wide; side faces ≈ 0.8-1.1 m true width with windows 0.6-0.8 m. Projection ≈ 0.6-0.9 m. Face boundaries are narrow pilasters or engaged colonnettes (Pacific Heights detail; Scott: colonnettes with Corinthian-ish caps). **M-H.**
- **Square bay:** front face ≈ 2.4-3.2 m holding **2 windows** (Walter) or a wide window flanked by narrow sidelights (Stick 2023: 3 lights). Returns 0.4-0.7 m, each with 1 narrow window or blank panels. Projection ≈ 0.6-0.8 m. **M.**
- **Round bay (Edwardian / QA turret):** 3-5 curved sashes. **M.**

**R14. Vertical extent.**
- The bay starts at the **parlour floor**. It does *not* start at grade: under it is basement wall flush with the bay, or a later garage.
- It rises through **2 storeys** (parlour + upper) and **runs into the main cornice**: the frieze and brackets wrap round the bay's faces, and the bay has no separate roof (Italianate, Stick). Liberty: Italianate "bay rising into the cornice".
- Italianate parlour bays **can be 1 storey only**, with a pair of upper windows above (Liberty #7 "pair of 2nd-floor windows above first-story bay"; Liberty Hill house). Early Italianates and Edwardian lower-cost buildings have 1-storey bays (Liberty #11, #33 "single-story bay").
- Between the storeys, each bay carries a panelled apron or spandrel band of about 0.5-0.8 m with a small cornice. **H.**

**R15. Bay cap.**
- Italianate and Stick: no separate cap. The building's bracketed cornice breaks forward over the bay and follows its plan. There is usually one bracket at each angle of the bay, plus intermediates.
- Queen Anne:
  - the bay stops under an overhanging gable whose corners are carried on brackets or corbelling (Steiner), or
  - it ends in a small pent or hipped roof under the main eave, or
  - it becomes a turret: a round or polygonal tower with a conical or ogee roof and finial, on corner or high-end lots only.
- Edwardian: a flat-topped bay under a deep boxed or modillioned cornice. **H.**

### D. Entry and stoop

**R16. Stoop.**
- **Straight run perpendicular to the facade**, beginning at or near the sidewalk, at the side of the lot opposite the bay.
- Risers: typical **10-14**, range 5-18 (Beideman 6 and about 5; 1321 8; Scott about 11; Walter 13; 2119 Bush 13+; Stick 2023 about 15; Washington about 17 with a landing).
- Width 1.1-1.5 m.
- Railing: a solid panelled newel at the bottom with a ball or urn finial; turned balusters (Italianate/Stick) or a solid panelled wall (Edwardian).
- Variants:
  - on setback lots or hills, an **intermediate landing**, or a stair **turning 90° to run parallel to the facade** (Liberty Hill cottage; Waller row);
  - mirrored pairs share adjacent stoops.

**H.**

**R17. Entry form by style.**
- **Italianate:** the door is almost flush with the facade. Door hoods are of two kinds:
  - a **flat entablature hood on large scrolled consoles**, projecting about 0.3-0.5 m (1321; TREA; Beideman);
  - a **columned portico**: square or round columns, often Corinthian, carrying an entablature with a balustrade on top (Liberty #2 "fat finials on door-hood balustrade"), projecting about 0.8-1.5 m (2119 Bush).
  
  Doors are **paired, double-leaf, with a transom** (Bulletin 18: "doors are paired and capped by a hood"). Door leaves about 2.4-2.7 m; door plus transom about 3.0-3.4 m. Some have a round-headed door opening with a semicircular transom (Liberty #7).
- **Stick:** the door sits in a **recessed porch** about 1-1.8 m deep. At the facade line are 2 turned or chamfered colonnettes (or square posts) carrying a beam with a small **gabled pediment**, often with a sunburst or spindle frieze (Stick 2023; Liberty #28 "sunburst in portico pediment").
- **Queen Anne:** a **round-arched recessed porch**, deep and shadowed, with spandrel ornament (1550 Page; Waller), or a columned portico with a pediment (Haight). Often with a balcony or recessed loggia above it (1550 Page; Liberty #18).
- **Edwardian:** a deep recessed entry framed by Ionic or box columns, with 1-3 doors.

**H.**

### E. Windows

**R18. Sash and proportion.** All are wood double-hung sash. Italianate: 2/2 early, 1/1 later. Stick/Queen Anne: 1/1 with Queen Anne upper sashes (a border of small, often coloured lights), and transom lights over bay windows. Edwardian: 1/1, sometimes with a leaded upper sash. Height:width:
- Italianate parlour windows ≈ **2.8-3.2 : 1** (about 1.0 × 2.9 m, 2119 Bush);
- upper floor ≈ 2.5 : 1;
- Stick ≈ 2.2-2.6 : 1;
- Queen Anne and Edwardian ≈ 1.6-2.2 : 1, often grouped.

Sills are about 0.4-0.6 m above the floor, so the windows nearly fill the storey: head about 0.4-0.7 m below the next floor. **M.**

**R19. Window heads and hoods by style.** **H.**
- **Italianate:** segmental-arched or round-headed sash. The surround is a moulded architrave with an **arched hood moulding** (label) with a keystone or crest (1249 Scott; Beideman 45). Alternatively, an **entablature hood** on small consoles crowned by a **triangular or segmental pediment**. Upper and lower floors often differ: 2119 Bush has segmental pediments on the parlour floor and triangular ones above. In bays, a segmental-arched head per face with **colonnettes between faces**.
- **Stick/Eastlake:** **flat heads**, squared surrounds of flat boards and vertical "sticks". Lintel boards with incised, chamfered, or rosette/fan decoration. A small **sunburst or fan panel** over windows or in a segmental tympanum (Walter; Stick 2023). Elongated "attenuated" brackets (Liberty #1, #27).
- **Queen Anne:** flat heads with **transom lights**. Palladian or round-arched feature windows in the gable or porch. Panels of carved swags or garlands, or stained glass.
- **Edwardian:** plain flat heads, minimal trim. Egg-and-dart or dentil bands at the floor lines.

**R20. Single window over the entry** (the bay-house type): one window in the entry column on the upper floor, in the same family as the bay windows but usually with a stronger pediment. Some have a pair, or a small balcony (Queen Anne). Scott: round-headed with a crested hood. TREA: flat entablature hood. **H.**

### F. The top

**R21. Italianate and Stick top: false front with bracketed cornice.**
- A flat-topped parapet facade hides a gable roof running front to back (Liberty: "most buildings have corniced false fronts masking gable roofs"; Bulletin 18: false fronts "distinguishing").
- The cornice band is about 1.1-1.8 m tall: architrave moulding, then a **panelled frieze**, then **scrolled brackets (modillions) in a regular rhythm**, then a projecting crown of about 0.5-0.8 m.
- The frieze sometimes has small horizontal attic lights or vents between the brackets (TREA; Beideman 45).
- The return of the false front is visible above the neighbours.

Counts:
- Bracket count on a 25 ft front is **8-12** (1321: 8; 2119 Bush: about 9 + 2 end; TREA: about 10 with the bay); spacing about 0.6-0.9 m.
- Brackets are often **paired at the ends and at the bay angles**, and a pair sits at each end of the cornice.
- Stick adds a central **gablet or pediment** over the bay, often with a sunburst (Stick 2023; Walter), and longer, thinner brackets.

**H.**

**R22. Queen Anne top: street-facing gable.**
- A front-facing gable, either full width or over the bay half, with a **steep pitch of about 50-60°** (estimated from the Steiner row).
- It **overhangs the bay** on brackets, has a moulded eave/cornice band at its base (often a full entablature with a frieze that continues across the facade), and ends in a pointed finial at the apex.
- Gable field: fish-scale or other patterned shingles, lattice or spindle panels, a sunburst or fan at the apex, and 1-3 attic windows (often a pair or a Palladian group).
- Sometimes a hip roof with a cross gable; sometimes the gable sits "above cornice" over a false-front band (Liberty #9).
- Turrets appear on **corner and high-end houses only** (1550 Page; Pierce & Waller; Masonic row; Liberty #19 "cone-topped turret"). In a mid-block row of builder Queen Annes (Steiner, Waller, Haight), most have a gable and no turret.

**H** for the gable; **M** for the pitch.

**R23. Edwardian top:** flat roof behind a deep projecting boxed cornice (Sanchez) or a modillion-and-dentil cornice (Hermann). No gable, no false-front frieze panels. Sometimes a low parapet. **H.**

### G. Trim vocabulary and materials

**R24. Trim by style.**
- **Italianate:**
  - scrolled consoles and modillion brackets;
  - panelled friezes;
  - segmental and triangular pediments, arched hood moulds with keystones;
  - engaged colonnettes on bays;
  - Corinthian portico columns;
  - corner boards treated as **pilasters** running the full height;
  - channel-rusticated boards at the basement;
  - **wooden quoins** on larger houses (Liberty #22);
  - balustrades on door hoods.
- **Stick/Eastlake:**
  - vertical and horizontal **stick boards** dividing the wall into panels;
  - incised and chamfered work;
  - turned spindles and spool friezes;
  - **sunbursts and fans**;
  - jigsaw brackets;
  - attenuated brackets;
  - rosettes;
  - high-relief floral/fruit panels (Eastlake).
- **Queen Anne:**
  - **fish-scale (imbricated) shingles** in bands or fields between floors and in gables (1550 Page);
  - spindlework friezes in porches;
  - turned posts and colonnettes;
  - carved swags, wreaths and garlands;
  - sunbursts in the gable;
  - finials;
  - balcony and roofline balustrades (Liberty #5);
  - leaded or coloured glass.
- **Edwardian:** egg-and-dart, dentils, modillions, Ionic box columns, occasional garlands, rusticated basement. Much plainer.

**H.**

**R25. Wall material.** Redwood **horizontal rustic (channel/drop) siding**, with an exposure of about 12-15 cm and a visible shadow line, on almost all fronts (Liberty: "Rustic siding covers most buildings"). Queen Annes mix siding with shingle bands. Masonry fronts are rare for this type. Common later alterations:
- stucco or asbestos shingle re-cladding (1920s-1960s), which strips the ornament;
- garage doors;
- Edwardian basements in concrete block.

**H.**

**R26. Paint.**
- **Period, c.1870s-1880s Italianate:** light stone-imitating colours (cream, buff, grey, white), with trim close in tone, to read as masonry.
- **1880s-1890s Stick and Queen Anne:** darker "earth" palettes (olive, ochre, Indian red, umber) with 2-3 contrasting colours picking out structure.
- The **multicolour "painted lady" schemes** of 4-7 saturated colours, gilt and contrasting panel fields are a **1960s-70s and later revival**; the term was popularised by a 1978 book. Many fronts were painted battleship grey or white mid-century. Evidence for today's look: Steiner, Stick 2023, Walter and 1550 Page are modern revival schemes; HABS 1960s-70s photos show plainer finishes.
- Generator: choose period-plausible (2-3 colours, light for Italianate) or modern "painted lady" as an explicit mode.

**M** (period colours are from general knowledge and not verified in a source here).

### H. Variation

**R27. With date.**

| Period | Style | Front |
|---|---|---|
| 1860s-early 1870s | Flat-front Italianate | 3 openings, pediments, no bay |
| 1870s-mid 1880s | Bay Italianate | Slanted 2-storey bay, arched sash, consoles |
| 1880s | Stick | Square bay, flat heads, sticks, sunbursts, gablet on the false front, recessed porch |
| 1890s | Queen Anne | Gable front, mixed bays, shingles, arched porches, turrets on corners |
| 1900s-1915 | Edwardian / Classical Revival | Flats with 2-3 doors, curved or angled bays rising to a flat cornice, egg-and-dart |

There is much overlap and hybridisation, for example Queen Anne porticoes on Stick houses (Liberty #1). **H.**

**R28. With width and wealth.**
- **Narrower or cheaper:** flat front, or a 1-storey bay; a hood on consoles instead of a portico; fewer brackets; a 1-storey cottage.
- **Standard 25 ft:** 1 bay + entry column.
- **Wider or richer:** 2 bays + central portico, quoins, a third storey, a tower or belvedere (Westerfeld), side yards, Corinthian columns.
- Builder-developer rows (TREA, Kavanaugh on Steiner) **repeat one design or mirror it**, with small variations in ornament. Owner-built streets (Liberty) have no repeats. **H.**

**R29. On hills.** Each house is level and steps with the slope. Cornice and floor lines step house by house, and stoop riser counts change along the row (Noe Valley; Waller). The basement is taller on the downhill side. **H.**

**R30. Side walls.** Between row houses there is a party wall or a gap of 0-1 m. Side walls are plain siding, without ornament. Light-well notches or a "notchback" sit partway back (Liberty #25). A false front's flat parapet profile and the gable roof behind it show only where the neighbour is lower. **M.**

### I. What a generator is likely to get wrong
1. **Symmetry on a 25 ft front.** It should be bay + entry column. Symmetry is only for double-width houses.
2. **Bays from the ground up.** The bay starts at the parlour floor. Below it is basement wall, or a later garage.
3. **Equal bay faces.** The centre face is wider. On square bays the returns are very narrow, and the front often carries 2 windows.
4. **Wrong bay shape for the style.** Slanted = Italianate, square = Stick. Don't mix them on one Italianate house.
5. **A gable on an Italianate**, or a false front with a turret on every Queen Anne. Turrets are for corners and big houses.
6. **A weak or missing cornice.** The bracketed cornice band is about 10-12% of facade height and is the dominant horizontal. It wraps round the bay.
7. **Small windows.** Parlour windows are about 1 × 2.8-3 m, nearly floor to ceiling.
8. **Front door at grade.** It is at the parlour level, up 10-14 steps. The stoop is straight and starts at the sidewalk.
9. **Ornamented side walls**, or ornament spread evenly. Ornament concentrates on the bay, the hoods, the cornice and the entry.
10. **Painted-lady colours as "period".**
11. **Too few storeys in the overall height**, or squat proportions. The front is about 1.6-1.9 × width tall to the cornice.
12. **One door on flats.** Flats show one door per unit.

## 3. Open questions
1. **Measured storey heights.** I found no HABS measured drawing of a standard 25 ft row house. My heights are photo-scaled (±15%). A HABS measured set or a building-permit drawing would fix parlour and upper floor-to-floor heights and the cornice band height.
2. **27.5 ft vs 25 ft lots.** The 27.5 × 137.5 ft figure for Western Addition lots is unverified. Liberty (Mission) is documented at 25 × about 115 ft. What is the distribution by district?
3. **Bay side angle.** Is it 45° (half-octagon) everywhere? Some slanted bays look closer to 30-35° off the facade plane in photos. Plan drawings are needed.
4. **Bay projection over the property line.** Did 1870s-90s ordinances allow bays over the sidewalk (as the modern Planning Code §136 does, with limits of about 2-3 ft)? This matters for zero-setback rows.
5. **Original paint.** Period colour evidence (paint analysis, 1880s trade literature) was not checked here.
6. **Basement windows.** What did original raised basements look like under the bay before garages? Panelled solid, or small windows? Only 2119 Bush (arched windows) and Liberty #22 (porthole windows) give direct evidence.
7. **Frequency data.** What share of surviving houses are flat-front, bay Italianate, Stick or Queen Anne? Liberty gives about 1/3 Italianate, 7 Stick and 6 Queen Anne out of about 48. A citywide count would calibrate generator weights.
8. **Queen Anne gable pitch.** It is estimated from oblique photos (50-60°). An elevation drawing would confirm it.
