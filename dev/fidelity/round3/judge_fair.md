# Round 3 blind fidelity review: judge A (fair)

Judged from the 16 images in `images/` only. I read no code, docs, key or earlier report. Identifications come from the drawings and from my own knowledge of the reference buildings.

**Scale.** Plausibility (P): could this exist in that city? Recognisability (R): would an informed viewer name the city and type unprompted? Both 1 to 5.
**Verdict.** *pass*: an architectural historian would accept it as a schematic elevation of a real type. *pass-at-a-glance*: the type and city read immediately, but an expert spots errors on a second look. *fail*: wrong or unreadable.

## Errors that recur across a family

**Amsterdam (A and B)**
- Every window is drawn as a uniform dark-blue slab with a faint cross. A 17th–18th c. canal house has white-painted wooden frames and small-pane sashes (later 6-over-6 or 2-over-2). The white frame against dark brick is the most recognisable Amsterdam feature after the gable, and it is missing here: the frames take the wall colour.
- There are no shutters, no fanlight with tracery over the bel-étage door, and no carved door surround. Door frames on these houses are usually the richest element of the front.
- The stoop rises parallel to the facade as a single flight. That happens, but the classic Amsterdam *stoep* is a short perpendicular or two-sided flight with an iron *stoephek*. It reads more like a Brooklyn stoop.
- Wall anchors (*muurankers*) are present, which is a good detail. They are drawn as thin bars, not the decorative S or fleur shapes.
- The ground floor sits on a pale rendered plinth with a basement door under the stoop. That is correct.

**Venetian Gothic (C)**
- The hipped roof sits too high and is too prominent. Grand Canal palaces show at most a sliver of roof behind a dentilled cornice or a low parapet.
- The water gate rises straight from the ground line. On a canal front it would sit on an Istrian-stone base at the water, sometimes with steps. Painting the green band as a lawn, rather than water or a *fondamenta*, weakens the reading.
- The side windows are single lights (*monofore*). Larger palaces more often pair them, or frame the corners with *paraste* and stone quoins.
- Correct details: the tripartite plan; the central *polifora* stacked on the piani nobili; the dentil (*dente di sega*) string courses; the Istrian-stone frames around stucco walls; the funnel chimneys (*camini a imbuto*); and the stone-faced ground floor.

**Veneto-Byzantine (D)**
- The upper loggias have turned balusters. These are a Renaissance element. 12th–13th c. fronts used solid marble parapet slabs (*plutei*) or none at all. This is the error an expert would spot first.
- The stilted round arches with *patere* (roundels) and *formelle* (square panels) in the frieze are right, but the arches lack the extradosed archivolt (the thin raised moulding that springs from the abacus).
- There is no crowning crenellation (*merli*), and no corner turrets (*torreselle*) on the long versions. The Fondaco dei Turchi has both, though both are partly restorations.
- The capitals are blank blocks. That is acceptable at this scale.

## Per image

**img00: Amsterdam neck-gable house, c. 1650–1700.** Comparable to the neck gables on Herengracht and Keizersgracht, and Prinsengracht's narrow 2-bay houses. The claw pieces (*klauwstukken*), pedimented neck and hoist beam are right. Errors: only 2 bays at this height looks thin; 3 is the norm. The dark-grey brick reads as a modern painted front. The window issue above applies. The neck sits directly on the body with no cornice or band.

**img01: Venetian Gothic palazzo, mid-14th c.** Comparable to Palazzo Ariani's simpler neighbours, or the small Gothic houses on the Rio della Misericordia. The plain pointed (lancet-like) arches are earlier than the ogee. They are plausible, but less characteristic of Venice than the ogee. The quadrifora with balcony and the funnel chimney are right. Errors: the roof is too prominent; the water-gate base is missing.

**img02: Venetian Gothic palazzo, c. 1430–60.** Comparable to Palazzo Bernardo and Palazzo Giustinian. The 5-light ogee polifore stack correctly. The side windows with iron balconettes and the mezzanine windows on the ground floor are good. Errors: the upper polifora has no balcony while the lower one does. That reverses the usual hierarchy, though some cases exist. The cornice is weak.

**img03: Venetian Gothic palazzo, c. 1450.** Comparable to Palazzo Pisani Moretta. The two piani nobili with 6-light polifore and the low attic mezzanine are right. Errors: the ground-floor side windows are round-headed, which is a Renaissance mix and odd next to a Gothic water gate. The polifore have no tracery, which is unusual for a palace this size.

**img04: Amsterdam cornice-front house, c. 1750–1850, rendered.** Comparable to the 19th c. *lijstgevels* on Herengracht and Keizersgracht. The bracketed cornice and the dormer with hoist beam are right. Errors: a full white render is more The Hague than Amsterdam. The 4 bays all have the same windows, but a merchant house would stress the door bay. A cornice house usually also has an attic balustrade or a crest over the dormer. Without the dormer it could pass for a New York rowhouse.

**img05: as img04, red brick.** The brick suits Amsterdam better. The same window and stoop issues apply, and the New York reading is stronger here.

**img06: Venetian Gothic palazzo, c. 1450–70.** Clearly modelled on Ca' Foscari, or the Palazzi Giustinian next door: the upper polifora has quatrefoil tracery and the lower is plain, exactly as at Ca' Foscari. This is the best image in the set. Errors (minor): the roof is visible; the side windows lack balconies on the tracery floor; the quatrefoils float with no enclosing frame.

**img07: Amsterdam cornice-front house, 3 bays, cream render.** Its proportions are the best of family B. It has the same window and stoop issues.

**img08: Venetian Gothic house, tall and narrow, c. 1400.** Comparable to Palazzo Contarini Fasan's proportions, and the red-stucco Gothic houses in Cannaregio. The trifora and side windows with balconettes are good. Errors: plain pointed arches, as in 01; the roof is visible; the attic windows are grouped in a way that looks Renaissance.

**img09: Veneto-Byzantine casa-fondaco, 12th–13th c.** Comparable to Ca' da Mosto (original 2-storey state) and Ca' Farsetti/Loredan. The ground arcade, the patere-and-formelle frieze and the stilted upper arches are right. Errors: the balustrades (see the family notes above); the archivolts are missing; the wings are only slightly differentiated.

**img10: Amsterdam neck-gable house, 3 bays.** This is the best of family A. It has the correct proportions, brick colour and claw pieces. The window treatment is the only real error.

**img11: Veneto-Byzantine, compact.** It reads like a smaller Ca' Loredan. The family errors apply. The wide central arch flanked by narrower ones is good.

**img12: Veneto-Byzantine fondaco, long front.** It immediately suggests the Fondaco dei Turchi: a long ground arcade under a longer upper loggia. Errors: the corner turrets and crenellation are missing; the balusters.

**img13: Amsterdam neck gable, 3 bays.** It is the same house as 10 with the door bay on the left. Same verdict.

**img14: Veneto-Byzantine with tall portico columns.** The slender, tall ground columns resemble the Fondaco dei Turchi's ground portico. The proportions are good. The family errors apply.

**img15: Venetian florid Gothic (*gotico fiorito*), c. 1440–70.** The ogee arches with fleuron finials, the pink stucco with Istrian-stone dressings, and the ogee water gate with finial are textbook features. Comparable to Palazzo Bernardo and Ca' Contarini-Fasan. Errors (minor): the roof is visible; the side windows have no balconettes.

## Where to improve first
1. Amsterdam windows: give them white frames, small-pane sashes and a fanlight over the door. This is the cheapest fix with the biggest payoff.
2. Veneto-Byzantine: replace the balusters with solid plutei and add extradosed archivolts.
3. Venice: lower or hide the roof, and draw water (with a stone base course) instead of a lawn.
