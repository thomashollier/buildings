# Round 3: sceptical blind judge (B)

Judge B looked only at `dev/fidelity/round3/images/img00.png` … `img15.png`. No code, docs, key or earlier reports were read. Reference buildings are named from knowledge of the real stock; no measurements were taken.

Scores run 1 to 5. **Plausibility** asks whether this could be a real building of that type. **Recognisability** asks whether an expert would name the city and period without a caption. Verdicts:

- **pass**: survives a close look by a specialist.
- **pass-at-a-glance**: right city and type at thumbnail size, but a specialist finds giveaways within seconds.
- **fail**: wrong or muddled type, or the giveaways dominate.

The brief was to hunt for giveaways, so the verdicts are deliberately harsh.

## Giveaways common to the whole set

These are what a sceptic spots first, before any single building.

1. **Template reuse is obvious side by side.** 04, 05 and 07 are the same cornice, dormer, stoop and basement assembly with only the colour or bay count changed. 10 and 13 are mirror images. 02, 03 and 06 share one roof, the same chimneys and the same plinth, as do 01, 08 and 15. Real streets vary cornice profiles, floor heights and dormer types from house to house.
2. **Grass in front of everything.** Venetian water fronts should stand on a canal with water steps and a fondamenta edge. Amsterdam houses should stand on a brick quay with a stoep. The green ground plane removes the single strongest context cue.
3. **Glazing is wrong for the period everywhere.** The Amsterdam windows are big plate-glass rectangles split by a faint cross. A house from 1700 to 1780 should have small-paned sashes with 12 to 24 lights, or at least 6-over-6. The Venetian windows show no leaded or rondelle glazing and no wooden scuri (shutters).
4. **The Venetian roofs are too present.** Each shows a steep red hip roof with a deep bracketed eave. Real canal palazzi read as a flat block topped by a dentilled stone cornice, with a low roof that is barely visible from the water. The funnel chimneys (camini a imbuto), on the other hand, are a correct and welcome Venetian cue.
5. **No material texture or relief.** None of the brick fronts has relieving arches (ontlastingsbogen) over its windows. There are no rusticated or Istrian-stone quoins, and in the Venetian set no marble patere or crosses beyond a few roundels. In flat colour everything reads as stucco.

## Per-image notes

### img00: Amsterdam neck gable, 2 bays, dark brick
- **Right:** the neck gable has claw pieces (klauwstukken) and a crowning pediment, plus a loft door with a hoist beam (hijsbalk). The stoop has an iron railing, with the basement (souterrain) door under it. Wall anchors appear as small bars, and floor heights diminish upward.
- **Errors:**
  - A two-bay front with a full neck gable is unusual. Two-bay houses more often carry spout or bell gables, while the neck gable belongs on three-bay fronts.
  - The claw pieces are bare quarter circles with a pair of discs. Real klauwstukken are carved sandstone scrolls and volutes, often with fruit or dolphins.
  - The gable stands straight on the brick with no transition, sill moulding or cornice band.
  - The ground-floor left window runs almost down to the stoop landing, which is an odd proportion. The plain cream basement band looks like render rather than the hardstone (Belgian bluestone) it should be.
- **Scores:** plausibility 2.5, recognisability 4. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

### img01: Venetian Gothic, small, cream
- **Right:**
  - Tripartite front with a central quadrifora (four-light window) on both upper floors, and flanking single lights.
  - Arched water portal and dentilled string courses.
  - Funnel chimney.
- **Errors:**
  - The arches are plain lancets. Venetian Gothic after about 1350 is identified by the ogee (inflected) arch with a fleuron tip, and these could pass for generic Gothic Revival.
  - The quadrifora capitals are blocks with no foliate carving.
  - The single lights have no balconies, although Venetian single windows usually carry small stone balconies.
  - The ground-floor windows are stark rectangles with no stone frame.
  - The roof, as noted above.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3, recognisability 4. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

### img02: Venetian Gothic, large, ochre, one piano nobile
- **Right:**
  - Ogee single lights and a central pentafora (five lights).
  - Paired single windows at each end, which is the correct canonical Venetian rhythm.
  - Ground floor plus a mezzanine of small square windows, which is correct for a fondaco-derived palazzo.
- **Errors:**
  - The upper pentafora has no balcony while the lower one has a full balustrade, which inverts what is usual. Both floors normally carry a balcony, with the piano nobile the richer one.
  - The single-light balconies are iron grilles. Stone balusters would be expected, and iron reads as a 19th-century addition.
  - The polifora has no tracery and no framing rope moulding.
  - The ground-floor portal is pointed rather than ogee, which is inconsistent with the ogee windows above.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3, recognisability 4. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

### img03: Venetian Gothic, two equal piani nobili
- **Right:** the two-piano-nobile scheme, as at Pisani Moretta. Hexafore with stone balconies and single lights with balconettes. An ogee land or water portal. An attic of small windows grouped over the polifora.
- **Errors:**
  - The two noble floors are identical. Real examples differentiate them, usually with quatrefoil tracery on the upper one, and here neither has tracery.
  - The ground-floor windows are round-headed, which breaks the ogee vocabulary used everywhere else on the front.
  - The attic windows are too regular, and the six grouped attic lights look mechanically centred.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3.5, recognisability 4. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

### img04: Amsterdam cornice house, 4 bays, white stucco
- **Right:** the lijstgevel with bracketed cornice and a central dormer carrying a pediment, loft door and hoist beam. Stoop, basement and wall anchors. Floor heights diminish.
- **Errors:**
  - The cornice is a plain slab on bare consoles. Real lijstgevels have a frieze with festoons or garlands and a profiled cornice, and Louis XIV/XV examples often have a raised central crest (kuif).
  - The window architraves are not expressed, and there is no ground-floor plinth in hardstone.
  - The door is pushed to the very edge of a four-bay front without any emphasis such as a frontispiece, fanlight or door surround.
  - This is the same template as 05 and 07.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3, recognisability 3. Without the hoist dormer this could be Copenhagen or Hamburg. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

### img06: Venetian Gothic, quatrefoil tracery
- **Right:** this is the best of the Venetian set. The upper hexafora carries quatrefoils above the arches, in the late-Gothic Ca' Foscari and Giustinian manner, while the lower polifora is plain. That hierarchy is correct. The rest of the vocabulary is consistent: ogee lights, ogee portal with fleuron, and an attic band.
- **Errors:**
  - The quatrefoils are isolated discs sitting on the arches. The real thing is a continuous interlaced tracery screen in which the quatrefoils grow out of the ogee arches and share stone between them.
  - The polifore lack the framing rope or dentil border.
  - The capitals are plain.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3.5, recognisability 4.5. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance, close to a pass if it were set on water.

### img09: Veneto-Byzantine casa-fondaco
- **Right:** a ground-floor portico of round arches on columns, opening to the water. A continuous upper arcade with patere (roundels) and formelle (square panels) in a band, which is good and recognisable. Paired lights at the ends.
- **Errors:**
  - The arches are pure semicircles. Veneto-Byzantine arches are stilted, with the extrados often drawn up into a slight horseshoe or point, as at Ca' da Mosto and Ca' Loredan.
  - The ground portico has only three arches. A real portico runs as a continuous colonnade across the centre.
  - There are no marble revetment, crosses or inlaid discs.
  - The front ends in a flat parapet with no crenellation and none of the later added storeys. A survivor this pure, with only two storeys, essentially does not exist.
- **Scores:** plausibility 3, recognisability 3.5. **Verdict:** pass-at-a-glance.

## What would move the set from "glance" to "pass"

1. **Put the buildings in context.** Use water and steps for Venice and a brick quay for Amsterdam. This is the cheapest and biggest win.
2. **Period glazing.** Amsterdam needs small-paned sashes. Venice needs scuri or leaded panes.
3. **Venetian skyline.** Use a dentilled stone cornice with the roof nearly hidden.
4. **Vary within type.** Use more than one cornice profile, claw-piece design and dormer, and stop the exact mirroring.
5. **Carve the key details.** Ogee everywhere in late-Gothic fronts, interlaced tracery rather than discs, stilted Byzantine arches, and real capitals.
6. **Brick relief.** Add relieving arches over the Amsterdam windows and hardstone plinths.
