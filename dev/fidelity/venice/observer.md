# Venetian palazzo canal facade (Veneto-Byzantine c.1150-1300, Gothic c.1300-1500): rules from photographs

Independent observer report. I worked only from photographs (Wikimedia Commons, mostly straight-on Grand Canal shots by Didier Descouens, Moonik and others) and a few web texts. I did not look at any existing model.

Dimensions are estimated from photos. The scale references were:
- a standing adult (1.7 m), in the campo photos of Palazzo Soranzo and Palazzo Fortuny and the Ca' d'Oro fondamenta;
- a measured portego of 7.5 m wide x 5.8 m high at Ca' Sagredo, from the hotel's published room data (the polifora spans the portego, so this calibrates the polifora width);
- a polifora light taken as about 0.9-1.1 m wide;
- a wing window taken as about 1.1-1.3 m wide.

Pixel ratios were measured on 960 px-wide images. Perspective from the water foreshortens upper storeys, so storey ratios carry about ±10 %.

## 2. Rules

Confidence: **H** = seen consistently in at least 10 facades; **M** = consistent in 4-9 facades, or estimated through scale; **L** = few examples, or inferred.

### A. Frontage and the tripartite division

**R1. Frontage width.** Gothic palazzi fall into three size classes.

| Class | Width | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Palazzetto | 6-12 m | Contarini Fasan about 6 m; Molin red about 11 m; Benzon Foscolo about 12 m |
| Normal palazzo | 14-21 m (typical) | Loredan Ambasciatore about 16-17 m; Barbaro about 17 m; Cavalli-Franchetti about 19 m; Pisani Moretta about 19-21 m; Mastelli about 14 m |
| Grand palazzo | 23-28 m | Ca' d'Oro about 25 m; Ca' Foscari about 25-28 m |

Doubled palazzi run 35-50 m (Giustinian twins, Soranzo). Byzantine fondaco-type fronts run 40-50 m (Fondaco dei Turchi).
- Typical 18 m; range 6-50 m.
- Confidence: M for the absolute values (scale estimates), H for the classes.

**R2. Tripartite division.** On normal and grand palazzi the facade is a central zone (the portego, expressed by the polifora) between two solid wings.
- The centre (the polifora, including its end piers) takes **30-46 % of the width; median about 36 %**. Measured shares: Cavalli 33 %, Loredan Ambasciatore 33 %, Barbaro 34 %, Tiepolo Passi 35 %, Pisani Moretta 39 %, Ca' Foscari 42 %, Mastelli 46 % (with its flanking single lights).
- Each wing is about 27-35 %.
- The polifora's absolute width tracks the portego width: **6-9 m**. Sagredo's 8-light polifora spans a 7.5 m portego.
- So on wider facades the centre share shrinks, and the extra width goes into the wings as more windows. It does not go into a wider polifora.
- Confidence: H.

**R3. Windows per wing, per floor.** The number of single windows grows with width:

| Facade width | Windows per wing |
|---|---|
| Under 12 m | 0-1 |
| 14-21 m | 2 (the normal case) |
| Over 23 m | 2-3 (Ca' Foscari 2 per wing on the main floors, 3 at ground level) |

- Wing windows are spaced fairly evenly. The wing window nearest the polifora sits about 1-1.5 m from the polifora's end pier.
- The outer window sits about 1-1.5 m in from the corner quoin. Exception: corner windows (R18).
- Confidence: H.

**R4. Symmetry.** About 60 % of the Gothic facades I sampled are symmetric about the polifora axis: Pisani Moretta, Foscari, Cavalli, Barbaro, Loredan Ambasciatore, Tiepolo Passi, Mastelli, Moro.

About 40 % are asymmetric, in four recurring layouts:
- **(a) Polifora shifted towards one end.** One wing has 1 window, the other 2-3: Sagredo (1 wing window on one side, 3 on the other), Bernardo, Giustinian Pesaro, the Ca' da Mosto neighbour, Molin red.
- **(b) "L" or Ca' d'Oro type.** The polifora sits near one edge and a broad blank or sparse wing fills the other side. At Ca' d'Oro the right wing is about 40 % of the width with only 1-2 windows per floor.
- **(c) Polifora at the very corner.** Palazzo Ariani: a 6-light polifora at the left edge, the rest of the facade a wing with paired single windows.
- **(d) Doubled palazzo.** Two complete tripartite units side by side, often mirrored, each with its own polifora and water gate: Giustinian, Soranzo.
- The polifore of different storeys usually stay on the same vertical axis, even when the facade is asymmetric.
- Confidence: H for the layouts, M for the 60/40 split.

**R5. Narrow facades (6-12 m) are bipartite or mono.** Either the polifora fills nearly the whole width and the wings shrink to quoins (Contarini Fasan: a 3-light on the first floor, 2 single windows on the second), or the facade is polifora plus one wing (Molin red: a 4-light plus 1-2 windows).
- Confidence: M.

### B. Storeys and storey heights

**R6. Storey count, Gothic.** A normal palazzo has **water storey + 2 piani nobili + a lower top floor or attic**: 3 full storeys plus an attic/top storey.
- Smaller houses: water storey, 1 piano nobile, top floor.
- Grand houses: water storey, PN1, PN2, full third floor (Ca' Foscari is 4 storeys).
- Total height from the water to the cornice: about **15-22 m**. The facade is roughly as tall as it is wide on 16-20 m fronts (Loredan Ambasciatore height:width about 0.9; Pisani Moretta about 0.95; Ca' d'Oro about 0.8).
- Confidence: H for the counts, M for the metres.

**R7. Storey count, Veneto-Byzantine.** The original Byzantine palazzo had only **2 storeys**: a ground arcade/portico and one piano nobile arcade. Floors 3-4 on Loredan, Farsetti, da Mosto and Businello are 16th-18th c. additions in a different style (rectangular or round-arched Renaissance windows, plain plaster).
- A generator that builds a "Byzantine" facade 4 storeys high in one style is wrong.
- Confidence: H.

**R8. Storey heights.** Estimated from photos, the storeys are nearly equal; the top storey is clearly lower.

| Storey | Height | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Water storey (incl. wing mezzanine) | 4.5-6 m | Pisani Moretta about 5.6; Fortuny about 5.5; Cavalli about 5.5 |
| Piano nobile 1 | 4.5-6.5 m | Ca' d'Oro about 6.8 (exceptional); Fortuny about 6.0; Pisani Moretta about 5.2 |
| Piano nobile 2 | 4.2-6 m | usually equal to PN1, or 5-10 % less |
| Top floor / attic | 2.8-4 m | about 55-70 % of a piano nobile; often only small windows in a frieze band |

- **Which floor is tallest:** usually PN1, but often within 5 % of PN2 and of the water storey.
- At Ca' Foscari PN2 is visually tallest, because a sculpted frieze band sits above its polifora.
- The water storey is **not** a low basement. It is as tall as a piano nobile, because it contains the double-height androne and, in the wings, a mezzanine.
- Ceiling reference: Ca' Sagredo portego is 5.8 m high.
- Confidence: M.

**R9. Mezzanines (ammezzati).** These sit **in the wings only, inside the water storey**. They show as two tiers of small windows in each wing:
- a lower tier: barred, round-headed or square, about 0.7-0.9 m wide x 1.0-1.3 m tall;
- an upper tier: rectangular, about 0.8-1.0 x 1.2-1.6 m, set just under the PN1 string course.

Evidence: Pisani Moretta, Cavalli, Barbaro, Loredan Ambasciatore, Ca' Foscari (one tier).
- The centre bay (androne) has no mezzanine. It holds the water gate, sometimes flanked by narrow lights.
- Occasionally a second mezzanine sits in the wings between PN1 and PN2 (Zorzi Bon, Fortuny). This is uncommon.
- Confidence: H for wings-only, M for the sizes.

**R10. Size and wealth.** Richer houses get:
- taller storeys rather than more of them;
- a second full polifora on PN2 (it gets an equally large polifora, not a smaller one);
- tracery richness;
- Istrian stone panel frames around every wing window;
- for a few, stone veneer (Ca' d'Oro).

Poorer or smaller houses get:
- a polifora on PN1 only (3-5 lights);
- a smaller PN2 window group (e.g. a 4- or 5-light without tracery, or paired single windows);
- plain stone frames and plastered brick.

Confidence: M.

### C. The polifora (central multi-light window)

**R11. Number of lights.** Range 3-8; **typical 4-6**.
- Even counts are as common as odd. With an even count, the central axis falls on a colonnette, and the water gate below still sits on the axis.

| Lights | Examples |
|---|---|
| 3 (trifora) | Contarini Fasan, Benzon Foscolo, Minotto |
| 4 | Barbaro, Loredan Ambasciatore, Mastelli PN2, Molin red, Pisani's neighbours |
| 5 | Agnusdio, Tiepolo Passi, Cavalli, Giustinian Pesaro, da Mosto neighbour |
| 6 | Pisani Moretta (both floors), Bernardo, Ariani, Ca' d'Oro |
| 7-8 | Fortuny (about 7), Sagredo PN1 (8), Ca' Foscari (8) |

- The PN1 and PN2 polifore usually have the **same number of lights and the same axis**. Sometimes PN2 has fewer (Ca' Foscari top floor 4-5) or PN2 is a narrower group.
- Confidence: H.

**R12. Light and colonnette dimensions.**

| Element | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Light (clear width) | 0.85-1.1 m (typ. 0.95) | |
| Colonnette shaft diameter | 0.2-0.3 m | about **1:4 to 1:5 of the light width** |
| Capital | about 0.35-0.4 m wide x 0.3-0.4 m tall | carved foliage |
| End supports | pilaster half-piers, 0.35-0.6 m | not colonnettes; often fluted or roped |
| Light height, sill to apex | 2.6-3.6 m | about **3-3.5x the width** |
| Springing line | about 65-72 % of the height | |
| Sill | stands on the balcony floor (the light is a door) | |

- Measured at Agnusdio: light 150 px, column 35 px. At Pisani Moretta: light 42 px, column 12 px.
- Confidence: M.

**R13. Light head form, by date** (Ruskin's "orders", simplified).

| Period | Head form | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Byzantine, to c.1250 | round stilted arch, sometimes with a small raised point on the extrados (1st order) | Fondaco, Loredan, da Mosto |
| c.1250-1320 | pointed stilted arch or plain lancet, often trilobed (2nd-3rd order) | Falier, Fortuny top floor |
| c.1320-1420 | cusped ogee: trefoil intrados, ogee extrados ending in a small finial (4th-5th order); **this is the default Venetian Gothic light** | Agnusdio, Tiepolo Passi, Mastelli, Barbaro, most wing windows |
| c.1420-1480 | same ogee lights plus a **tracery band**: interlaced arcs carrying a row of quatrefoils in circles (one per column), filling a rectangle above the lights about 0.9-1.3 m tall (about one light width) | Ca' Foscari, Pisani Moretta (both floors), Cavalli PN1, Bernardo PN2, Loredan Ambasciatore PN1, Giustinian, Molin red, Ca' d'Oro (two superimposed tracery rows on PN1) |
| c.1470-1500 | round-headed lights return on Gothic massing (transitional) | Moro San Barnaba, Businello upper floors |

- The tracery band usually appears on **only one of the two polifore**, the more important floor. That can be PN1 (Cavalli, Loredan Ambasciatore) or PN2 (Bernardo, Ca' Foscari, Fortuny). The other floor has plain cusped ogees.
- Pisani Moretta, with both floors traceried, is the rich exception.
- Confidence: H for the forms, M for the dates.

**R14. Polifora framing.** The polifora is set in a **rectangular Istrian stone frame**: the end piers plus a top band with dentil (dentello) edging. The frame stands out against the brick or plaster.
- On florid examples a carved relief frieze or statue niches flank it (Ca' Foscari reliefs; Loredan Ambasciatore shield-bearer niches between the polifora and the wings).
- Confidence: H.

**R15. Polifora balcony.** A **continuous** stone balcony runs the full width of the polifora on every piano nobile. It rests on 4-8 stone corbels, projects about 0.4-0.7 m, and has a balustrade about 0.9-1.0 m high.
- The balustrade has small turned colonnettes (later baluster type) or older pierced panels (quatrefoil/wheel tracery at Contarini Fasan).
- Sometimes the balcony's ends turn out further to form small projecting balconies at the polifora ends (Fortuny, Cavalli).
- Top-floor polifore (Ca' d'Oro's upper loggia, Loredan Ambasciatore PN2) may be recessed loggias with no projecting balcony.
- Confidence: H.

### D. Single windows in the wings

**R16. Size.** Clear opening 1.0-1.3 m wide x 2.6-3.4 m tall (h:w about 2.5-3); the sill is at floor level (French-door type) on the piani nobili. Head forms:
- the **same head form as the polifora on that floor** (cusped ogee with a finial; 4th/5th order);
- on 15th-c. rich houses, a quatrefoil oculus above the head inside the frame (Cavalli, Ca' d'Oro, Bernardo);
- on the top floor: plain pointed, or rectangular (later alteration).

Confidence: H for forms, M for sizes.

**R17. Frame.** An Istrian stone architrave about 0.15-0.25 m wide follows the arch, with a carved finial (fleuron) at the apex.
- On richer houses the window is also set in a **rectangular stone-edged panel** (cornice a riquadro), about 0.3-0.4 m wider than the window on each side, running from the balcony to above the finial: Pisani Moretta, Cavalli, Ca' Foscari, Giustinian, Fortuny, Barbaro.
- Between the wing windows: small square stone panels (formelle) or round paterae (Barbaro, Cavalli, Loredan Ambasciatore).
- Confidence: H.

**R18. Wing balconies.** Individual balconies (poggioli) sit under **most piano nobile wing windows**: on 2-3 corbels, about the window width plus 0.2-0.3 m each side, projecting 0.35-0.5 m.
- PN1 wing windows almost always have them. PN2 often do.
- Top-floor and water-storey windows never do. Exception: later iron balconettes.
- Plain or poorer houses omit them on PN2 (Soranzo has small iron ones).
- Confidence: H.

**R19. Corner windows (finestre d'angolo).** In a minority (about 5-10 %), the outermost wing windows sit right at the corner: the corner is replaced by a colonnette and the balcony wraps or ends at the edge (Mastelli, Donà Giovannelli).
- Confidence: M.

### E. Water gates and land doors

**R20. Number and position.** Usually **one water gate, on the polifora axis**, opening into the androne.
- Variants: **two gates** side by side under the polifora (Pisani Moretta), at about the quarter points (Bernardo), or one per half of a doubled palazzo (Giustinian).
- Some facades have a secondary small gate in a wing (Barbaro: an older Gothic gate at the left corner).
- Rare: no gate on the main canal front. Contarini Fasan, about 6 m wide, has only windows at water level.
- Confidence: H.

**R21. Size and form.**
- Width **2.2-3.5 m** (typ. 2.6). Height to apex **3.8-5.5 m**.
- The arch springs about 2.6-3.5 m above the water.
- Gothic gates are **pointed (lancet) or ogee**, often with a carved tympanum or iron grille in the lunette (Cavalli, Loredan Ambasciatore).
- Byzantine gates are round stilted arches inside a ground arcade.
- The frame is Istrian stone with dentil or rope edging, and 1-3 steps descend into the water.
- Mooring poles (paline, often striped) stand 1-3 m in front, 2-6 per facade.
- Confidence: M.

**R22. Relation to the polifora.** The gate is centred under the polifora but is **much narrower**: about 1/3-1/2 of the polifora width.
- On even-numbered polifore the gate apex sits under the central colonnette.
- At Ca' Foscari and Mastelli the gate is flanked by 1-2 narrow pointed lights that pick up the polifora rhythm.
- Confidence: H.

**R23. Land doors.**
- The land door is normally on the opposite (calle/campo/courtyard) front: rectangular, about 1.4-2.3 m wide x 2.8-4 m high, in a stone frame, often with a pointed or ogee lunette or relief above.
- Ogee-arched courtyard portals are also common (Van Axel, Agnusdio, Ca' d'Oro).
- On canal fronts with a fondamenta, the land door replaces the water gate on the same axis.
- Confidence: M.

### F. Horizontal articulation, corners and roofline

**R24. String courses.** A thin Istrian stone band (0.12-0.25 m) runs at every floor level, usually at the balcony and sill line, often with dentil or sawtooth edging.
- On florid brick facades every storey is boxed by dentilled stone strips (Ca' Foscari, Giustinian, Bernardo, Cavalli).
- Confidence: H.

**R25. Corners.** One of three treatments, all in white Istrian stone:
- (a) a **dentil-edged vertical strip** framing the whole facade, about 0.3-0.5 m wide (Ca' Foscari, Bernardo, Cavalli, Barbaro);
- (b) **alternating long/short quoins** (Contarini Fasan, Giustinian, Loredan Ambasciatore);
- (c) a **twisted rope (torciglione) colonnette** at the arris, about 0.15-0.25 m diameter (Ca' d'Oro, Contarini Fasan, Fortuny, Mastelli).
- Rope mouldings also edge frames on rich facades.
- Classical pilasters are **not** Gothic; they mark Renaissance work (Bernardo Nani, Priuli Ruzzini).
- Confidence: H.

**R26. Cornice and eaves.** A projecting eaves cornice about 0.4-0.8 m deep, in one of three forms:
- a dentil band plus stone or wooden **corbels/modillions** at about 0.5-0.8 m spacing (Fortuny, Zorzi Bon, Mastelli, Cavalli);
- a Gothic corbel-table frieze with small arches;
- a plain moulded stone cornice.

Pisani Moretta has a deep attic frieze with pierced quatrefoil panels below the cornice.
- Confidence: H.

**R27. Crenellation.**
- Gothic palazzi normally have **none**. Ca' d'Oro is the exception: a crest of white spiky pinnacles with ball finials (originally gilded) above the cornice. The Doge's Palace has merlons too.
- Byzantine and early buildings had small **triangular or stepped "Islamic" merlons** with finials. The Fondaco dei Turchi's are a 19th-c. restoration based on this type, and old views show such merli on 13th-c. houses.
- Confidence: H for Gothic none; L/M for how common Byzantine merli were.

**R28. Corner towers (torreselle), Byzantine only.** On the grandest Veneto-Byzantine fronts (Fondaco dei Turchi; claimed for the Loredan type), the two end bays form slightly projecting tower blocks:
- about 1/6-1/5 of the width each;
- each **one storey higher** than the central arcade block;
- with their own smaller arched windows (4-5 per tower top) and merlons.
- Most Byzantine houses had no towers.
- Confidence: M for the Fondaco, L for the general rule.

**R29. Roof and chimneys.**
- Low hipped or pitched roof of coppi (pantile), about 20-30°, ridge parallel to the canal. Often a central dormer (abbaino) and/or a wooden altana (roof terrace on posts).
- **Chimneys are distinctive:** 1-4 tall stacks rising 2-4 m above the eaves, usually at the side walls or on the facade plane. Tops take one of three forms:
  - an **inverted cone / funnel (fumaiolo a imbuto)**, the most recognisable Venetian form (Van Axel, Sagredo, Giustinian Pesaro, Priuli);
  - a bell or castellated head;
  - a plain square shaft with a small hood.
- Confidence: H.

### G. Byzantine facade grammar (c.1150-1300)

**R30. The arcade facade.** The ground storey has a **water portico**: an open arcade of 3-7 round stilted arches on single marble columns (Ca' da Mosto 3; Loredan 5; Farsetti 5 plus closed side bays; Fondaco 10).
- Above it, the piano nobile carries a **continuous arcade** with about **twice as many, narrower arches**. It often extends across most of the width, with wing groups separated only by a wider pier: Farsetti and Loredan about 70-80 % of the width; da Mosto 7 central lights plus 1 each side.
- A continuous balcony runs under it.
- Confidence: H.

**R31. Byzantine proportions.**
- Arch rise about 0.5 x span, plus a stilt of 0.3-0.5 x span.
- Columns slender (height:diameter about 8-10), with Byzantine basket or leaf capitals.
- Piano nobile arches about 0.7-1.0 m wide; portico arches about 1.8-2.5 m.
- Spandrels and the frieze above carry **paterae (carved roundels) and formelle (rectangular relief panels)**, plus veneer of coloured marble.
- Confidence: M.

### H. Materials and colours

**R32. Istrian stone for all trim.** It covers polifore, frames, balconies, corbels, string courses, quoins, cornice, gates and the water-level plinth. Colour off-white #E8E4DA, weathering to grey #C9C5BA with dark streaks below projections.
- The **plinth** is a band of Istrian ashlar 0.5-1.5 m above water level, with a green-black algae band at the waterline (Moro, Cavalli, Fortuny).
- Confidence: H.

**R33. Wall finish. About half the Gothic examples show exposed brick; the other half are plastered.**

| Finish | Colour | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Exposed brick, warm buff to orange | about #B98C68 to #C9A27E, patchy | Ca' Foscari, Giustinian, Fortuny, Ariani, Zorzi Bon, Loredan Ambasciatore, Contarini Fasan |
| Plaster (intonaco/marmorino), salmon/rose | #D98F7E, #E3A898 | Pisani Moretta, Soranzo, Sagredo |
| Plaster, Venetian red | #9C3A2E | Molin red |
| Plaster, ochre | #D4A85A | Cavalli |
| Plaster, cream/white | #E9E2D2 | Barbaro, Benzon Foscolo, Moro |
| Stone veneer | | rare: Ca' d'Oro, white Istrian stone with red Verona marble panels |

- Plaster commonly flakes to show brick in the water storey.
- Some brick facades were originally rendered or painted with fictive brick; today's exposed brick is partly a 19th-20th c. look.
- Confidence: H for the palette, M for the originality question.

**R34. Joinery and glass.**
- Openings have wooden frames, dark brown or green. Original glass is round bullseye panes (rui) in lead (Fortuny, Ca' d'Oro).
- Green louvred shutters (persiane) are a later but ubiquitous addition, especially on rectangular top-floor windows.
- Confidence: M.

### I. Variation and things a generator would get wrong

**R35. Variation.**
- **By date:** round to pointed to cusped ogee to ogee with quatrefoil band to round again (R13). Byzantine fronts have 2 storeys and arcades; Gothic fronts have 3-4 storeys with polifora and wings. Corner rope mouldings and panel frames are 15th c.
- **By wealth:** tracery band, two equal polifore, panel frames, stone veneer, crest.
- **By width:** number of wing windows (R3) and bipartite layouts on narrow lots (R5). Polifora size stays roughly constant (6-9 m).

**R36. Likely generator errors.**

1. Centring a 3-light polifora on every facade. Real counts are 4-6, often even, and about 40 % of facades are asymmetric.
2. Making the polifora proportional to facade width. Its width is fixed by the portego (6-9 m); the wings absorb extra width.
3. A low water storey. It is as tall as a piano nobile.
4. Mezzanine windows across the whole facade. They belong in the wings only.
5. Giving the PN2 polifora fewer lights or a different axis than PN1. It is usually the same axis and count.
6. Giving every floor the same tracery. The quatrefoil band goes on one floor (sometimes both on the richest houses), never on the top floor or attic.
7. A water gate as wide as the polifora, or off-axis. It is about 1/3-1/2 of the polifora width and on the axis, except in the doubled/asymmetric types.
8. Balconies on the top floor or under every window. They go under the polifora (continuous) and the piano nobile wing windows (individual).
9. Crenellation on ordinary Gothic palazzi. Only Ca' d'Oro has it.
10. Classical pilasters or quoins on Gothic fronts. Use dentil strips or rope mouldings.
11. Plain chimneys. Venice's are tall, with funnel tops.
12. Byzantine palazzi built tall in one style. Originally 2 storeys, plus later different upper floors.
13. Uniform window spacing across the whole facade. Gothic facades are explicitly grouped: dense centre, sparse wings with wide blank brick or plaster between the groups.
14. A white or uniformly stone-coloured wall. Most walls are brick or coloured plaster, with white trim.

## 3. Open questions

1. **Absolute widths.** I found no measured survey (Trincanato, Maretto) online. Widths and storey heights are photo-scale estimates (±15 %). A surveyed elevation of Pisani Moretta, Ca' Foscari or Cavalli would firm up R1, R8, R12 and R21.
2. **Frequency statistics.** The 60/40 symmetric/asymmetric split and "half brick, half plaster" come from about 30 Gothic fronts, biased towards famous Grand Canal palazzi. Minor-canal anonymous houses are under-sampled (only Mastelli, Zorzi Bon, Ariani, Van Axel and the Rio Santi Apostoli houses).
3. **Byzantine merlons and torreselle.** How common were they before 1300? The evidence is mostly the restored Fondaco and old views such as de' Barbari 1500, which I did not inspect.
4. **Original wall finishes.** Was today's exposed brick originally plastered, painted with fictive brick, or bare? Facade frescoes (e.g. by Giorgione on the Fondaco dei Tedeschi) are lost.
5. **Which floor gets the tracery band.** It is PN1 in some and PN2 in others; I could not find a rule tied to date or interior plan.
6. **Top-floor windows.** Many top floors now have rectangular windows. How many were originally small pointed windows and how many are rebuilt is unclear from photos.
7. **Water gate sill level relative to the mean tide**, and step counts, are not measurable from these photos.
