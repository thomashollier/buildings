# Judge B: blind adversarial review of 16 generated facades

Reviewer stance: sceptical. I looked only at `img00.png` to `img15.png` and at photographs on the web. Pixel measurements come from the drawings: I read coordinates off the 600 px wide images and cropped details to check them. Where I give metres, the scale assumes the facade width stated in that section.

The 16 images fall into three families:

| Family | Images | What they are trying to be |
|---|---|---|
| A | 00, 06, 07, 10, 11, 14 | Amsterdam canal houses (grachtenpand): neck, bell, step and cornice gables |
| B | 02, 03, 05, 09, 13, 15 | Venetian Gothic palazzi (c. 1380-1480), with a central polifora and lateral ogee monofore |
| C | 01, 04, 08, 12 | Veneto-Byzantine "casa-fondaco" palazzi (12th-13th c.); 01 and 12 are clearly modelled on the Fondaco dei Turchi |

### Facts taken from these references

These are the facts the verdicts below rely on.

**Amsterdam stoops (R10, R13, R15, R17, R18)**
- A platform (*bordes*) sits directly in front of the door and is at least as wide as the door.
- The flights run parallel to the facade and descend sideways, and they always have an iron railing.
- Under the platform is a basement door or a grille. There is never a glazed window half-hidden behind the treads.

**Amsterdam gables**
- The hoisting hatch and the beam above it are always on the gable's axis. In R12, R15 and R17 the hatch is the single central opening at the top.
- In 3-bay houses, the window is about 0.65-0.75 of the bay width (R17: about 95 px window in a 125 px bay).
- Window glazing is sash work with small panes: 6/6, 8/8 or 9/9 in the 18th c., 2/2 in the 19th c.

**Venetian Gothic**
- On every palazzo I checked, the two piani nobili have the same number of polifora lights, stacked column over column: Pisani Moretta 6/6, Ca' Foscari 8/8, Giustinian 6/6. Only an attic polifora may have fewer lights (Ca' Foscari has 4 above 8), and its columns then stand over columns below.
- Arches spring from the capitals. The ogee is convex at its foot and concave towards a pointed tip, often with a finial.
- Balconies are stone (balusters on corbels): Pisani Moretta, Ca' Foscari, Giustinian. Iron railings appear only as later replacements (Palazzo Dolfin, R5).

**Veneto-Byzantine**
- The composition is tripartite: a central portico and loggia, flanked by solid wings with few openings (Ca' Loredan, Ca' Farsetti, Ca' da Mosto).
- Columns are smooth, free-standing marble shafts carrying stilted arches.
- Spandrels carry paterae and formelle (round and rectangular carved marble plaques).
- Parapets are stone balustrades.

## Family A: Amsterdam canal houses

**Common scale:** I take a 3-bay front as 7.5 m wide, so 1 px is about 0.018 m (img00). Storey heights then come out at 3.4 m for the bel-etage and 3.0-3.2 m for the floors above. That is plausible: real bel-etages are 3.5-4 m and upper floors about 3 m.

**Faults shared by all six:**

- **(a) The stoop is physically impossible.** The stair rises left to right across the door bay. Its top tread, at the door-sill height (y about 1050), lies *to the right of* the door (door x 155-218, top tread x 229-243 in img00). So the door hangs over the lower steps with nothing under it. The single flight also has no railing, and it runs across a glazed basement window. I checked this in a zoomed crop.
- **(b) The glazing reads as 2×2 or 2×3 panes.** Real pre-1850 sashes have 12-18 small panes. The frames are beige rather than the near-universal white or cream.
- **(c) The window head is the same in every storey and every gable type.** There are no relieving arches or keystones and no lintel variation.
- **(d) The cornice or gable sits directly on the top storey.** There is no frieze band or attic.

### img00: Amsterdam 3-bay house with a raised neck gable (halsgevel), c. 1640-1760
**Confidence:** high.

**Measured**
- The neck is 165 px wide on a 414 px facade (0.40). Real necks are 0.33-0.5. OK.
- The gable is 310 px of 1065 px total height (0.29). Real gables are 0.2-0.3. OK.
- Window to bay is 78/114 (0.68). OK.
- Bel-etage window to storey height is 140/185 (0.76). OK.

**Discrepancies**
- The stoop (a). Seen in R10, R13 and R15.
- The claw pieces (*klauwstukken*) are bare concave quarter-circles. Real ones are carved sandstone scrolls that bulge *outwards* (convex, foliate). The drawn outline makes the neck look like a funnel.
- The pediment has no sandstone dressing and no cornice returns, and there is no hoisting beam projecting under it. The dark stub may be meant as the beam end.
- The 2×2 glazing (b).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS.

**Top fixes**
1. Rebuild the stoop: a platform in front of the door, a sideways flight, a railing, and a basement door under the platform.
2. Make the claw pieces convex scrolls in a stone colour, and add a stone pediment with cornice.
3. Use small-pane sash glazing in white frames.

### img14: the same neck-gable type
**Confidence:** high.

The proportions are almost identical to img00 (neck 0.40 of width, gable 0.25 of height). It has the same stoop fault and the same funnel claw pieces. The two images could be one house drawn twice; a street of these would look cloned.

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. The fixes are the same as for img00.

### img06: 2-bay house with a bell gable (klokgevel), c. 1660-1790
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- **The hatch is off axis.** In the gable, a window sits on the left and the hoisting hatch on the right. The hoisting beam (the dark stub) is on the centreline, so it would lower goods past the hatch. In every bell or neck gable I checked (R12, R15, R17), the hatch is the single central top opening.
- **The crown is a full semicircle 227 px wide** (61% of the facade width). Real bell gables end in a small curl, a segmental cap or a pediment, typically 15-30% of the width (R12, R15, R17). Here the crown reads as a Baroque church top.
- **The bell sides start at full width.** That is legitimate (R17), but there are no ornamental scrolls at the foot. The bell is a bare silhouette.
- The stoop (a).

**Verdict:** FAILS. The off-axis hatch combined with the dome-like crown is wrong at first glance.

**Top fixes**
1. Put the hatch on the axis, with at most one window per side below it or none.
2. Replace the semicircle with a small ornamental crown.
3. Fix the stoop.

### img07: 3-bay cornice house (lijstgevel), c. 1750-1880
**Confidence:** high.

**Measured**
- The facade is 395 px wide.
- Storeys measure 205, 191, 179 and 163 px going up, which is the correct diminishing sequence.
- The top-floor windows are squatter (87 px tall against 128 px below), which is correct.

**Discrepancies**
- **The cornice** is a thin slab with spaced blocks, and it overhangs *past both party walls*. Real cornices (R13, R14, R18) are deep wooden entablatures with paired consoles. They stop at or just short of the party wall, and often have a central raised crest or a hoisting beam through the cornice.
- There is no attic and no dormer.
- The stoop (a).
- The glazing (b).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. This is the most believable of the six.

**Top fixes**
1. Fix the stoop.
2. Make the cornice a deeper console cornice that stops at the party walls.
3. Add small-pane sash glazing.

### img10: narrow 2-bay house with a step gable (trapgevel), c. 1600-1665
**Confidence:** high.

**Measured**
- There are 8 steps per side. Each riser is about 55 px and each tread about 13 px.
- The gable outline (outer step corners) runs from (123,550) to (232,82), a pitch of **about 77°**.
- The gable height to the width ratio is 470/354 = 1.33.

**Discrepancies**
- **The pitch is too steep.** Amsterdam roofs, and so step gables, are about 50-62°, with gable height about 0.8-1.0 × width (R19, R11). At 77° the gable is a spire, not a roof end. This is my strongest single objection to this image. Confidence: medium-high.
- **The treads are too narrow.** At 13 px (about 0.22 m at a 6 m width) the treads are too slim. Real steps are 1-2 brick lengths deep and capped with sandstone.
- **Period mix.** These are 17th-c. gables, but the windows are 18th/19th-c. flush sash windows. There are no relieving arches, no sandstone banding and no shutters (R11, R19). Sash replacement in old houses is real, so this is a flaw, not fatal.
- The stoop (a).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. It is borderline.

**Top fixes**
1. Lower the pitch to about 55-60° and use fewer, deeper steps (5-7 per side).
2. Add brick relieving arches over the windows and shutters on the lower lights, or else commit to an 18th-c. remodelled house.
3. Fix the stoop.

### img11: 3-bay house with a step gable
**Confidence:** high.

The pitch is **about 74°** ((107,475) to (226,70)). Gable height to width is 405/388 = 1.04. There are 8 steps per side. The faults are the same as img10, slightly milder.

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. The fixes are the same as for img10.

## Family B: Venetian Gothic palazzi

**Scale:** I take img13 as 15 m wide (452 px). That gives a 5.6 m ground floor, piani nobili of 6.5 m and 5.9 m, and a 3.9 m attic, which is plausible. The lateral monofore are 47×126 px (a 2.7:1 ratio), against 2.5-3:1 in real ones. OK.

**Faults shared by all six:**

- **(e) The ogee is drawn upside down in curvature.** Each window head is a Gaussian "bell": it leaves the jamb with a *horizontal* tangent (flared, concave foot) and rises to a rounded convex top. A real Venetian ogee (Pisani Moretta, Ca' Foscari, Giustinian, Dolfin) is the opposite. It springs vertically or near-vertically from the capital, is convex in its lower half, reverses to concave and ends in a sharp point, often with a finial. The bell outline is the single most obvious "fake" signal; it reads as Russian onion or Mughal rather than Venetian.
- **(f) The polifora columns have no structural role.** The columns run from balcony to near the arch *apex*, and their capitals sit beside the bell tips. The arches spring from the window jambs about 90 px *below* the capitals (checked in a crop of img13). Real arches spring from the capitals, and the spandrel above them is solid stone, often pierced with quatrefoil tracery.
- **(g) The balconies are iron.** All balconies have iron bar railings on a thin slab. The 15th-c. default is a stone balustrade on corbels (R6, R7, R8). Iron railings appear only as later replacements (R5, Ca' da Mosto), so this is a flaw, not fatal. But every image has iron and none has stone, which shows a rule rather than variety.
- **(h) No Istrian-stone dressing.** There are no white corner quoins, no dentil frames, no tracery band, and no roof chimneys (*fumaioli*). All the references have at least the dentil frame or quoins.

### img13: palazzo with a 3-light polifora on both piani nobili (3/3), single monofore at the sides, central water portal
**Confidence:** high.

The lights are stacked correctly (3 over 3). The portal is a rectangular door under an arched lunette, which is plausible (Pisani Moretta, R6). The faults are only (e)-(h).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. It is the best of the family.

**Top fixes**
1. Use the true ogee profile, springing from the capitals.
2. Make the balconies stone balustrades.
3. Add white stone quoins, a dentil frame and chimneys.

### img02: polifora 3 lights on the second floor over 4 lights on the first
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- **The light counts do not match:** 3 over 4. The two main piani nobili should match and stack (6/6, 8/8, 6/6 in the references). With 3 over 4, the upper columns stand over voids below.
- The small attic windows over the polifora, at 245/300/354 px, align with the 3-light bay, which is good.
- Faults (e)-(h).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS.

**Top fixes**
1. Use the true ogee profile.
2. Use equal light counts on the piani nobili.
3. Use stone balconies.

### img03: wide palazzo, 2 monofore per side, polifora 4 over 5, mezzanine with 7 square windows
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- The light counts are 4 over 5 (a mismatch).
- The mezzanine windows at 239, 300 and 360 px do not line up with the 5 lights at 230, 262, 300, 336 and 370 px.
- Faults (e)-(h).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. The fixes are the same as for img02.

### img05: 3 monofore per side, a polifora on three floors (4/4/5)
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- An attic polifora is legitimate (Ca' Foscari), but this one has *more* lights below than above on the main floors (5 under 4).
- A polifora on three stacked floors plus 3+3 lateral windows makes a very grand front, about 15 bays.
- The ground floor has a single portal for a front this wide. The double palazzi (Giustinian, R7) have one portal per house.
- Faults (e)-(h).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS.

**Top fixes**
1. Use the true ogee profile.
2. Stack equal light counts.
3. Use stone balconies.

### img09: polifora 3/3/4, 1 monofore per side
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- The 3 over 4 at the lowest pair is a mismatch.
- The ground floor of a 490 px front carries only a portal and two small windows, leaving large blank wall. Real ground floors (Ca' Foscari, Pisani Moretta) carry a window in every upper-floor axis.
- Faults (e)-(h).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS.

**Top fixes**

2. Use matching light counts.
3. Put ground-floor openings on every bay axis.

### img15: 2 monofore per side, polifora 4/4/5, **two water portals in the outer bays**
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- **The portals are in the wrong place.** There are two portals, in the outer bays (x about 145 and 455), while the polifora, which marks the *portego* hall behind it, is central. The water gate opens into the *androne* on the main axis. Twin portals occur only when there are twin houses, each with its own central axis (Giustinian, R7), or as a central pair (Pisani Moretta, R6). Outer-bay portals under a single central polifora contradict the plan.
- Faults (e)-(h), and the 4-over-5 count mismatch.

**Verdict:** FAILS.

**Top fixes**
1. Put the portal (or a central pair of portals) on the polifora axis.
2. Use the true ogee profile.
3. Use matching light counts.

## Family C: Veneto-Byzantine fondaco palazzi

**Faults shared by all four:**

- **(i) The columns are fluted pilasters.** Every arcade "column" is drawn with vertical flutes. Veneto-Byzantine shafts are smooth marble, usually reused spolia (Fondaco, Loredan, Farsetti, da Mosto). Fluting is a classical and Renaissance motif, so this is an anachronism of about 250 years.
- **(j) The arches do not bear on the columns.** The capitals sit about 20 px *above* the arch springing (checked in a crop of img12). The semicircular arches rise from the wall beside the column, not from the capital. Real arches are stilted and spring from the capital and impost block.
- **(k) The portico is glazed.** The ground arcade is drawn as glazing with mullions, down to the ground. The real porticoes are open voids (they were landing halls for boats). Allowing for the flat drawing style, the infill still reads as a shopfront.
- **(l) Plain spandrels.** There are no paterae or formelle, which are the defining Veneto-Byzantine ornament (Fondaco, Loredan and da Mosto all have them). The arch extrados have no dentil-moulded archivolt.
- **(m) Iron railing on the loggia.** The originals have a stone balustrade or pluteus parapet (Fondaco, Loredan, Farsetti). Ca' da Mosto's iron rail is later.

### img12: Fondaco dei Turchi type (corner towers with merlons, 7-arch portico, 13-arch loggia)
**Confidence:** very high.

**Measured**
- The upper-to-lower arch ratio is 13/7 = 1.86. The Fondaco has 18/10 = 1.8. Good.
- The lower arches are about 42×95 px (1:2.2), which is good.

**Discrepancies**
- Faults (i)-(m).
- Each tower's ground floor has one large round-arched window. On the real Fondaco the arcade continues into the tower bases.
- The upper parts of the towers have 3-light and 2-light groups, which is reasonable.
- **Note:** the Fondaco's towers and merlons are an 1860s restoration (Berchet). The "type" is a single, much-rebuilt building, so variants of it are inherently speculative.

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. It is recognisable at a glance.

**Top fixes**
1. Use smooth round columns with the arches springing from the capitals.
2. Add paterae and roundels in the spandrels, and stone archivolts.
3. Make the portico open (dark void) and the loggia parapet stone.

### img01: the same tower type without merlons, 5-arch portico and 9-arch loggia
**Confidence:** high.

**Discrepancies**
- Without merlons the towers read as generic Italianate villa towers (Osborne House, 19th-c. Rundbogenstil). The skyline loses its Venetian identity.
- Faults (i)-(m).

**Verdict:** PASSES WITH FLAWS. It is borderline: an expert might read it as a 19th-c. Rundbogenstil building rather than a medieval one.

**Top fixes**
1. Add the merlon crest or a dentil cornice.
2. Smooth columns, with the arches springing from the capitals.
3. Add paterae.

### img04: two storeys, 6-arch portico across the full width, 11-arch loggia
**Confidence:** medium. It is meant as a Veneto-Byzantine palazzo, but it could equally be read as a market loggia or orangery.

**Discrepancies**
- **There are no solid side wings.** Every surviving example frames the portico and loggia with walled wings that have single or paired windows (Loredan, Farsetti, da Mosto, and the Fondaco's towers). An arcade running from end wall to end wall with 12 px end piers is not a palazzo composition.
- A blank band about 30 px deep lies between the loggia balcony and the portico crowns. On Loredan and da Mosto this zone carries paterae and reliefs.
- Faults (i)-(m).

**Verdict:** FAILS.

**Top fixes**
1. Add solid wings of 1-2 bays each side and reduce the arcade to the central 3-5 arches.
2. Smooth columns, with the arches springing from the capitals.
3. Add paterae in the band, stone balustrades and an open portico.

### img08: two storeys, 4-arch portico, 7-arch loggia across the full width
**Confidence:** medium.

This is the same composition as img04 at a smaller size, with the same faults. The 4-arch portico gives an even number of bays, so there is no central arch on the axis. The references all have an odd count, with a central arch (Loredan 5, da Mosto 3, Fondaco 10 is the exception).

**Verdict:** FAILS. The fixes are the same as for img04, plus an odd arch count.

## Patterns across the 16 images (likely generator rules)

1. **Ornament is applied as overlay with no tectonics.** Columns and arches are positioned independently. In Venetian Gothic the capitals sit near the arch apex; in Byzantine the capitals sit above the springing. Arches never spring from capitals. A rule such as `springing_y = capital_top_y` would fix both families.
2. **Wrong ogee curve primitive.** A Gaussian or bell function is used for the "ogee". It needs an S-curve that is convex low, then concave, and ends in a point, with vertical tangents at the springing.
3. **Polifora light counts are chosen per floor at random.** This produces 3/4, 4/5 and 3/3/4 stacks. The count should be fixed per facade, with the attic allowed n/2 lights at most and stacked over columns.
4. **Balcony material is hard-coded to iron.** It appears on all ten Venetian images. Stone balustrade should be the default for Gothic and Byzantine, with iron only as a rare "later alteration" option.
5. **The stoop is a straight diagonal flight anchored at the door's far jamb.** The stair geometry ignores the door position and the basement window. Model it as a platform plus a sideways flight plus a railing, with a basement door below.
6. **Identical window module everywhere.** The same 2×2 frame and beige surround appear in 17th-c. step gables and 19th-c. cornice houses. No period coupling between gable type and window type (relieving arches and shutters for pre-1670).
7. **Gable geometry is not tied to roof pitch.** The step gables reach 74-77°. The gable height should be derived from width × tan(about 55-60°).
8. **Hoisting features are not tied to the axis.** The hatch is placed in a window slot (img06). The hatch and beam should always sit on the gable centreline.
9. **Composition rules missing at the facade scale.** Portals are not tied to the polifora axis (img15). Ground-floor openings are not on the upper axes (img09). Veneto-Byzantine fronts have no wings (img04, img08). The arch count is even, leaving no central arch (img08).
10. **Signature ornament is missing.** There are no paterae (Byzantine), no tracery or quatrefoils (Gothic), and no Istrian-stone quoins or dentil frames (Venice). The canal houses lack carved claw pieces and sandstone dressings, and there are no chimneys anywhere. The silhouettes are right, but the identifying details that an expert looks at second are absent in every image.
11. **Near-duplicates.** img00 and img14 are almost the same house. img04 and img08 are the same composition. Variety in the generator's output is lower than the seed count suggests.
