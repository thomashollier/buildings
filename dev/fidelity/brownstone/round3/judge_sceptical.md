# Brownstone round 3: sceptical blind judgement

Judge: independent architectural historian, blind. I looked only at `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and at Wikimedia Commons photographs. Every drawing started as "fake until proven otherwise". Measurements are approximate and taken from the drawings scaled to 1300 px tall.

What these photographs show and the drawings don't:
- **A:** each stoop has a heavy cast-iron balustrade that rises continuously from a big newel at the sidewalk to the landing. Door and window hoods sit on scroll consoles directly on the architrave, and the doorway is a deep round-arched recess.
- **B:** cast-iron stoop railings run the full flight, and the window lintels are plain.
- **C:** stoops have solid brownstone wing walls the full height of the flight, and every bay carries its own cornice.

None of the 16 passes a specialist without flaws.

### 00
- **Stoop railing is missing.** There is a newel block at the foot and a stub of railing at the landing (about 3 ft), but nothing in between. In elevation a real stoop shows a continuous band of balusters from the newel up to the landing rail (Ref A).
- **Weak door surround.** The door frame is a pair of thin pilasters with a small segmental "bump" floating on top. It has no consoles, and the hood is barely wider than the frame. Ref A's hoods project on scroll consoles about 1/4 of the opening width beyond each jamb.
- **Every lintel is the same thin strip,** including the parlor. A house with a bracketed cornice gives its parlor windows at least a bracketed cornice hood.
- **Thin cornice.** There are only 4 brackets across about 25 ft, with a deep blank frieze. The brackets do line up with the piers, which is good. Real cornices also carry modillions or dentils between the brackets.
- Floor lines are drawn as continuous stringcourses on every storey, which a plain brownstone facade does not have.

### 01
- Same stoop fault as 00: newels and landing stubs only.
- **Lintels.** Plain slab lintels on brick are right for the 1850s (Ref B). Brownstone sill bands the same weight as the lintels are not.
- **Door hood floats.** It is a segmental cap sitting above a flat entablature, a double head that no builder used.
- The parlor windows are about 1.5× the upper windows. That is plausible.

### 02
- **The bay's narrow side windows half-vanish behind the corner piers.** It reads as a column standing in front of a window, not as a foreshortened canted side.
- **The bay runs into the left party line.** The outer pier sits on the facade edge, but a bay cannot project across the lot line. Real bays are set in about 1–2 ft (Ref C).
- **The bay has no top.** It stops at the second-floor head with no cornice, balcony or roof (compare Ref C, where each bowfront carries its own cornice).
- **Dwarf "tombstone" top windows.** The top-floor windows are a semicircle on a stub only about 0.3× the opening's width, set in a square frame whose corners stay empty. Romanesque top floors use full-height round-arched openings, often as an arcade.
- **The cornice is a flat slab with tiny blocks,** where a Romanesque front has corbelled brick or a parapet.

### 04
- **Fails the most basic test: top-floor bays don't stack.** The three top windows sit at roughly x = 252/452/655. Below them, the bay front is centred near 553 and its sides near 400 and 707, so the middle top window lands on a pier.
- Bay flush with the right party line, and the bay has no top.
- The bay's piers run through the stoop zone and the areaway.
- The stoop has solid cheek walls, which is good, but they stop about 1/3 of the way below the landing.

### 03, 06, 11, 13 (the Italianate brownstone family, the strongest group)
- **Floating hoods.** The parlor and upper hoods hover over the arched or segmental heads with a gap of roughly 0.2–0.3× the window width. In Ref A the hood sits directly on the architrave.
- **No stoop balustrade,** again (newels and landing stubs only).
- **06:** the left-bay windows are about 25% wider than the other two on every floor. That is an immediate tell, because windows on one floor of a row house are identical.
- **11:** the right-bay windows on the upper floors are about 12% wider. The basement has no areaway railing even though the stoop has newels.
- **03:** the door hood is a segmental arch perched on a flat entablature (the same double head as in 01).
- **13:** the most convincing drawing. All bays have equal widths, the hierarchy is right (round parlor, then segmental, then a short segmental attic) and the pedimented doorway is right. It still lacks a stoop rail, console brackets and cornice modillions.

### 05
- **Uneven bays.** The right-bay windows are about 25% wider than the left two.
- **Cornice.** The flat cornice with tiny blocks is acceptable for Greek Revival, but the height of 4 storeys over a basement is late for that style.
- **The door surround,** pilasters with an entablature, is correct for c.1845 (Ref B).
- No stoop railing.

### 07
- **Jogged stoop.** The upper half of the stoop is wider than the lower half. A stoop has one width.
- **No railing or newel at all,** only a bare flight of steps. Even a lay viewer notices that.
- Otherwise the same as 00.

### 08
- **Uneven bays.** The right-bay windows are about 25% wider than the other two on all upper floors.
- **Dwarf arched top windows,** the same fault as in 02.
- The upper stoop cheek walls are taller than the lower ones, which produces the same jog as 07 in masonry.

### 09
- **Jogged stoop with no rails and no newels,** the same as 07.
- The hoods float as in 03.
- The pedimented doorway is otherwise good.

### 10, 12, 15 (bay-fronted houses)
- **The bay sits on the party line (10 right, 12 left, 15 right).** There is no return of wall.
- **No bay top.** The bay stops dead under a plain upper floor.
- **The top floor ignores the bay.** In 12 and 15 the attic windows are spaced evenly across the whole width, so the middle one sits over a pier of the bay.
- **Clustered brackets.** The cornice brackets come in arbitrary pairs (12: about x = 130/177, 383/441, 610), where real brackets sit one per pier.
- **10:** the bay rises from the basement to the 4th floor, but the top storey is flat with no transition.

### 14
- **Jogged stoop.** The lower flight is narrower than the upper flight.
- The cheek walls stop about 4 ft short of the landing.
- **Dwarf arched attic windows,** the same fault as in 02.
- **The base reads as one band.** The parlor and basement share one rusticated band with no water table or sill course separating them.

## Top giveaways across all 16, ranked, with fixes

1. **The stoop has no railing (14 of 16; 07 and 09 have nothing at all).**
   - *Fix:* always draw the stoop rail as a continuous baluster band on each side of the flight, from the newel to the landing.
   - Make it about 0.35–0.4 of the parlor-floor height and 3–4 in wide in elevation.
   - Draw the newel 1.2× the rail height, with a cap.
   - Brownstone wing walls instead of iron: about 40% for Italianate, about 90% for Romanesque/Renaissance (Ref C).
   - The wall must run the full height of the flight.
2. **Bay windows that break the grid (02, 04, 10, 12, 15).**
   - *Set the bay in* from the party line by at least 1 pier width (about 6% of the facade width).
   - *Cap the bay* with its own cornice, or carry it to the main cornice.
   - *Align the upper floors with the bay.* Any floor above the bay must put its windows on the bay's front and side axes: 2 windows over the front plus 1 per side, or 1 wide window per side.
   - *Draw the side windows as visible openings* between the two corner piers, not hidden behind them.
3. **Uneven window widths within a floor (05, 06, 08, 11).**
   - Use 1 window width per floor across all bays.
   - Allow ≤3% variation, or none.
4. **Floating hoods and double door heads (03, 06, 09, 11, 13; 00, 01, 03).**
   - Set the hood bottom equal to the architrave top, with a gap of 0.
   - Add scroll consoles 0.15× the opening width each side, running down to about 1/4 of the window height.
   - Use one head per door: a segmental *or* a triangular pediment on consoles, never a segment perched on a flat entablature.
5. **Jogged stoops (07, 08, 09, 14).** Give the stoop one constant width from the sidewalk to the landing. The landing is the same width as the flight.
6. **Dwarf arched top windows in the Romanesque set (02, 04, 08, 14).**
   - The arched opening needs a total height of at least 1.6× its width; the stub is currently about 0.3×.
   - The arch should spring from imposts, and the square frame should be dropped.
   - Alternatively, group the top floor into a 4–5 arch arcade (probability about 50%).
7. **Thin cornices.**
   - Use 5–8 brackets on a 20–25 ft front, one over each pier plus one at each end.
   - Add modillions or dentils between them.
   - Make the frieze about 0.6× the bracket height, with panels.
   - The Romanesque set needs corbelled brick or a parapet, not a flat slab.
8. **Parlor hierarchy and identical lintels (00, 01, 05, 07).** Give the parlor windows a hood or cornice one grade heavier than the floors above. At present every floor has the same strip.
9. **Floor lines drawn as stringcourses on every storey.** Draw only the water table and the sill courses that exist. Plain brownstone fronts have no line at each floor.

## Bottom line

The Italianate brownstones (03, 06, 11 and especially 13) are recognisable at once and would fool a lay viewer. A specialist rejects every drawing in the set within seconds, because of the missing stoop railings. The bay-fronted and Romanesque variants (02, 04, 10, 12, 14, 15) fail on grid alignment and the bays' party-line and missing-top problems before style even comes into it.
