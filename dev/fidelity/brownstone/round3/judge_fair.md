# Brownstone round 3: blind judgement (fair judge)

I judged 16 flat elevations (img00 to img15) without opening any other file in the repository. I compared them against photographs on Wikimedia Commons. My scores look at massing, rhythm, proportion, hierarchy and the characteristic elements. They ignore the flat colour rendering.

R means Recognisability (1 to 5): how quickly the city and type read. P means Plausibility (1 to 5): could this exact front stand on a real block.

## The four families

The 16 drawings fall into four families.

- **Italianate brownstone, flat front** (00, 01, 03, 05, 06, 07, 09, 11, 13). All read immediately as New York or Brooklyn, 1855 to 1875.
- **Italianate with a bay** (10, 12, 15). The type is right, but the bay is drawn wrongly.
- **Romanesque or Queen Anne, red brick over a rusticated brownstone base** (02, 04, 08, 14). These read as Park Slope or Bed-Stuy, 1885 to 1895, but the roofline and the bays give them away.
- Every family uses the same black spear-point areaway fence, the same pair of dark onion-capped stoop newels, and the same 4-bracket cornice. Seen side by side, the set reads as one generator.

## Top giveaways, ranked, with fixes

### 1. Bays drawn as free-standing posts that cut through windows
This affects 02, 04, 10, 12 and 15. It is the single most damning error.

- **Draw one volume.** Give the bay one continuous outline, set out with its own vertical edges.
- **Proportions.** The front face should take about 50 to 60% of the bay width. Each canted flank, foreshortened at 45 degrees, should take about 20 to 25%.
- **Posts.** Mullion posts go only at the two angles and the two outer edges, each 0.25 to 0.35 m wide.
- **Flank windows.** A flank window equals the flank face minus its posts. A post must never overlap glazing.
- **Distance from the lot line.** Keep at least 0.45 m (1.5 ft) of plain wall between the bay and each lot line. The bay should be 45 to 55% of a 20 to 25 ft front.
- **Base and top.** Start the bay on its own base at grade, or on the areaway wall. End it with either:
  - its own cornice plus a cap or iron balcony railing at the top of the parlor or second floor (about 60% of cases), or
  - a run to the roof, with the main cornice breaking forward over it (about 40%, ref F).

### 2. The stoop has no continuous railing or balustrade
This affects all 16. It is worst in 07 and 09, which have none at all. In 04, 08, 10 and 14 the cheeks are oversized.

- **How it looks in front elevation.** The stoop runs perpendicular to the façade, so its railing is seen end-on. It shows as a continuous vertical silhouette on each side of the treads, from grade up to the landing plus 0.9 m.
- **Iron railing (about 65%):** a cast-iron newel at the sidewalk about 1.0 to 1.2 m tall. Behind it, a solid band of balusters rising to the landing plus 0.9 m.
- **Stone balustrade (about 35%), ref B:** a plinth or pier at the bottom about 1.0 m tall. Behind it, a cheek wall whose top is at the landing plus 0.8 to 0.9 m, with a coping 0.1 to 0.15 m deep.
- **Never** draw slabs that rise 1.5 m or more above the landing.
- **Stoop width.** Make it 1.6 to 2.0 m, at least as wide as the door enframement, never narrower.

### 3. Window hoods float clear of the openings, and every floor gets the same lintel
This affects all the Italianates, and is worst in 03, 06, 09, 11 and 13.

- **Placement.** The hood sits directly on the window head. The gap must be 0.
- **Shape.** On segmental or round-arched windows, the hood follows the arch (ref A).
- **Hierarchy, parlor floor:** the hood depth is about 0.2 times the window width. Use a pediment or a segmental hood, carried on scrolled consoles. Each console is about 0.1 to 0.12 times the window width and drops 0.15 to 0.25 times the window height.
- **Hierarchy, upper floors:** a lighter hood, about 0.12 to 0.15 times the window width, with small consoles or ears.
- **Hierarchy, top floor:** a simple lintel or none.
- **Sills** project on small corbels.

### 4. The parlor floor is not dominant
This affects 00, 05, 07, 12 and 13.

- **Storey heights.** Make the parlor storey about 1.25 times the upper storeys (about 4.0 m against 3.2 m). Make the top storey about 0.85 times.
- **Parlor windows.** Make them 1.35 to 1.5 times the second-floor window height, with sills 0.3 to 0.5 m above the floor. In about 30% of cases they should run to the floor and open onto an iron balconette (refs A and B).

### 5. The cornice
This affects all 16.

- **Bracket count.** Four brackets on a 20 to 25 ft front reads as a toy. Real fronts have 6 to 10 brackets, evenly spaced, landing on the piers between windows. The end brackets are often paired, and the frieze is panelled between them (refs A and C).
- **Even spacing.** Never cluster brackets over bay posts (10, 12 and 15).
- **Lot lines.** The cornice must stop flush at the lot lines because it continues on the neighbour. Drop the side overhang.
- **Depth.** Make the cornice plus frieze 0.35 to 0.45 of a storey.

### 6. The Romanesque and Queen Anne roofline is wrong
This affects 02, 04, 08 and 14.

- **Replace the thin flat coping.** In about 60% of cases use a gable, a dormer or a pyramidal or mansard roof (refs D and E). In the rest, use a heavy corbelled-brick or brownstone cornice with a frieze at least 0.3 storey deep.
- **Drop the semicircular lunettes on a full storey.** Use full-height round-arched windows, or a paired or triple arcade. Keep lunettes for inside gables.
- **Add belt courses.** Put brownstone belt courses at the sill lines of every brick storey.
- **Add an oriel.** In about 40% of cases, put an oriel at the second floor (ref E).

### 7. The houses stand alone
This affects all 16.

- **Draw the neighbours.** Rowhouses are always attached. Draw neighbour slivers 0.5 to 1.5 m wide at each side, with cornice lines that differ by 0 to 1.5 m, or at least run the party-wall line to grade.
- **Why it matters.** Isolated, the tall narrow 1:2.8 fronts (05, 08 and 13) read as towers.

### 8. The areaway
- **Add the wall.** Real areaways have a low brownstone wall 0.6 to 0.9 m high, with a coping, and the iron fence stands on it (ref C). Here the fence plants straight in the ground.
- **img11** has no areaway at all.

### 9. Repetition across the set
- **Problem.** Every drawing uses the same newel shape, the same fence pattern and the same 4-bracket cornice.
- **Fix: newels.** Rotate among 3 or more types: a cast-iron urn-top, a turned post, and a stone block.
- **Fix: fence.** Rotate among 3 or more types: spear-point, hairpin, and a plain bar with a top rail.
- **Fix: door.** Vary the door enframement: a segmental hood on consoles, a triangular pediment, or a round arch with a keystone.

### 10. There is no band course at the parlor floor line
This affects the flat-front Italianates.

- **Fix.** Add a projecting water table or band course 0.15 to 0.25 m deep at the parlor floor line.
- **Basement.** Rusticate the basement in about 50% of cases (refs C and F).
