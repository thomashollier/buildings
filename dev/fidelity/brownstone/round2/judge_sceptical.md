# Brownstone round 2: sceptical blind judgement

Judge: independent architectural historian (blind; only `images/img00.png` to `img15.png` and Wikimedia Commons consulted).
Scale: R = recognisability 1-5, P = plausibility 1-5. "Expert at a glance" = would a New York rowhouse specialist accept it as a real building in a two-second look.

What every real example above shares and the drawings mostly lack: (1) a stoop you can read as steps, with 8-14 risers and a solid stone cheek wall or a cast-iron railing that rises with the steps to a big newel; (2) a clear decorative hierarchy, heaviest at the parlor-floor entrance and windows, lighter going up; (3) cornice brackets that sit on the piers between windows, never over an opening; (4) the house reads as one slice of a continuous row, never a free-standing tower.

## Per-drawing verdicts

### img00: R 4, P 3, WITH FLAWS
Brooklyn (Park Slope / Carroll Gardens), Italianate brownstone, c. 1855-70; basement + parlor + 2, 3 bays, door right.
- Stoop is a blank pink slab: no treads, no risers, no cheek wall. Every real stoop (HAR, 3RD, PSL) reads as a stack of horizontal steps, and the stone cheek walls are as tall as the railing. Two thin poles on lollipop newels are not a railing.
- Cornice brackets land over the windows: of 8 evenly spaced brackets, 3 sit directly above window openings. Real cornices put a bracket at each end and on the piers (often paired), see HAR top floor.
- Window enframements are three hairlines (a thin sill and a thin strip head). Italianate brownstones have full molded architraves plus projecting hoods, at least on the parlor floor (HAR). Upper floors get the same weightless strip as the parlor floor, so no hierarchy.
- The door surround (segmental pediment over a rectangular transom) is the right idea but too shallow and floats with no consoles.
- Thin incised line at every floor level reads as panel joints; brownstone facades are smooth ashlar between basement and cornice.

### img01: R 3, P 2, NO
Manhattan (Greenwich Village / Chelsea), meant as Greek Revival brick rowhouse, c. 1835-45; door left.
- Style is mixed: the flat dentilled cornice and plain lintels say Greek Revival (WAV), but the windows are 2-over-2 with tall parlor windows and the door is an Italianate double door with no sidelights or transom bar. Greek Revival houses have 6/6 sash and a door framed by pilasters and a plain entablature with sidelights/transom (WAV).
- No stoop railing at all and the stoop is a blank box flanked by two odd stub walls. WAV shows a 5-6 riser stoop with wrought-iron rails; this one is far too high (a full basement storey) for that period and has no rails.
- Cornice is a thin plank with tiny blocks hanging below, with no frieze. Greek Revival cornices have a fascia and frieze, often with attic frieze windows.
- Brownstone basement band is right (WAV).

### img02: R 4, P 3, WITH FLAWS
Brooklyn, Italianate brownstone, c. 1860-70; door right with triangular pediment over round-arched transom.
- Top-floor window heads collide with the cornice: the hoods run into the bracket feet, with no frieze strip between. Real top-floor openings sit below a clear wall band (HAR).
- Brackets again fall over window openings.
- Triangular pediment directly over a round arch transom with no consoles; real entrances carry the hood on tall scrolled consoles (HAR) and the door sits in a deep reveal.
- Stoop slab and pole railings as in img00.
- Green-black cornice is acceptable.

### img03: R 4, P 3, WITH FLAWS
Brooklyn or Manhattan, brick Italianate with brownstone trim, c. 1855-65; door left.
- Same stoop slab and pole railings.
- Window hoods on the 2nd floor are heavier than on the parlor floor, which inverts the hierarchy.
- Top-floor hoods jammed under the cornice; brackets over openings.
- Brick rows of this type normally have a brownstone basement and stoop; that is present and correct.

### img04: R 3, P 2, NO
Brooklyn, low Italianate brownstone, basement + parlor + 1, c. 1860s.
- Big blank wall band between the upper windows and the parlor-floor door hood with no floor line; the parlor storey reads about 1.5x too tall compared with the upper storey.
- Stoop has neither railing nor newels; it is a slab between two tall rectangular cheek blocks whose tops stop halfway up the door, which no real stoop does (cheek walls follow the steps' slope; PSL).
- Areaway fence covers only the left bay and stops at the stoop. That is fine, but with no stoop rail the composition reads unfinished.
- Brackets over windows; window trim is hairlines.

### img05: R 3, P 2, NO
Brooklyn Heights / Cobble Hill, Anglo-Italianate or Romanesque-tinged brownstone, c. 1850s; basement + parlor + 1.
- The rustication lines on the parlor storey are not horizontal: they visibly tilt and converge. Channelled rustication is laid level (KAN), so this is a geometry bug an expert spots instantly.
- Massing is too squat for its width: the facade is about 0.78 of the height wide, which reads as a 30+ ft wide two-storey house. Real Anglo-Italianates (KAN) are 3-4 storeys on 18-22 ft.
- Round-arched windows on every storey; in the Anglo-Italianate type the arches are kept to the parlor floor and the upper windows are square-headed (KAN).
- Keystones are little rectangles perched above the arch, not set into it.
- Pole railings with the slab stoop; the stoop is also too high for the type (KAN has a low 4-6 riser stoop).

### img06: R 3, P 2, NO
Brooklyn, Italianate brownstone, c. 1860s; door right.
- Top floor mixes window heads: two segmental heads and one square head in the same storey. Real rows keep one head shape per storey (HAR, PSL).
- The top-right window butts directly into the cornice.
- No areaway fence at all in front of a sunken basement; this is unsafe and unseen in historic rows (3RD).
- Stoop slab and pole railings.

### img07: R 4, P 3, WITH FLAWS

### img08: R 3, P 2, NO
Manhattan (Upper West Side / Harlem), brownstone, c. 1870-85; basement + parlor + 3, very narrow (about 2.6 times as tall as wide).
- The parlor floor is no taller than the floors above it; its round-arched windows are about the same height as the 2nd-floor windows. The parlor storey should be the tallest in the house, with windows about 15-25% taller than the 2nd floor's.
- Round-arched windows under flat straight hoods with a gap between: the hood should follow the arch or sit on a square-headed frame.
- Top floor mixes two segmental heads with one square head again.
- 5 storeys on 3 narrow bays with no stringcourse or hierarchy is a tenement silhouette rather than a single-family brownstone.

### img11: R 2, P 2, NO

- The parlor and basement show a tripartite window (a wide light flanked by two narrow ones) between pilasters, but its centre is about half a bay off the window axes of the floors above. Real alterations re-use the bay axes, and an expert reads this misalignment as an impossibility.

## Top giveaways, ranked, with fixes

1. **The stoop is a blank slab with pole "railings" (16 of 16).** This is the single strongest tell (HAR, 3RD, PSL).
   *Fix:* draw risers as horizontal lines: risers = round(parlor floor height above grade / 7 in), giving 10-14 for a full-basement brownstone and 5-7 for Greek Revival or Anglo-Italianate (WAV, KAN). Give the stoop 6-8 ft overall width with the outer 10-14 in on each side as a solid stone cheek wall (brownstone colour), its top sloping in section; in elevation this shows as a wall whose top is about 30-36 in above each tread, so render it as a stepped or sloped band rising from the newel to the door. Either a cast-iron railing (thin balusters every 4-5 in plus a rail) or a solid stone balustrade, chosen with a probability of about 0.6 iron to 0.4 stone for Brooklyn Italianate. Newels should be 14-18 in square and 42-54 in tall, and should never stand alone on poles.
2. **Cornice brackets over window openings (about 14 of 16).**
   *Fix:* place brackets on the piers: one at each end, then one or a pair centred on each pier between windows (for 3 bays, 4 positions; paired gives 8 brackets). Put modillions or dentils at 6-10 in centres between the brackets, and a panelled frieze. Never put a bracket on a window's axis.
3. **Flat hierarchy and hairline trim (16 of 16).**
   *Fix:* give the parlor floor the heaviest treatment: a full architrave 5-7 in wide around each window plus a projecting hood (cornice-type or segmental) on 2 consoles, with the hood projecting 6-9 in. On the 2nd floor use an architrave plus a lighter hood, and on the top floor an architrave only, or a simple segmental head. Sills 4-6 in tall on small blocks. The entrance hood should be about 1.3 times the width of a window hood and carried on scrolled consoles 2-3 ft long (HAR).
4. **Mixed window heads within one storey (img06, img08, img13).**
   *Fix:* choose the head shape per storey, not per window. Segmental top-floor heads are fine, but all three must match.
5. **Misaligned bay axes and hybrid grammars (img11, img12).**
   *Fix:* any parlor-floor alteration (tripartite or bay window) must keep the upper-floor axes: centre the wide light on a bay axis and carry the pilasters to a cornice or entablature. Probability of a remodelled parlor in a row: at most 0.1, and never on the upper floors.
6. **Stray stepped stoop profile outside the facade (img07, img09, img10, img13, img14).**
   *Fix:* clip the stoop to the front elevation. The side profile must not render, and nothing may cross the party-wall line.
7. **Top-floor windows jammed into the cornice (img02, img03, img06, img15).**
   *Fix:* keep a plain wall band of 18-30 in (about 0.15-0.25 of a window's height) between the top-floor window heads and the bracket feet.
8. **Wrong storey proportions (img04, img08) and massing (img05, img15).**
   *Fix:* storey heights for basement : parlor : 2nd : 3rd : top should run about 0.75 : 1.2 : 1.0 : 0.95 : 0.85, with parlor windows 15-25% taller than the 2nd floor's. Facade width should be 0.33-0.5 of the height to the cornice top for 4-storey houses and up to 0.65 for 3-storey ones, never 0.75 or more.
9. **Rustication not level (img05).** *Fix:* draw rustication joints exactly horizontal, at 12-16 in centres, and restrict them to the basement and parlor storeys (KAN).
10. **Style mixing in Greek Revival (img01).** *Fix:* if brick plus dentil cornice is chosen, use 6/6 sash, a low stoop of 5-7 risers with iron rails, an entrance with pilasters, entablature, sidelights and transom, and plain flat lintels (WAV).
11. **Missing areaway fence in front of a sunken basement (img06, img10).** *Fix:* always draw the fence or a low stone areaway wall when the basement sill is below grade.
12. **Free-standing silhouette.** Every drawing shows sky on both sides, but a rowhouse is a slice of a row. *Fix:* show neighbouring party walls, or at least stub walls of adjacent facades of slightly different height or cornice, so the cornice line continues.
