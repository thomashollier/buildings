# Brownstone: three review rounds

R is recognisability and P is plausibility, each out of 5. Each round's key sits beside its images.

| Round | Method | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Round 1 | A photo-only observer, an adversary testing the rules against photographs, and a grader | Colour D; openings, entrance, cornice, ornament and variety C; proportions and ironwork B (`grading.md`) |
| Round 2 | 16 blind drawings, two judges | Fair judge: 4 of 16 fail, all read as New York 1850–75. Sceptical judge: R 3.3, P 2.4, 9 fail |
| Round 3 | 16 fresh blind drawings, two new judges | Fair judge: R 3.5, P 2.6, none clean; the flat-front Italianates best, every bay front and Romanesque front fails. Sceptical judge: R 3.3, P 2.3, 9 fail |

Plausibility did not rise between rounds 2 and 3. Round 2's complaints were fixed: the blank stoop slab, the brackets over windows, mixed heads on one floor, and misaligned bays. Round 3 then found new ones in the bay fronts and the Romanesque tops.

## After round 3 (applied, not judged again)

- **Bay fronts:** a bay is set 0.3 m in from each party wall, banded at each floor's head, with its arrises clear of the lights.
- **Stoop railings:** iron balusters run from the newel to the top rail over a low stone cheek.
- **Parlour floor:** it is taller (4.0–4.4 m). Its round-headed windows carry a moulded archivolt instead of a flat hood.
- **Cornice brackets:** they are paired on the piers.
- **Romanesque tops:** half of these fronts end in a coped gable with a small round-arched light, the rest in a full-depth cornice.

## Still open
