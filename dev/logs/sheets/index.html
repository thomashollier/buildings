Contact sheets

Twenty seeds in one grid is the cheapest way to catch the single broken outlier among many (BRIEF.md). Every building on every sheet below is generated by the real generate() pipeline and drawn by the real renderer's own draw_building -- nothing here is a mock. A cell shown in red is a situation whose generation raised an exception; the exception text is printed in the tile itself rather than the cell silently vanishing.

Caught while building this harness -- the porte piétonne renders as a plain window. elements.draw_opening special-cases opening_type == "carriage_entrance" (a solid brown double door) but has no branch for "pedestrian_entrance", so it falls through to the generic window path -- same blue glass fill, same cross-mullion, same frame. The IR is not at fault: catalogue.entrance_for correctly places exactly one porte_piétonne slot on every building that does not earn a carriage arch (any class on a street under 12 m, and modest/ordinary on any street), and the node carries opening_type='pedestrian_entrance' with its own ABSOLUTE 1.40×2.90 m rectangle -- the renderer simply discards that fact, the same offence A4.1/A6.8/A7.3 already named twice. Since the porte piétonne is, per the catalogue's own docstring, “the commoner Paris entrance” (most of the city's immeubles have one, not a carriage arch), this silently reproduces Amendment 6.1 (“There is no door. Anywhere.”) for every modest and ordinary building, and for any class on a narrow street. See the street-width sweep (w=8–10 m: three shop-like glass openings, no door), the class sweep (modest/ordinary columns: the labelled 1.40×2.90 m door is indistinguishable from the window beside it) and the block below (9 ordinary parcels, one continuous run of glass at ground level -- not one visible door along the whole wall). Fix belongs in render/svg/elements.py::draw_opening, not in this harness or in qualification.

Look for the ONE building that stands out -- a facade with no bays, a storey stack a module too tall, a roofline that broke its envelope, ironwork or a socle wildly out of family with its neighbours. That single outlier is exactly what this sheet exists to catch; nothing here should surprise you twice.

2. Street-width sweep

seed 42, 1890, comfortable, shop -- street width 8→38 m
What to look for: The height must move in STEPS, not a ramp: several widths in a row give the SAME height, then it jumps. The règlement's own breakpoints (7.80 m, 9.75 m, 20.00 m) are straddled closely on this sheet -- 9.75 vs 9.80, 20.00 vs 20.25 -- so the jump should land exactly between one pair of adjacent cells and nowhere else. A height that creeps up gradually instead of jumping is the regulatory table being fitted rather than looked up.

3. Date sweep

seed 42, street 18 m, parcel 13x18 m, comfortable, shop -- 1830→1905 at 5-year steps
What to look for: This is the visual proof of the two-chronologies design. The ROOFLINE must visibly change SHAPE right at 1884/85 -- a 45° diagonal comble before, a circular-arc comble after -- because that is a decree, a step function. Everything else (the socle's rustication, the ironwork pattern, the keystone) should turn over GRADUALLY across the sheet, often at a different pace and on different years than the roof and than each other, because those are fashion, not law. If the roof drifts instead of jumping, or the ironwork jumps in lockstep with the roof, the two chronologies have collapsed into one.

4. Class sweep

street 18 m, seed 42, shop -- modest→grand at 1870 and again at 1895
What to look for: This is the sheet most worth studying closely: the absolute/proportional split made visible. Read the printed door dimensions left to right along each row -- they should barely move (identical within an entrance kind: modest/ordinary share one pedestrian-door size, comfortable/grand share one carriage-arch size), because a door is sized to a body and a carriage, not to ambition. Meanwhile the bays, window sizes and cornice should grow visibly richer toward 'grand'. A door that grows with the building has silently become PROPORTIONAL, which is the exact failure Amendment 1 named.

5. A block

110 m frontage, 18 m street, 1870, ordinary, seed 5, one chamfered corner -- 9 parcels
What to look for: This must read as ONE WALL -- a continuous cornice line, continuous string courses and balcony lines running across every party wall without a seam, and the chamfered corner at the left end sitting in as a third short facade with its own roof and rhythm, not a gap or a blank stub. If you can pick out where one building ends and the next begins by anything other than the party-wall hairline, the block has failed at the one thing it exists for.
